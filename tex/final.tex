\title{Maxwell--Jones Structure for Vector-Sensor-Aided Distributed
Near-Field RIS-OFDM Localization and Synchronization: Information
Splitting, Sufficient Compression, and Certified Clock Profiling}

\author{Qingshan Gong,~\IEEEmembership{Member,~IEEE}
\thanks{Manuscript received XX XX, 2026; revised XX XX, 2026.}}

\markboth{IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications,~Vol.~XX, No.~XX, 2026}%
{Gong: MKSC-GI Initialized CCOP-JVP for Distributed Near-Field RIS-EVS-OFDM Systems}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We consider joint 3-D localization and synchronization for a single-antenna
user in a wideband OFDM uplink observed by an electromagnetic vector-sensor
(EVS) array only through multiple distributed near-field reconfigurable
intelligent surfaces (RISs). The direct path is blocked, all cascaded paths
share an unknown clock offset, with complex gains and Jones polarization
coefficients as nuisance parameters. Exploiting the factorization of each
panel response into a space--frequency term and a calibrated Maxwell--Jones
term, we develop a two-stage MKSC-GI+CCOP-JVP estimator. MKSC-GI applies
Maxwell--Kronecker compression before shared-delay recovery and combines
Jones-aware panel association, Rayleigh-spaced beam-space acquisition, and
common-geometry fusion to obtain a reliable position basin. CCOP-JVP
eliminates the Jones coefficients by variable projection and profiles the
common clock along its unitary phase-ramp orbit, enabling FFT-assisted
branch-and-bound with a deterministic optimality-gap certificate. We further
establish information preservation under the matched white-Gaussian model
and how EVS component selection shapes geometric information and panel
separability. At $-10$~dB, the proposed method achieves a $0.316$-mm median
position error with $0.31\%$ position and clock catastrophic-event rates,
versus $1.77\%$--$2.40\%$ position failures for the closest competing
routes. No proposed-route catastrophe occurs at or above $0$~dB, and the
residual accuracy boundary is set by RIS-to-BS angular calibration rather
than noise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{E}{lectrically} large reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)
panels extend the radiative near-field region, where spherical-wave curvature
conveys both range and angular
information~\cite{wymeersch2020risloc,elzanaty2021bounds,cui2023nearfield,%
yang2024nfris}, so several distributed panels can act as passive anchors for
joint 3-D localization and synchronization. An electromagnetic vector sensor
(EVS) adds a complementary observable: its co-located electric and magnetic
components map each two-dimensional Jones state to a calibrated six-component
Maxwell response~\cite{nehorai1994vector,khan2020sixcomponent}. We study this
architecture when the direct link is blocked, several distributed RIS
panels are observed by a single shared EVS array, and all cascaded delays
share an unknown common clock offset -- a setting in which, we argue next,
three difficulties interact.

Near-field RIS localization already rests on a solid base: distributed and
multi-RIS designs turn several panels into geometric diversity through
panel-wise measurements, learned angle extraction, or blockage-aware panel
selection~\cite{ma2023distributed,fadakar2024multiris,liu2025multiris}, and
asynchronous analyses carry position bounds and estimators to links whose
delays absorb an unknown
clock~\cite{fascista2022rissync,gong2024async,yan2025ris}. These works share
a premise rather than a receiver: the evidence reaching the estimator is
already panel-resolved, through panel-dependent spatial evidence, angle
extraction preceding asynchronous refinement, or group training with
canonical polyadic decomposition (CPD) and panel selection. A single shared
aperture removes that premise: every passive path here arrives at the same
array within one observation, so a multilinear decomposition returns
algebraic labels rather than physical ones, and one swapped label misassigns
a range, a near-field response and a clock replica together. The first
difficulty is therefore reliable panel association and basin acquisition
rather than asymptotic accuracy, requiring an observable beyond the
space--frequency signature. Polarization is such an observable, entering RIS
work along two lines that have not met: as a controllable resource exposing
co- and cross-polar beamforming gains~\cite{han2022dual,munawar2024dual}, and
as an unknown recovered jointly with angle and
delay~\cite{nehorai1994vector,khan2020sixcomponent,gong2026aimdf}. Neither
line reaches distributed near-field RIS geometry, and the closest model
makes the gap concrete: the extremely large-scale RIS channel-estimation
model of~\cite{yang2024nfris} shares our cascaded near-field structure but
ties no common clock across panels and carries no polarization dimension, so
a path there is described entirely by the space--frequency signature just
argued to be insufficient. An EVS supplies the missing dimension without
enlarging the aperture, since the retained Maxwell response of a path is
fixed by the calibrated RIS-to-BS direction of its own panel;
Section~\ref{sec:analysis} shows this response separates panels left
degenerate by space and frequency, while at most two retained components
destroy that separation.

The second difficulty is dimensional. Full-EVS reception yields
$6M_A\,N\,T$ complex samples in the raw observation tensor before Hankel
smoothing, and a generic reduction can destroy exactly the delay subspace,
the profiled
likelihood, or the localization information the later stages need, so any
compression must be information preserving rather than merely size
reducing -- a requirement existing acquisition machinery does not carry.
Tensor estimators exploit RIS training multilinearity, typically through
CPD with alternating least squares
(ALS)~\cite{lin2021tensor,liu2025multiris,kolda2009tensor,%
sidiropoulos2017tensor}; sparse and Bayesian methods search a discretized
spherical manifold using polar dictionaries, orthogonal matching pursuit
(OMP), sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) and variational Bayesian inference
(VBI), or off-grid refinement by space-alternating generalized
expectation--maximization
(SAGE)~\cite{cui2022channel,bayraktar2022momp,tipping2001sbl,li2024vbi,%
pati1993omp,fleury1999sage}; and near-field beam training uses codebooks
designed against wideband beam
split~\cite{yang2024nfris,zheng2025wideband}. We retain their multilinear
front end and coarse spherical search but not their discretization: a
solution accurate on average yet mislabeling a panel is not merely imprecise
here, and a fixed-count dictionary undersamples precisely the large panels
that motivate near-field operation, since angular resolution sharpens with
aperture. Two exact constructions replace that discretization, an adjoint
rewriting into the element domain and a Fresnel dechirp at a trial range,
whose candidate spacing tracks the Rayleigh limit at a cost decoupled from
dictionary size (Section~\ref{subsec:beamspace}); being proposals re-scored
by the exact spherical criterion, enlarging the candidate set can only lower
the achieved objective, so the fixed-grid resolution floor of dictionary-only
recovery is not inherited.

The third difficulty is the clock itself. It enters every cascaded delay
inside the same OFDM phase, and the blocked direct path removes the delay
difference that would otherwise cancel it, so a joint search over position,
clock and Jones nuisances is nonconvex and its numerical behavior depends on
the coordinate scaling between position and clock. Variable projection (VP)
eliminates linear nuisance parameters from nonlinear least
squares~\cite{golub1973varpro,liu2026extended}, and recent joint
localization--synchronization work seeks global solutions by semidefinite
reformulation in other timing models~\cite{peng2025sdp}; that relaxation
suits a timing model that lifts to a linear one, which the complex OFDM
likelihood here does not. It carries something stronger instead: the common
clock acts on it as an exact phase-ramp orbit along which the Jones Gram
matrix is invariant, so the conditional clock can be profiled directly in
the exact likelihood coordinates and solved with a deterministic
optimality-gap certificate, while the outer 3-D position search remains
local. Our own EVS-OFDM
estimator~\cite{gong2026aimdf} supplies the multidimensional front end but
lacks distributed RIS geometry, physical panel association, sufficient
aggregate-subspace compression, common-geometry initialization and clock
profiling; to our knowledge no existing method combines a distributed
free-Jones EVS likelihood, Jones-aware physical panel association, and a
certified conditional clock solve in one estimator. At low signal-to-noise
ratio (SNR) the dominant empirical failure mode is a small probability mass
of gross wrong-basin solutions rather than uniform variance inflation, so a
local error bound alone cannot describe the operating regime.

The paper rests on a single structural premise: the sensing block of the
$k$th RIS panel factorizes as
\begin{equation}
	\bA_k(\bchi)=
	\underbrace{\bc_k(\bchi)}_{\text{space--frequency geometry}}
	\otimes
	\underbrace{\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}}_{\text{retained Maxwell--Jones}},
	\label{eq:premise}
\end{equation}
where $\bchi=[\bp^{\mathsf T},\Delta t]^{\mathsf T}$ collects the unknown
position and common clock, $\bc_k$ carries all geometry and clock
dependence, and $\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}\in\C^{d_{\rm evs}\times2}$ is fixed
by the calibrated RIS-to-BS direction and receiver selection. What makes
\eqref{eq:premise} consequential is where the unknowns sit:
$\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}$ does not depend on $\bchi$, so calibrated
infrastructure fixes the polarimetric manifold of the sensing operator,
while all position and clock dependence is confined to the
space--frequency factor. Practical high-dimensional acquisition and
refinement schemes commonly rely on a discretized manifold, a generic
reduced-order projection, or a relaxed/restarted nonlinear search, each
trading tractability for an approximation whose impact may be difficult to
quantify. Here, the Maxwell--Jones factorization permits
structure-preserving alternatives to the first two, while the common-clock
orbit replaces the jointly scaled clock coordinate by an exact conditional
profile; C1--C3 develop these alternatives at the level of information, of
data and of optimization:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1: Maxwell--Jones information splitting.} We show that
polarization enters the Fisher information as a scalar, not as a geometry:
the free-Jones Fisher information of each RIS panel reduces to a
mode-independent geometric kernel weighted only by its retained
polarization energy. Receiver choice becomes a statement about which
panels a mode reweights, and we identify when it fails -- at most two
retained Maxwell components, of full row rank, leave no polarimetric
separation between panels sharing a space--frequency signature.
\item \textbf{C2: Sufficient compression and reliable acquisition
(MKSC-GI).} We develop a parameter-independent Maxwell--Kronecker
compression that preserves the profiled-likelihood optimizer and free-Jones
information under the stated model conditions, while suppressing
out-of-subspace noise before delay recovery. Building on this compressed
representation, a Rayleigh-spaced beam-space search and scale-profiled
common-geometry fusion return a physically labeled initializer without a
fixed-grid resolution floor.
\item \textbf{C3: Certified common-clock profiling (CCOP-JVP).} We exploit
the common clock's unitary phase-ramp action to profile it exactly rather
than optimize it as a fourth outer coordinate: its orbit leaves the Jones
Gram matrix invariant, converting the conditional clock profile into a
finite trigonometric polynomial; branch-and-bound then returns an
inner solution certified globally optimal to a prescribed tolerance by a
deterministic objective-gap bound at every visited position, reducing the
outer search from four to three dimensions.
\end{itemize}

Together C1--C3 yield the two-phase MKSC-GI+CCOP-JVP estimator: Phase~I
converts the unlabeled multilinear factors into a physically associated
near-field initializer, and Phase~II refines position and clock on the
exact complex OFDM likelihood under a fixed directional Jones penalty.
Numerical results show that the resulting estimator primarily improves
basin reliability rather than high-SNR inlier precision, motivating the
joint reporting of conditional accuracy, basin capture, catastrophic-event
rates and clock certificates in Section~\ref{sec:simulation}.
Sections~\ref{sec:system_model}--\ref{sec:simulation} present the signal
model, the estimator, the C1--C3 analysis and complexity, and the Monte
Carlo evaluation, respectively.

\section{System and Channel Model}
\label{sec:system_model}

We consider a blocked-LoS wideband uplink from a single-antenna user
equipment (UE) to a base station (BS) equipped with an $M_A$-element
electromagnetic vector-sensor (EVS) uniform linear array (ULA), assisted by
$K$ distributed reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) panels of
$M_R=M_{Rx}M_{Ry}$ elements each. The BS and RIS panels are fixed, calibrated infrastructure, whereas the
UE position, a common UE--BS clock offset, and the per-panel complex Jones
coefficients are unknown. The UE's direct path to the BS is weak enough to
be neglected under obstructed propagation, as is standard in RIS-aided
localization~\cite{fascista2022rissync,zhao2021timing}, so the signal
arrives only after reflection at the $K$ panels, which sweep $T$ known
phase configurations.

Each electrically large panel operates in a one-sided near-field cascade:
the propagation regime of each hop is set by its link distance relative to
the aperture- and wavelength-dependent near-/far-field boundaries, not by
carrier frequency alone. For the reference panel diagonal $D\simeq0.226$~m
at $f_c=60$~GHz, these boundaries place the reactive boundary at $0.94$~m
and the Fraunhofer distance at $20.5$~m, so the $28.6$--$31.2$~m RIS--BS
links lie in the panel far field and use center-frequency plane waves,
whereas the $3.6$--$4.2$~m UE--RIS links fall in the radiative near field
and retain exact spherical-wave phase differences. This one-sided
convention is that of XL-RIS channels~\cite{yang2024nfris,cui2022channel},
applied once per panel.

The UE transmits pilots on $N$ subcarriers spaced by $\Delta f$, giving an
occupied bandwidth $B_{\rm occ}=(N-1)\Delta f$. Retaining $d_{\rm evs}$ of the six Maxwell components gives the
receive dimension $I=d_{\rm evs}M_A$, and the receive tensor
$\mathcal Y\in\C^{I\times N\times T}$ holds one such block per subcarrier
and configuration. Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry} shows the reference geometry.
Calibrated infrastructure fixes the RIS--BS spatial and Maxwell manifolds,
whereas the UE position and common clock enter through the UE--RIS
wavefront and cascaded OFDM delay, respectively.
Section~\ref{subsec:calibrated} builds the former, Section~\ref{subsec:defchain}
the latter, and Section~\ref{subsec:obsmodel} assembles them into the
observation model and the estimation problem.

\subsection{Calibrated RIS--BS and EVS Response}
\label{subsec:calibrated}

Deployment calibration provides the BS phase center $\bp_B$, the RIS panel
centers $\br_k$ and rotations $\bR_k\in{\rm SO}(3)$, the element coordinates
$\brho_{k,m}\in\R^3$ in the $k$th panel frame, and the BS array and EVS
Maxwell manifolds. The RIS training codes $\bOmega_k\in\C^{T\times M_R}$ are
prescribed and likewise known, with constant-modulus entries implemented by
passive phase shifters, $[\bOmega_k]_{t,m}=e^{j\omega_{k,t,m}}/\sqrt{M_R}$,
as in RIS channel-estimation practice~\cite{lin2021tensor}. Elements are
centered on an
$M_{Rx}\times M_{Ry}$ grid of spacings $(d_x,d_y)$, the BS ULA lies along the
global $x$ axis, $\{\bm u_x,\bm u_y,\bm u_z\}$ are the global axes, and
$\kappa=2\pi/\lambda_c$ is the center-frequency wavenumber; explicit
coordinate and gauge conventions are in the supplement. Calibration error is
not assumed away but swept in Section~\ref{sec:simulation}.

Consequently, the RIS--BS distance and propagation direction of panel $k$,
\begin{equation}
	d_{{\rm RB},k}=\|\bp_B-\br_k\|_2,
	\qquad
	\bs_{{\rm RB},k}=\frac{\bp_B-\br_k}{d_{{\rm RB},k}}
	\label{eq:rbgeom}
\end{equation}
are known before UE estimation. Because this hop lies beyond the panel
Fraunhofer distance, it contributes a plane-wave panel response and a BS
steering vector,
\begin{align}
	\big[\ba_{{\rm RB},k}\big]_m
	&=e^{-j\kappa\,\brho_{k,m}^{\mathsf T}\bR_k\bs_{{\rm RB},k}},\nonumber\\
	\big[\bv_{{\rm B},k}\big]_i
	&=e^{j\kappa x_i\,\bm u_x^{\mathsf T}\bs_{{\rm RB},k}}.
	\label{eq:arb_vb}
\end{align}

The same calibrated propagation direction also fixes the polarization
manifold seen by the EVS array. A transverse plane wave along
$\bs_{{\rm RB},k}$ has Maxwell pair
$(\bE,\bH)=(\bq,\bs_{{\rm RB},k}\times\bq)$ in impedance-normalized units, so
with the transverse basis
\begin{equation}
	\bq_{k,1}=\frac{\bs_{{\rm RB},k}\times\bm u_z}
	{\|\bs_{{\rm RB},k}\times\bm u_z\|_2},
	\qquad
	\bq_{k,2}=\bs_{{\rm RB},k}\times\bq_{k,1},
	\label{eq:qbasis}
\end{equation}
with the supplementary fallback axis that keeps \eqref{eq:qbasis} well
conditioned near the pole, the six-component vector-sensor
manifold~\cite{nehorai1994vector,khan2020sixcomponent} specializes to the
calibrated Maxwell--Jones block
\begin{equation}
	\bTheta_k^{(6)}=\begin{bmatrix}
	\bq_{k,1} & \bq_{k,2}\\[1pt]
	\bq_{k,2} & -\bq_{k,1}\end{bmatrix}\in\C^{6\times2},
	\label{eq:Theta}
\end{equation}
whose columns are orthogonal of squared norm two, so
$[\bTheta_k^{(6)}]^{\mathsf H}\bTheta_k^{(6)}=2\bI_2$. The basis is a gauge:
another transverse frame differs by a $2\times2$ orthogonal factor the Jones
state absorbs. A fixed receiver selection
$\bS_{\rm mode}\in\R^{d_{\rm evs}\times6}$, with $d_{\rm evs}=1,2,6$ for the
scalar, dual-polarized, and full-EVS modes retaining respectively the first
electric component, the first two electric components, and all six, then
gives the retained block
$\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}\triangleq\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_k^{(6)}
\in\C^{d_{\rm evs}\times2}$. Full EVS presumes calibrated co-located electric
and magnetic components with matched noise and dynamic range; the reduced
selections stand for receivers retaining fewer.

Hence the RIS--BS spatial and Maxwell manifolds are fixed before UE
estimation; only the associated complex Jones-domain path coefficients
$\bx_k$ remain unknown. All position and clock dependence enters through
the UE--RIS wavefront and the OFDM delay developed next.

\subsection{UE-Dependent Near-Field Response and Common Clock}
\label{subsec:defchain}

The unknown nonlinear parameter collects the UE position and the single
clock, $\bchi=[\bp^{\mathsf T},\Delta t]^{\mathsf T}
=[x,y,z,\Delta t]^{\mathsf T}\in\R^4$, with $\bp$ in a known service box
$\mathcal P$ and $\Delta t$ in a known interval $\mathcal T$ shorter than the
unambiguous delay period $1/\Delta f$. Distributed panels deliver mutually
asynchronous cascaded delays~\cite{zhao2021timing}; here each is a geometric
path length plus one UE--BS offset shared by every panel, as in asynchronous
RIS localization~\cite{fascista2022rissync,gong2024async,yan2025ris}. What
distinguishes the present case is that the blocked direct path leaves nothing
to difference $\Delta t$ against, so it stays coupled to range in every
cascaded delay; path-specific constant phases are absorbed into the Jones
coefficients.
Seen in the $k$th panel frame the UE is at $\bxi_k(\bp)=\bR_k(\bp-\br_k)$,
from which
\begin{equation}
	d_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp)=\|\bxi_k(\bp)\|_2,
	\qquad
	\bs_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp)=\frac{\bxi_k(\bp)}{d_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp)}
	\label{eq:urgeom}
\end{equation}
with $d_{{\rm UR},k}=\|\bp-\br_k\|_2$ because $\bR_k$ is a rotation. The
first two components of $\bs_{{\rm UR},k}$ are the direction cosines
$(u_k,v_k)$, so the local parameterization searched in
Section~\ref{sec:algorithm} is
$\boldsymbol\eta_k=(d_{{\rm UR},k},u_k,v_k)$, in bijection with $\bxi_k$ on
the half-space in front of the panel.
The near-field structure stays inside the RIS response,
\begin{equation}
	\bh_k(\bp) = \bOmega_k\,\bg_k(\bp),
	\qquad
	\bg_k(\bp) = \ba_{{\rm RB},k}\odot\ba^{\rm NF}_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp),
	\label{eq:hk}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
	\big[\ba^{\rm NF}_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp)\big]_m
	&= e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_c}\delta_{k,m}(\bp)},\nonumber\\
	\delta_{k,m}(\bp)
	&= \big\|\bxi_k(\bp)-\brho_{k,m}\big\|_2
	- d_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp),
	\label{eq:delta}
\end{align}
is the exact wavefront difference between element $m$ and the panel center.
No Fresnel or parabolic approximation enters the forward model; hence
$\bh_k(\bp)$ retains the exact spherical-wave dependence across the panel
aperture.

Combining \eqref{eq:rbgeom} and \eqref{eq:urgeom}, the cascaded delay and
its OFDM phase ramp are
\begin{align}
	\tau_k(\bp,\Delta t)
	&= \underbrace{\frac{d_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp) + d_{{\rm RB},k}}{c_0}}_{
	\textstyle \tau_{g,k}(\bp)} + \Delta t,\nonumber\\
	\bd_k(\bp,\Delta t)
	&= \big[\,e^{-j2\pi n\Delta f\,\tau_k(\bp,\Delta t)}\,\big]_{n=0}^{N-1}
	\in\C^N,
	\label{eq:dk}
\end{align}
with $c_0$ the speed of light. Spatial manifolds are evaluated at the carrier
$f_c$ while this delay phase is retained on every subcarrier; the term so
neglected is the spatial-wideband phase $\epsilon_{{\rm sq},k}$, whose
largest value over subcarriers, elements and the three panels is $0.102$~rad
in the reference configuration. This is an audit of the neglected term and
not a measurement of its effect on the estimate; the closed-form design
condition and the per-panel audit are supplementary.

The per-panel sensing block separates into three independent modes, the
near-field/far-field spatial response, the OFDM delay ramp and the
calibrated Maxwell--Jones response, whose Kronecker product is the
premise~\eqref{eq:premise}, now established rather than asserted:
\begin{equation}
	\bA_k(\bchi)
	= \bh_k(\bp)\otimes\bd_k(\bp,\Delta t)\otimes\bBm_k
	= \bc_k(\bchi)\otimes\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})},
	\label{eq:Ak}
\end{equation}
where $\bBm_k=\bv_{{\rm B},k}\otimes\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}\in\C^{I\times2}$
is the calibrated receive block and, by associativity of the Kronecker
product, $\bc_k(\bchi)=\bh_k(\bp)\otimes\bd_k(\bp,\Delta t)\otimes
\bv_{{\rm B},k}\in\C^{TNM_A}$ isolates the space--frequency factor. One
scalar sample shows how: pilots are unit modulus and removed by pilot
normalization, so with $y_{i,n,t}$ the sample at receive index $i$,
subcarrier $n$, and configuration $t$,
\begin{equation}
	y_{i,n,t}\!=\!\sum_{k=1}^{K}\!
	\Big(\!\sum_{m=1}^{M_R}\!\![\bOmega_k]_{t,m}
	[\bg_k]_m\!\Big)\!
	e^{-j2\pi n\Delta f\tau_k}
	[\bBm_k]_{i,:}\bx_k\!+\!n_{i,n,t},
	\label{eq:scalar}
\end{equation}
\noindent where $\bg_k=\bg_k(\bp)$. The inner sum is the reflection at panel
$k$, the $t$th entry of $\bh_k(\bp)$ in \eqref{eq:hk}, carrying both the
spherical UE--RIS and plane-wave RIS--BS phases through $\bg_k$; the
exponential is the OFDM ramp of the cascaded delay, and
$[\bBm_k]_{i,:}\bx_k$ is the Maxwell--Jones response at receive index $i$.
All $K$ panels reflect in every configuration, so their contributions add
coherently, and because \eqref{eq:scalar} separates in $t$, $n$ and $i$,
ordering $\mathcal Y$ with the EVS index fastest and stacking reproduces
\eqref{eq:Ak}. The data, sensing matrix, Jacobian and information bounds
therefore inherit $\bS_{\rm mode}$ while $\bc_k$ does not.

\subsection{Observation Model and Estimation Problem}
\label{subsec:obsmodel}

Stacking the panels as
$\bA(\bchi)=[\bA_1(\bchi),\ldots,\bA_K(\bchi)]\in\C^{M\times 2K}$ and
vectorizing $\mathcal Y$ in the same order gives
\begin{equation}
	\by = \bmu(\bchi,\bx) + \bn = \bA(\bchi)\,\bx + \bn,
	\qquad
	\bn\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\sigma^2\bI_M),
	\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
where $M=I\,N\,T$ and the noise is white across receive channels,
subcarriers and RIS configurations, with power $\sigma^2$ \emph{not}
provided to the estimator. Gain and polarization
combine as $\bx_k=\beta_k\be_k\in\C^2$ with
$\be_k=[\sin\gamma_k e^{j\psi_k},\ \cos\gamma_k]^{\mathsf T}$ and
$\bx=[\bx_1^{\mathsf T},\ldots,\bx_K^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}\in\C^{2K}$, the
unit Jones state $\be_k$ carrying $\gamma_k\in[0,\pi/2]$ and
$\psi_k\in(-\pi,\pi]$ because its global phase is absorbed into the complex
gain $\beta_k$; only the product $\beta_k\be_k$ is identifiable. The gain
absorbs path loss, RIS efficiency and the center-frequency phase of the
cascade, and both factors are taken flat over the occupied band.

The model rests on four further assumptions. Panel phase shifts are scalar
and identical for the two polarizations, so a panel redirects a wave without
mixing its Jones components. The cyclic prefix is long enough to cover the
cascaded delay spread, so the standard per-subcarrier OFDM model applies
without delay-induced intersymbol or intercarrier interference; residual
carrier-frequency offset and oscillator phase noise are neglected. Each RIS
panel contributes one dominant cascaded component, with no direct leakage
or additional diffuse multipath in the nominal model, so the model order
equals the known panel count $K$. And the $T$ configurations are swept
within one coherence block, so $\bchi$, the Jones states and the gains stay
constant over them. Section~\ref{sec:simulation} probes departures from the
nominal model through model-order mismatch, colored noise, additional
propagation components, and the training budget the coherence assumption
implies, and reports model order as the one assumption that fails abruptly.
Spatial-wideband effects and general multipath remain outside the
estimator model.

Because $\sigma^2$ is unknown, profiling it at
$\hat\sigma^2=\|\by-\bA\bx\|_2^2/M$ concentrates the log-likelihood to
$-M\ln(\pi e\hat\sigma^2)$, strictly decreasing in residual energy, so
maximum-likelihood estimation of $(\bchi,\bx)$ is the nominal least-squares
problem
\begin{equation}
	\min_{\bp\in\mathcal P,\ \Delta t\in\mathcal T,\ \bx\in\C^{2K}}
	\big\|\by-\bA(\bp,\Delta t)\,\bx\big\|_2^2.
	\label{eq:ml_problem}
\end{equation}
The $2K$ Jones coefficients enter linearly and admit closed-form elimination,
whereas position and clock enter nonlinearly through \eqref{eq:delta} and
\eqref{eq:dk}. Nonconvex in four real coordinates, \eqref{eq:ml_problem} is
the data-fidelity term of both phases: Phase~I supplies a physically
associated initializer, and Phase~II minimizes it augmented by the
directional Jones penalty~\eqref{eq:penalized_short}
(Remark~\ref{rem:peb_reading}). One structural consequence closes the model:
the $K$ cascaded delays supply only $K$ constraints on the four unknowns, so
at $K=3$ they leave one direction that range-only reasoning cannot recover
and the near-field aperture supplies, the phase slope and curvature of
\eqref{eq:delta} varying with position across a
panel~\cite{wymeersch2020nearfield}; Section~\ref{sec:analysis} states the
local rank condition.

\section{Proposed Estimator: MKSC-GI Initialized CCOP-JVP}
\label{sec:algorithm}

Section~\ref{sec:system_model} leaves the estimator with
\eqref{eq:ml_problem}, nonconvex in the four coordinates of $\bchi$ and
carrying $2K$ linear Jones nuisances. Direct joint optimization of
\eqref{eq:ml_problem} is sensitive to initialization: position and clock sit
inside every one of $INT$ samples, and a small residual alone does not
identify the correct physical panel assignment or basin. What follows
therefore takes the
observation apart in the order in which structure removes obstacles: it
applies an information-preserving receive compression under the conditions
of Section~\ref{sec:analysis}, extracts the
delays without knowing position or clock, recovers the remaining factors
linearly once the delays are fixed, assigns physical panel labels to those
factors, and fuses the labels into one common position. Only then does it
optimize \eqref{eq:ml_problem}, augmented by a fixed directional Jones
penalty, and profiles the common clock through a certified inner solve. The
first five steps are Phase~I,
\emph{Maxwell--Kronecker sufficient compression with common-geometry
initialization} (MKSC-GI), and the last Phase~II, \emph{common-clock
orbit-profiled Jones variable projection} (CCOP-JVP).

\subsection{Maxwell--Kronecker Factor Recovery}

\textit{1) Shared-delay recovery and sufficient compression:}
Position and clock are entangled in every cascaded delay, so neither can be
estimated before the delays themselves. Equation \eqref{eq:dk} shows the way
out: the delay of path $k$ reaches the observation only through the single
pole $z_k=e^{-j2\pi\Delta f\tau_k}$, shared by the two frequency factors
$\bd_{P,k}=[z_k^p]_{p=0}^{P-1}$ and $\bd_{L,k}=[z_k^\ell]_{\ell=0}^{L-1}$, so
under the pole-separation, smoothing-dimension and snapshot-rank conditions
of Section~\ref{sec:analysis} the $K$ delays are identifiable from the
frequency axis alone, with no knowledge of $\bchi$. Smoothing that axis into
a Hankel pair with $P+L-1=N$ turns the shared pole into a shift invariance
that subspace methods read off
directly~\cite{roy1989esprit,gong2026aimdf}, forming
$\mathcal Z\in\C^{I\times P\times L\times T}$. Both factors are always
rebuilt from the same estimated $z_k$, never projected independently, which
keeps the delay one degree of freedom per path rather than two.

Before the poles are estimated, the receive dimension can be reduced
without loss under the conditions established in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
Each EVS-array path lies in a known two-dimensional Maxwell--Jones
subspace fixed by \eqref{eq:premise}, so every admissible response lies in
the span of those $K$ known planes, of dimension at most $2K$, and the
orthogonal receive directions carry noise but no signal. Since $\bBm_k$
already carries the receiver selection, set $\bPsi_k=\bBm_k\in\C^{I\times2}$
and let $\bQ_S\in\C^{I\times r}$ hold the left singular vectors of
$\bPsi=[\bPsi_1,\ldots,\bPsi_K]$ whose singular values exceed $10^{-12}$ of
the largest, so $r\le2K$ numerically and $I=96$, $r=6$ in the reference
setting. The basis uses no UE position, gain or true Jones direction, and
only the EVS mode is compressed,
$\mathcal Z_{\rm d}=\mathcal Z\times_1\bQ_S^{\mathsf H}
\in\C^{r\times P\times L\times T}$. This projection plays three roles under
three distinct conditions, separated in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}; the role
used here is the removal of noise-only EVS directions.

\textit{2) Coupled factor recovery:}
Forward--backward total-least-squares estimation of signal parameters via
rotational invariance techniques
(ESPRIT)~\cite{roy1989esprit,gong2026aimdf} then estimates the $K$ poles
from $\mathcal Z_{\rm d}$, each projected onto the unit circle. Fixing the
poles reduces the remaining factor recovery to a single coupled
least-squares problem: with $\bD_\tau$ known, \eqref{eq:scalar} is
linear in every outstanding factor, so the coupled recovery is one
least-squares solve instead of the alternating iteration that unstructured
multilinear decompositions
require~\cite{kolda2009tensor,sidiropoulos2017tensor}, the algebraic economy
that makes tensor-based IRS channel estimation
attractive~\cite{lin2021tensor}; the difference here is that one pole
generates both frequency factors, so they are coupled rather than
independently identifiable. Writing $\bZ_{rT,PL}\in\C^{rT\times PL}$ for the
unfolding of
$\mathcal Z_{\rm d}$ that groups the EVS and training modes against the two
frequency modes, and $\bD_\tau=[\bd_{P,k}\otimes\bd_{L,k}]_{k=1}^K$, the
coupled factors follow from
\begin{equation}
 \hat\bF=\bZ_{rT,PL}\bD_\tau^{\ast}
 (\bD_\tau^{\mathsf T}\bD_\tau^{\ast}+\lambda_\tau\bI)^{-1},
 \label{eq:coupledls_short}
\end{equation}
whose loading $\lambda_\tau$ is a trace-scaled Gram term plus a positive
floor, both fixed in the supplement. Column $j$ of
$\hat\bF$ is reshaped to
$\C^{r\times T}$ and split by its leading singular pair into an EVS
space--polarization factor and an RIS training factor, the former lifted by
$\bQ_S$ to $\tilde\ba_j\in\C^{I}$ and the latter kept as
$\tilde\bt_j\in\C^{T}$, the ratio $\varrho_j=\sigma_{2,j}/\sigma_{1,j}$ of
that split retained as a per-column confidence used below to weight the
common-geometry fit.

\textit{3) Physical panel association:}
What the split returns is still algebraic: nothing ties column $j$ to panel
$k$, yet every downstream step needs that tie. Association is therefore
settled before any geometry is fused, by searching permutations under a
composite cost that combines three complementary cues: Maxwell-subspace
consistency identifies the panel-dependent receive manifold, spherical-wave
consistency tests the panel-local geometry, and the common-clock replicas
enforce a joint cross-panel constraint. For column $j$ and panel $k$,
\begin{equation}
 s_{j,k}=
 \frac{\|\bPi^{\perp}_{\bPsi_k}\tilde\ba_j\|}{\|\tilde\ba_j\|}
 +\min_{\boldsymbol\eta\in\mathcal E_k}
 \frac{\|\bPi^{\perp}_{\bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)}\tilde\bt_j\|}
 {\|\tilde\bt_j\|},
 \label{eq:assoc_score}
\end{equation}
with $\bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)$ the exact near-field panel response
\eqref{eq:hk} and $\mathcal E_k$ the local search box; both terms are
relative residual norms in $[0,1]$, so they combine without a calibration
constant. Writing $\hat d_{j,k}$ for the range coordinate of the minimizer
in \eqref{eq:assoc_score} and $\hat\tau_j$ for the delay of the estimated
pole, each pair also implies a clock replica
$\widehat{\Delta t}_{j,k}=\hat\tau_j-(\hat d_{j,k}+d_{{\rm RB},k})/c_0$,
and a permutation is scored by
\begin{equation}
 C(\pi)=\sum_{j=1}^{K}s_{j,\pi(j)}
 +\frac{w_{\rm clk}}{t_0}
 \big[\,\mathrm{sd}(\pi)+e_{\mathcal T}(\pi)\big],
 \label{eq:assoc_perm}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sd}(\pi)$ is the spread of the $K$ replicas
$\widehat{\Delta t}_{j,\pi(j)}$ about their mean, $e_{\mathcal T}(\pi)$ is
their mean excursion outside $\mathcal T$, $w_{\rm clk}=1/2$, and
$t_0=1$~ns.
The three cues are complementary: Maxwell mismatch alone cannot separate a
wrong panel under receiver modes that lose the required polarimetric
discrimination, RIS residual alone can favor a
near-field sidelobe, and delay alone is ambiguous near the coincidence
surface. The clock term also makes the decision joint, being a function of
the whole permutation, so $C(\pi)$ is not separable into pairwise costs and
is not reducible to a standard linear-assignment problem without
approximation. That structural
role is separate from an empirical one: Section~\ref{sec:simulation} sweeps
$w_{\rm clk}$ and finds no evidence that it improves basin capture. The
search over $C$ is two-tier, all $K!$ permutations ranked on a coarse
correlation and the two lowest re-scored with the exact spherical projection,
their relative gap retained as an assignment margin, so the fine search runs
once per panel rather than once per $(j,k)$ pair. The shortlist carries no
optimality certificate, and its first tier is the one part of the estimator
whose cost grows factorially in $K$, negligible at $K=3$ but a real limit on
panel count.

\subsection{Beam-Space Acquisition and Common-Geometry Initialization}
\label{subsec:beamspace}

Labelled factors are still only training-domain vectors; the geometry they
encode must be read back out through the model of
Section~\ref{subsec:defchain}, one panel at a time, before the $K$ readings
can be reconciled into a single position. With $\tilde\bt_k$ the
training-domain factor that $\hat\pi$ assigns to panel $k$, its local
range--direction estimate minimizes
\begin{equation}
 J_{\rm RIS}(\boldsymbol\eta)
 =\|\bPi_{\bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)}^\perp\tilde\bt_k\|^2,
 \quad \bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)=\bOmega_k
 [\ba_{{\rm RB},k}\odot\ba^{\rm NF}(\boldsymbol\eta)],
 \label{eq:Jris_short}
\end{equation}
over $\boldsymbol\eta=(d_{{\rm UR},k},u_k,v_k)$ as parameterized in
Section~\ref{subsec:defchain}. A fixed-count angular dictionary of the polar
or beam-training kind~\cite{cui2022channel,zheng2025wideband} undersamples
large panels because its step is set by the search box while the panel
resolution $\lambda_c/D$ improves with aperture. Beam-space acquisition
moves the dominant angular search from the training domain to the element
domain, avoiding reliance on a fixed-count dictionary in the reference
large-aperture regime. Because the training code enters $\bh_k$ only on
the left, the numerator correlation of \eqref{eq:Jris_short} satisfies the
identity
\begin{equation}
 \bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bt_k
 =\big[\ba^{\rm NF}(\boldsymbol\eta)\big]^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bg_k,
 \quad
 \tilde\bg_k=\ba_{{\rm RB},k}^{\ast}\odot
 (\bOmega_k^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bt_k),
 \label{eq:adjoint_short}
\end{equation}
so one adjoint pass moves the factor into the element domain for every
subsequent trial geometry, an exact rewriting of the correlation and not an
inversion of $\bOmega_k$. The Fresnel expansion of \eqref{eq:delta} splits
the remaining phase into a term linear in the direction cosines and a
quadratic term fixed by the trial range $r$, so the dechirp
$\tilde\bg_{k,r}[m]=\tilde\bg_k[m]e^{j\kappa\|\brho_{k,m}\|^2/(2r)}$
leaves, to Fresnel order, a plane-wave sum over the element lattice, and one
zero-padded two-dimensional discrete Fourier transform (2-D DFT), evaluated
in $(u,v)$ with $\kappa$ and $(d_x,d_y)$ as in
Section~\ref{subsec:calibrated}, scores every direction cosine at once. The
direction-cosine step tracks the Rayleigh limit and removes the boresight
azimuth singularity: for the reference $64\times64$ half-wavelength panel,
fourfold zero padding gives $\Delta u=1/128$, four samples per $1/32$
Rayleigh mainlobe, at an $O(N_xN_y\log(N_xN_y))$ cost decoupled from any
dictionary size. Range resolves more weakly than direction, so each visible
angular peak receives an exact spherical range scan before re-scoring by
\eqref{eq:Jris_short} and local refinement; the fast Fourier transform (FFT)
only proposes candidates and never replaces the spherical model.

Candidates are the union of the beam-space proposals and the fixed-count
dictionary, every member judged by the same exact criterion
\eqref{eq:Jris_short}, so enlarging the set can only lower the achieved
objective and a proposal that never attains the minimum can be dropped
without changing any output, which is the situation for the reference
$64\times64$ panel. The fixed-count set is retained only where the aperture
is swept down, the beam-space advantage reversing for a sufficiently small
panel; the candidate set changes acquisition coverage and cost, not the
forward model. Each panel-local fix $\hat{\boldsymbol\eta}_k$ maps through the calibrated
panel pose to a global position $\hat\bp_k^{\rm loc}$. These $K$ positions
disagree, and the common-geometry stage replaces them by the single
position that best explains all $K$ training factors at once -- the first
point at which every physically labeled panel is forced to agree on one UE
position. Unknown complex factor scales are eliminated in the objective
\begin{align}
 J_{\rm geom}(\bp)
 &=\sum_{k=1}^K w_k\left[\|\tilde\bt_k\|^2-
 \frac{|\bh_k^{\mathsf H}(\bp)\tilde\bt_k|^2}{\|\bh_k(\bp)\|^2}\right]
 \label{eq:Jgeom_short}\\
 	 &=\min_{\balpha\in\C^K}\sum_{k=1}^K w_k
 \|\tilde\bt_k-\alpha_k\bh_k(\bp)\|^2,\nonumber
\end{align}
whose profiled scale
$\hat\alpha_k=\bh_k^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bt_k/\|\bh_k\|^2$ makes the two lines
identical, so $J_{\rm geom}$ sees only the component of $\tilde\bt_k$
orthogonal to $\coll(\bh_k)$ and is blind to the unknown path gains. The
weights $w_k=\max\{e^{-\varrho_k/\varrho_0},w_{\min}\}$, with
$\varrho_0=1/2$ and $w_{\min}=0.05$, down-weight a column whose rank-one
split is poor while keeping every panel in the fit. Four deterministic
bounded L-BFGS-B~\cite{byrd1995lbfgsb} starts minimize
\eqref{eq:Jgeom_short}, built in fixed order from the clipped coordinate-wise
median $\hat\bp_0=\clip_{\mathcal P}\{\median_k\hat\bp_k^{\rm loc}\}$ and
listed in the supplement; no random restart and no oracle state is used.

At the selected $\hat\bp_{\rm GI}$, one unregularized fixed-position clock
profile refreshes the Jones directions,
\begin{equation}
 (\hat{\Delta t}_{\rm ref},\hat\bx_{\rm ref})=
 \arg\min_{\Delta t\in\mathcal T,\bx}
 \|\by-\bA(\hat\bp_{\rm GI},\Delta t)\bx\|^2.
 \label{eq:refresh_short}
\end{equation}
With $\hat\bx_{{\rm ref},k}$ the $k$th two-entry block of $\hat\bx_{\rm ref}$,
the anchor is $\hat\be_k=\hat\bx_{{\rm ref},k}/\|\hat\bx_{{\rm ref},k}\|_2$,
re-estimated rather than frozen at the common position using the globally
solved conditional clock profile, so the anchors are data adaptive and not
independent side information; the degenerate zero-norm case is handled in
the supplement.

\subsection{Common-Clock Orbit-Profiled Jones Variable Projection}
\label{subsec:ccop}

Phase~II returns to the exact criterion \eqref{eq:ml_problem} from the basin
just built. Its $2K$ Jones coefficients enter linearly and are eliminated in
closed form by variable projection~\cite{golub1973varpro,liu2026extended},
leaving four nonlinear coordinates. The common clock is shared by all panels
and enters every cascaded delay in the same direction as range. More
importantly, it is the only nonlinear coordinate along which the likelihood
admits an exact unitary symmetry. Let
$\bA_0(\bp)\triangleq\bA(\bp,0)$ and
$\bD_t(\Delta t)=\diag([e^{-j2\pi n\Delta f\Delta t}]_{n=0}^{N-1})$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \bU_t(\Delta t)=\bI_T\otimes\bD_t(\Delta t)\otimes\bI_I,
 \quad \bA(\bp,\Delta t)=\bU_t(\Delta t)\bA_0(\bp),
 \label{eq:orbit_short}
\end{equation}
and unitarity makes the Jones Gram clock invariant,
$\bA^{\mathsf H}\bA=\bA_0^{\mathsf H}\bA_0\triangleq\bG_0(\bp)$.
Phase~II uses a Gram-scaled block-diagonal penalty
\begin{equation}
 \bR_{\rm dir}(\bp)=\blkdiag_k\left[
 \lambda_k\tfrac12\tr\bG_{0,kk}(\bp)\,\bPi_{\hat\be_k}^{\perp}\right],
 \label{eq:Rdir_short}
\end{equation}
which penalizes departure of $\bx_k$ from the refreshed anchor direction
$\hat\be_k$ without freezing either coefficient. The dimensionless weight is
set to $\lambda_k=1$ throughout, so the penalty enters at the scale of the
per-path data Gram; being a fixed multiple of that Gram rather than a
function of the noise level it neither anneals nor vanishes with SNR, so the
free-Jones bound of Section~\ref{sec:analysis} is a reference and not a
claimed finite-sample bound. The weight is swept in
Section~\ref{subsec:res_c1}.

Phase~II accordingly minimizes
\begin{equation}
 J(\bp,\Delta t,\bx)=\|\by-\bA(\bp,\Delta t)\bx\|_2^2
 +\bx^{\mathsf H}\bR_{\rm dir}(\bp)\bx,
 \label{eq:penalized_short}
\end{equation}
whose first term is the nominal criterion \eqref{eq:ml_problem}. With
$\bH=\bG_0+\bR_{\rm dir}$ and
$\bb=\bA_0^{\mathsf H}\bU_t^{\mathsf H}\by$, the inner minimizer is
$\hat\bx=\bH^{-1}\bb$ and analytic Jones elimination gives
$F(\bp,\Delta t)\triangleq\min_{\bx\in\C^{2K}}J=\|\by\|^2-Q(\bp,\Delta t)$
with $Q=\bb^{\mathsf H}\bH^{-1}\bb$, hence the 3-D outer objective
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde F(\bp)=\|\by\|^2-
 \max_{\Delta t\in\mathcal T}Q(\bp,\Delta t).
 \label{eq:nested_short}
\end{equation}
Grouping the sufficient-statistic correlations by tone as
$\bb(\bp,\Delta t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\bu_n(\bp)
e^{j2\pi n\Delta f\Delta t}$, the quadratic form through the
clock-independent $\bH^{-1}$ retains only tone differences $\ell=n-n'$.
Hence, for fixed $\bp$, $Q$ is a real trigonometric polynomial of degree at
most $N-1$,
\begin{equation}
 Q(\bp,\Delta t)=\sum_{\ell=-(N-1)}^{N-1}c_\ell(\bp)
 e^{j2\pi\ell\Delta f\Delta t},\quad c_{-\ell}=c_\ell^\ast,
 \label{eq:trig_short}
\end{equation}
so one factorization of $\bH$ and one zero-padded FFT supply all initial
clock candidates. With
$L_2=(2\pi\Delta f)^2\sum_\ell\ell^2|c_\ell|$, an interval centered at
$t_c$ with half-width $r_t$ obeys
\begin{equation}
 Q(t)\le Q(t_c)+|Q'(t_c)|r_t+\tfrac12L_2r_t^2.
 \label{eq:bound_short}
\end{equation}
Newton refinement and branch-and-bound therefore return an incumbent with
$Q(\Delta t^\star)-Q(\hat{\Delta t})\le U-Q(\hat{\Delta t})
\triangleq g_{\rm cert}$, where $U$ is the largest surviving interval upper
bound. Subdivision stops once $g_{\rm cert}\le\epsilon_{\rm clk}$, an
absolute-plus-relative tolerance fixed in the supplement under the cap
$N_{\rm cap}=2\times10^{4}$, and the bound remains valid if that cap is
reached. Theorem~\ref{thm:clock} states the guarantee, which covers the
continuous interval and not merely the FFT grid.

The outer bounded search starts from $\hat\bp_{\rm GI}$ and uses the
envelope-theorem gradient of $Q$ when the maximizing clock branch is unique
and interior; a nearly tied pair of peaks is instead handled as a branch
event triggering a Powell safeguard~\cite{powell1964direct}, detailed in the
supplement. The certificate is only for the inner clock maximum, not the
nonconvex outer optimum, and the noise variance is not an input to the
optimization. The supplement collects the two phases as pseudocode.


\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section proves three structural claims. C1 characterizes how EVS
component selection reshapes geometric information and panel discrimination;
C2 establishes the statistical sufficiency of the Maxwell--Kronecker
compression; and C3 proves the exact nested clock profile, its unit
invariance, and the branch-and-bound certificate, after first fixing the
free-Jones reference information and local identifiability condition they
rest on. Full proofs are supplementary.

\textit{A) Reference information and identifiability:}
Let $\bD_\chi=[\partial(\bA\bx)/\partial\chi_i]_{i=1}^4$. Eliminating the
unknown complex Jones coefficients from the white-Gaussian Fisher
information matrix gives the data-only free-Jones equivalent Fisher
information matrix (EFIM)
\begin{equation}
 \bJ_\chi^{\rm free}=\frac{2}{\sigma^2}
 \Real\{\bD_\chi^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}.
 \label{eq:efim_short}
\end{equation}
Local position--clock identifiability after eliminating the Jones nuisance
requires
\begin{equation}
 \rank_{\R}[\Real\{\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}^{\mathsf T},
 \Imag\{\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}=4,
 \label{eq:rankcond}
\end{equation}
which is exactly $\bJ_\chi^{\rm free}\succ\mathbf0$, since
$\Real\{\bD_\chi^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}$ is the Gram matrix
of that real stacking. When $\bJ_\chi^{\rm free}\succ\mathbf0$, partitioning
its position and clock blocks,
$\bJ_p^{\rm eff}=\bJ_{pp}-\bJ_{pt}J_{tt}^{-1}\bJ_{tp}$, gives the position
and clock error bounds (PEB, CEB),
${\rm PEB}=\sqrt{\tr[(\bJ_p^{\rm eff})^{-1}]}$ and
${\rm CEB}=\sqrt{[(\bJ_\chi^{\rm free})^{-1}]_{4,4}}$. The pseudoinverse
projector is part of the statement, not a numerical convenience: it keeps the
nested-mode PEB ordering valid when a scalar or dual mask makes the Jones
block singular. With $K=3$, the cascaded delays supply at most three
independent constraints on the four position--clock coordinates and
therefore cannot identify $\bchi$ by themselves; in generic geometry one
direction remains, supplied by the near-field aperture, since after
complex-scale elimination the projected tangents $\bPi_{\bh_k}^{\perp}
[\partial\bh_k/\partial x,\partial\bh_k/\partial y,
\partial\bh_k/\partial z]$ retain the phase slope and curvature of
\eqref{eq:delta}. The test is conditional and excludes neither global
panel-permutation nor finite-bandwidth ambiguities; its proof and the exact
spatial-narrowband design condition are supplementary.

\begin{remark}[Status of the reference bound]
\label{rem:bound_status}
Equation~\eqref{eq:efim_short} treats gains and Jones directions as unknown
deterministic nuisances and does not use the Phase~I anchors, so it is the
free-Jones data-model reference. It is conservative relative to a genie with
known Jones directions, whose gain is numerically negligible in the tested
geometry (Section~\ref{subsec:res_setup}). The clock Schur complement removes
the clock as a reported coordinate while retaining the information carried by
the shared delay, and is not equivalent to declaring the clock known.
\end{remark}

\textit{B) C1---Maxwell--Jones information splitting:}
\begin{theorem}[C1: Maxwell--Jones information splitting]
\label{thm:split}
\textbf{(i) Information consequence.} For one panel, write
$\bA=\bc\otimes\bTheta$, $\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\bTheta\bx$,
$\dot\bc_i=\partial\bc/\partial\chi_i$, and
$\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_i=(\partial\bTheta/\partial\chi_i)\bx$. Then
\begin{equation}
 [\bD_\chi^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi]_{ij}
 =\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^2\dot\bc_i^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bc}^\perp\dot\bc_j
 +\|\bc\|^2\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_i^{\mathsf H}
 \bPi_{\bTheta}^\perp\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_j.
 \label{eq:split_short}
\end{equation}
Since calibrated infrastructure gives
$\partial\bTheta/\partial\bchi=\mathbf0$, the second term vanishes. Here and
in \eqref{eq:panelres_short}, $\bTheta=\bTheta_k^{(6)}$ is the \emph{full}
six-component block of \eqref{eq:Theta}, and $\bS_{\rm mode}$ ranges over
the candidate component selections of Section~\ref{sec:system_model}. The
mode retaining $\bS_{\rm mode}$ yields
$\bJ_\chi^{\bS_{\rm mode}}=\alpha_{\bS_{\rm mode}}\bJ_\chi^{\rm full}$ with
$\alpha_{\bS_{\rm mode}}
=\|\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta\bx\|^2/\|\bTheta\bx\|^2$ whenever the retained
signal is nonzero. This exact scaling is a single-panel statement; the
multi-panel case is treated after the theorem.

\textbf{(ii) Discrimination consequence.} For this comparison, define
$\bA_k^{(\bS)}(\bchi)=\bc_k(\bchi)\otimes
\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_k^{(6)}$. If a competitor block matches panel $k$'s
space--frequency signature, i.e.
$\bc_{k'}(\bchi')\parallel\bc_k(\bchi)$, its free-Jones residual is
\begin{equation}
 \|\bPi_{\bA_{k'}^{(\bS)}(\bchi')}^\perp
 \bA_k^{(\bS)}(\bchi)\bx_k\|
 =\|\bc_k\|\,\|\bPi_{\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_{k'}^{(6)}}^\perp
 \bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_k^{(6)}\bx_k\|.
 \label{eq:panelres_short}
\end{equation}
If the retained competitor block
$\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_{k'}^{(6)}\in\C^{d\times2}$ has full row rank for
$d\le2$, this residual is zero. With all six components it is at least
$\|\bc_k\|\|\bTheta_k^{(6)}\bx_k\|\sin\theta_{\min}$, where
$\theta_{\min}$ is the minimum principal angle between the two panel
Maxwell subspaces.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof sketch:} the projector onto $\coll(\bc\otimes\bTheta)^\perp$
splits as $\bP_{\bc}\otimes\bPi_{\bTheta}^\perp+\bPi_{\bc}^\perp\otimes\bI$,
and substitution gives both claims; the full proof is supplementary.
Equation~\eqref{eq:panelres_short} bounds one pairwise hypothesis distance
and so identifies a discrimination mechanism; it neither forces the full
multi-panel hypothesis distance to vanish nor yields a closed-form threshold
SNR.

For one panel, C1 fixes exactly what component selection can change: the
information eigenvectors are unaffected and any finite CRB derived from the
same single-panel EFIM scales by $\alpha_{\bS_{\rm mode}}^{-1/2}$. The exact
single-panel scaling does not
extend directly to the multi-panel EFIM, because \eqref{eq:efim_short} uses
$\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}$ with the full $\bA=[\bA_1,\ldots,\bA_K]$, which in
general does not factor into per-panel projectors; the per-panel sum is
exact only when the panel blocks and their projected tangents are mutually
orthogonal. In approximately block-orthogonal regimes -- small off-diagonal
Gram terms between panels -- this admits the intuitive panel-wise
representation
$\bJ_\chi^{\rm free}\approx\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\sum_k
\|\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_k^{(6)}\bx_k\|^2\bG_k$, with
$[\bG_k]_{ij}=\Real\{\dot\bc_{k,i}^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bc_k}^\perp
\dot\bc_{k,j}\}$ the selection-independent per-panel geometric matrix; this
approximation is not itself a claim of the theorem. Each panel retains a
different fraction of its polarization energy, so a selection reweights
panels unevenly and reshapes the aggregate geometry instead of rescaling it.
The separation statement \eqref{eq:panelres_short} is complementary: it
concerns a wrong-panel hypothesis and identifies a mechanism for gross
association failures that a local Cram\'er--Rao bound cannot capture,
without asserting global identifiability for the complete dictionary.

\textit{C) C2---Sufficient Maxwell--Kronecker compression:}
\begin{theorem}[C2: Maxwell--Kronecker compression]
\label{thm:compress}
Let $\bU_S=\bI_{NT}\otimes\bQ_S$ be the semi-unitary lifting matrix in the
vectorization of \eqref{eq:obs}, and suppose
$\coll[\bA(\bchi)]\subseteq\coll(\bU_S)$ for all admissible $\bchi$. Write
$\by_c=\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\by$ and
$c_\perp=\|(\bI-\bU_S\bU_S^{\mathsf H})\by\|^2$. Under the matched
white-Gaussian model:
(i) for known $\sigma^2$, $\by_c$ is sufficient for $(\bchi,\bx)$;
(ii) for unknown $\sigma^2$, the pair $(\by_c,c_\perp)$ is sufficient for
$(\bchi,\bx,\sigma^2)$, and $\by_c$ alone still preserves the maximizer in
$(\bchi,\bx)$ of the $\sigma^2$-profiled likelihood, because
\begin{equation}
 \|\by-\bA(\bchi)\bx\|^2
 =\|\by_c-\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\bA(\bchi)\bx\|^2+c_\perp
 \label{eq:isometry_short}
\end{equation}
and $c_\perp$ does not depend on $(\bchi,\bx)$;
(iii) the free-Jones EFIM of $\by_c$ equals \eqref{eq:efim_short}, and
$\by_c$ alone preserves the profiled optimizer, the parameter derivatives
and the free-Jones EFIM, with residual and Jacobian rows reduced by $I/r$;
(iv) the noiseless delay poles are preserved provided the compressed
non-frequency snapshot matrix
$[\beta_k(\bh_k\otimes\bQ_S^{\mathsf H}\bBm_k\be_k)]_{k=1}^K$ has rank $K$,
which holds generically for nondegenerate panel responses and training when
$rT\ge K$ but is checked rather than inferred from dimension alone.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof sketch:} a unitary completion of $\bU_S$ separates the data into a
parameter-dependent projection and noise-only coordinates entering the
likelihood only through $c_\perp$, which gives (i) and (ii); the same
semi-unitary map preserves every Gram product in \eqref{eq:efim_short} and,
acting only on the EVS mode, commutes with the shared Vandermonde delay
factors. Details are supplementary.

The projection serves distinct roles under distinct conditions, detailed in
the supplement. Likelihood sufficiency and EFIM preservation require the
matched white-noise model and the stated subspace inclusion, whereas
delay-pole preservation additionally requires the compressed snapshot-rank
condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:compress}(iv). Phase~I, however, is an
ESPRIT-based subspace procedure rather than the exact ML optimizer;
projection may therefore improve its finite-sample recovery by suppressing
noise-only EVS directions without contradicting likelihood preservation.
Under colored noise an optimal processor would whiten, so the unwhitened
sweep of
Section~\ref{subsec:res_robustness} is a mismatch test and not an extension
of Theorem~\ref{thm:compress}.

\textit{D) C3---Certified common-clock profiling:}
\begin{theorem}[C3: nested certified clock profile]
\label{thm:clock}
Assume $\bH(\bp)=\bG_0(\bp)+\bR_{\rm dir}(\bp)\succ\mathbf0$.

\textbf{(i) Exact nesting.} Analytic Jones elimination and global inner
clock maximization nest the penalized criterion \eqref{eq:penalized_short}
exactly:
$F(\bp,\Delta t)=\min_{\bx}J(\bp,\Delta t,\bx)=\|\by\|^2-Q(\bp,\Delta t)$,
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\bp\in\mathcal P}\widetilde F(\bp)
 =\min_{\bp\in\mathcal P,\,\Delta t\in\mathcal T,\,\bx\in\C^{2K}}
 J(\bp,\Delta t,\bx),
 \label{eq:nestval_short}
\end{equation}
and the two argument sets correspond, $\arg\min_{\bp}\widetilde F$ being the
projection onto $\bp$ of $\arg\min_{\bp,\Delta t,\bx}J$, so pairing any outer
minimizer $\bp^\star$ with a global inner clock maximizer of
$Q(\bp^\star,\cdot)$ and with
$\bx^\star=\bH^{-1}(\bp^\star)\bb(\bp^\star,\Delta t^\star)$ recovers a joint
minimizer.

\textbf{(ii) Unit invariance.} The outer profile is invariant under every
invertible affine clock-unit reparameterization with $\mathcal T$
transformed accordingly.

\textbf{(iii) Certified clock solution.} The interval bound
\eqref{eq:bound_short} guarantees at every visited position
\begin{equation}
 Q(\Delta t^\star)-Q(\hat{\Delta t})\le g_{\rm cert}
 =U-Q(\hat{\Delta t}),
 \label{eq:cert_short}
\end{equation}
and $g_{\rm cert}\le\epsilon_{\rm clk}$ whenever termination occurs by the
prescribed tolerance rather than the subdivision cap.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof sketch:} unitarity in \eqref{eq:orbit_short} makes $\bH$ clock
independent, so completing the square eliminates $\bx$ exactly and the order
of minimization may be exchanged, while termwise differentiation of
\eqref{eq:trig_short} bounds the second derivative so that $U$ dominates the
unknown global maximum; the full proof is supplementary. The theorem
certifies the one-dimensional clock solve and removes its outer coordinate,
not global convergence of the 3-D position search. The certificate is also
separate from its stopping test: were the subdivision cap reached,
\eqref{eq:cert_short} would still be a valid a posteriori gap without
meeting the tolerance, so the correctness of the bound and the empirical
success of the test are two distinct facts.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the free-Jones reference bound]
\label{rem:peb_reading}
A sufficient condition for the penalized and free-Jones estimators to share
the same first-order high-SNR distribution is that the anchor error is
$O_{\mathsf P}(\sigma)$ and the regularization weights vanish; the frozen
choice $\lambda_k=1$ does not satisfy this tapering condition, so no
asymptotic-efficiency claim is made. The condition is sufficient rather than
necessary: the $\lambda$-sweep in Section~\ref{subsec:res_c1} shows that
setting the weight to zero moves no inlier accuracy metric by more than $2$
parts in $10^5$. The free-Jones PEB is therefore a reference for a data
model this estimator matches empirically, not a proven bound for it, and an
observed root-mean-square error (RMSE) slightly below it is neither a bound
violation nor a proof of asymptotic efficiency.
\end{remark}

\textit{E) Complexity:}
Two features of the resulting cost matter. Each Phase~II outer position
evaluation costs $O(qM_r+q^2(N+T))+O(q^2N^2)+O(qM_c\log M_c+q^3)
+C_{\rm cert}$ with $q=2K$, $M_r=rNT$, and $M_c$ the zero-padded clock-FFT
length, so the profiled solve acts on $M_r$ compressed rows instead of the
$M=I\,N\,T$ raw rows; and the Gram update is $O(q^2(N+T))$ rather than
$O(q^2M_r)$ because the Kronecker structure of \eqref{eq:Ak} makes each Gram
block a cached position-independent Maxwell--Jones factor times one length-$N$
and one length-$T$ inner product. The term-by-term derivation is
supplementary; Section~\ref{subsec:res_cost} verifies that the exact
spherical panel projection, rather than the certified clock profile,
dominates the measured runtime.
\section{Numerical Results}
\label{sec:simulation}

\subsection{Protocol and Reference Bounds}
\label{subsec:res_setup}

Unless varied, the matched scene uses $f_c=60$~GHz, $K=3$ oriented
$64\times64$ half-wavelength RISs, an $M_A=16$ EVS ULA, $N=63$
subcarriers at $\Delta f=5$~MHz, and $T=256$ training blocks, with the UE at
$(1.25,0.55,0.75)$~m and a true clock of $5$~ns in a $[0,10]$-ns search
interval; complete geometry and signal parameters are supplementary. Path
gains and Jones states are drawn independently per trial, and SNR is the
average pre-noise sample SNR on the active receive entries,
${\rm SNR}=10\log_{10}[\|\bmu_{\rm act}\|_2^2/(M_{\rm act}\sigma^2)]$, with
absolute path loss absorbed into this normalization; the receiver-mode
study instead fixes $\sigma^2$ from the full-EVS observation and reuses it
across masks. Phase~I acquires the basin when it returns a finite
$\hat\bp_{\rm I}$ with $\|\hat\bp_{\rm I}-\bp^\star\|_2\le0.1$~m.

Each accuracy cell contains $480$ paired trials, except the external
benchmark of Section~\ref{subsec:res_benchmark}, which uses $960$, the C3
factor control at $100$ and the serialized cost cells at $30$; methods in
one cell share the scene, RIS codes, Jones states, gains and noise
realization, verified by a stored hash, while different suites use
independent seed roots. A position estimate is catastrophic if the method
fails or its error exceeds $0.1$~m, a clock estimate if its absolute error
exceeds $1$~ns; moving the position threshold to $0.05$ or $0.20$~m leaves
every stated proposed-route rate unchanged. Rate intervals are exact $95\%$
Clopper--Pearson, p95 and p99 are nearest-rank, paired differences use
bootstrap intervals, and outlier exchanges use McNemar's exact test; all
hyperparameters, every baseline included, were fixed before the reported
cells were evaluated. Each fixed-SNR study is measured at $-10$~dB except
the model-order and RIS element-phase sweeps at $0$~dB. Because different
suites use independent seed roots, their catastrophic rates at nominally
the same configuration differ by sampling alone (e.g.\ $0.31$--$0.83\%$
across the benchmark, compression and internal suites, with overlapping
$95\%$ intervals); rates are therefore never pooled across suites, and the
full audit is supplementary.

At $-10$~dB, over $20$ Jones/gain realizations at the default position, the
median free-Jones PEB and CEB are $0.404$~mm and $3.35$~ps; oracle- and
free-Jones PEBs differ by under $0.005\%$, so the data-only bound is the
sole reference, and since the EFIM scales as $2/\sigma^2$, any CEB quoted
at another SNR is this median scaled by $\sigma$. The complete bound table
is supplementary.

\subsection{External Benchmark}
\label{subsec:res_benchmark}

Five methods run on shared realizations under a method-consistent
refinement protocol (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark_combined}): every acquisition
route receives the continuous read-out or refinement its own formulation
prescribes, since ALS-CPD refines in the CP-factor domain, near-field OMP
runs SAGE sweeps followed by a weighted least-squares read-out, and
VBI/SBL receives the same joint refinement because its closed-form
read-out cannot remove the common clock without a direct path -- stopped
at that as-published rule it has $0.49$--$0.81$~m median position error
and a clock error that does not vanish with SNR, so the stronger tier is
the conservative choice. All acquisition stages use fixed medium-size
configurations, adaptations to the common forward model rather than claims
that the reference methods were designed for this blocked-LoS polarimetric
scene; sources, grids, caps, stopping rules and the as-published tier are
supplementary.

At $-10$~dB the proposed, range-scaled 4-D VP and VBI/SBL routes have
nearly identical median position errors ($0.316$, $0.320$ and $0.325$~mm)
but catastrophic rates of $0.31\%$, $1.77\%$ and $2.40\%$; near-field OMP
also has $1.77\%$ and the ALS-CPD adaptation $44.1\%$. McNemar's exact test
on the paired catastrophic indicator rejects equality against all three
closest comparators ($p\le2.6\times10^{-3}$, Bonferroni-corrected at
$5\%$), while ALS-CPD is separated by $420$ exchanges against none. From
$-5$~dB upward the proposed route has no catastrophic trial and no
comparator solves a realization it loses, whereas near-field OMP settles
into a $0.79$-mm high-SNR floor (growing from $2.6\times$ to $253\times$
the proposed median) and the ALS-CPD adaptation keeps a $30$--$34\%$
catastrophic rate from $0$ to $30$~dB, a property of this evaluated
adaptation rather than the reference CPD method. Above $0$~dB the proposed
and range-scaled 4-D routes are numerically coincident
($<1.1\times10^{-8}$~m), so no high-SNR accuracy advantage is inferred; the
tail is read from the full distribution rather than a single quantile,
since the p99 of $960$ trials straddles both modes once a route carries
about $1\%$ catastrophic mass. Full tables and quantile diagnostics are
supplementary.

Synchronization mirrors the position ranking: at $-10$~dB the
clock-catastrophic rates are $0.31\%$ for the proposed route against
$1.56$--$7.50\%$ for the four comparators, and at $0$~dB the proposed
clock RMSE ($1.05$~ps) matches a $1.06$-ps CEB reference -- descriptive
rather than a bound test, since the reference is the unpenalized-model
median at the default position and not a per-trial bound
(Remark~\ref{rem:peb_reading}). The range-scaled 4-D route matches this
inlier precision and coincides in observation-domain NMSE among inliers,
separating only through the same wrong-basin tail mass that drives the
position result; the three external routes carry
$8.1\times10^{-4}$--$2.4\times10^{-2}$ NMSE among inliers, a property of
the geometries they acquire. Below the declared region the
initializer-free OMP grid has a lower catastrophic rate than the proposed
local pipeline at $-20$~dB ($2.60\%$ against $4.48\%$) and ties it at
$-15$~dB while keeping its resolution floor, which supports a coarse
global screen as a fallback initializer and prevents any claim of uniform
dominance outside the tested region. Full tables are supplementary.

\subsection{C1: EVS Information and Resolvability}
\label{subsec:res_c1}

\textbf{Does receiver mode change accuracy?} The C1 information-splitting
identity holds to numerical precision (single-panel EFIMs match the
predicted scalar relation to $5.7\times10^{-16}$; full audit supplementary).
At $0$~dB the scalar, dual and full modes have conditional RMSEs $0.815$,
$0.330$ and $0.133$~mm, consistent with their matched PEB order
(Fig.~\ref{fig:c1_combined}).

\textbf{Does it also change discrimination?} Yes, and by more than
information alone predicts: at $-10$~dB the catastrophic rates are
$14.17\%$, $2.29\%$ and $1.04\%$, a $13.6\times$ scalar/full
catastrophic-rate ratio against the $5.7\times$ matched-PEB ratio, because
reduced modes lose panel-label separation as well as energy. The resolvability map isolates the mechanism:
at $1$~ns delay separation full EVS resolves a pair with probability
$0.87$--$0.88$ regardless of Jones overlap, while the dual-polarized and
scalar modes collapse toward zero as overlap increases -- a tested null
(Cochran--Armitage trend test insignificant for full EVS, Holm-adjusted
$p=1.00$, against $p\le10^{-181}$ for the reduced modes). Two effects
therefore act, a shared bandwidth limit no mode escapes and a
polarization-subspace loss confined to the reduced modes; full test
statistics are supplementary.

\textbf{Is the penalty doing this, or the receiver?} A dedicated sweep of
$\lambda_k\in\{0,0.1,1,10\}$ shows the penalty is inert: inlier RMSE moves
by at most $2$ parts in $10^5$ across the whole range, and no weight
rescues or introduces a catastrophic trial (McNemar $p=1$ in all $45$
paired comparisons). The scalar mode's high-SNR floor (near $0.098$~mm at
$30$~dB) persists identically at $\lambda_k=0$, so it belongs to the
rank-one structure of single-component sensing, not to regularization; no
efficiency claim is extended to scalar sensing. For full EVS the RMS/PEB
ratio is $0.98$--$1.02$ across two independently seeded suites, consistent
with efficiency once $95\%$ paired-bootstrap intervals ($\pm6\%$) are
accounted for; the same resampling leaves no efficiency ordering among
modes, so the claimed separation is the information gap of
Fig.~\ref{fig:c1_combined}(a), a factor of $5.7$ and $2.3$. The full
$\lambda$-sweep, penalty magnitudes and bootstrap tables are supplementary.

\subsection{C2: Compression and Acquisition}
\label{subsec:res_c2}

\textbf{Does sufficient compression improve acquisition?} At $-10$~dB,
reducing the EVS receive dimension from $I=96$ to $r=6$ improves delay p95
error by $35\times$ ($4.87\to0.141$~ns), raises basin acquisition from
$97.50\%$ to $99.38\%$, and lowers the catastrophic position rate from
$2.29\%$ to $0.62\%$; the subspace-leakage ratio $\sigma_{K+1}/\sigma_K$
falls from $0.822$ to $0.353$, widening the delay-subspace margin exactly
where acquisition is fragile. From $0$~dB upward the compressed and raw
routes are numerically coincident, so the gain is low-SNR basin
stabilization rather than a change in the exact likelihood optimum -- an
empirical fact distinct from the deterministic likelihood isometry of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compress}, which is credited with no accuracy gain of its
own. Quoted only to expose the mechanism, a full component ladder at
$-20$~dB separates compression, common-geometry fusion and anchor refresh:
compression reduces the catastrophic rate from $21.5\%$ to $5.6\%$, and
common-geometry fusion further reduces it to $4.0\%$ -- not extra restarts,
confirmed against 4-/7-start and oracle controls agreeing within one
trial -- while anchor refresh leaves the rate unchanged but lowers
reconstruction NMSE by $27\times$, so the three mechanisms act on delay
denoising, basin acquisition and reconstruction respectively, not on
Phase~II accuracy. Full rank, leakage and component-ladder tables are
supplementary.

\textbf{Does acquisition scale with aperture?} Growing the panel from
$16^2$ to $64^2$ elements lowers conditional RMSE from $1.66$ to $0.42$~mm
while holding the catastrophic rate in $[0.21,0.62]\%$; over the same range
fixed-count Phase~I basin acquisition rises from only $14.58\%$ to
$95.62\%$, whereas beam-space acquisition holds $99.6\%$ and $99.4\%$ at
the two endpoints, so aperture robustness belongs to the acquisition
stage. Accuracy also
improves monotonically with $M_A$, $T$ and bandwidth, and at $K=4$ the
proposed and range-scaled 4-D routes have catastrophic rates $1.88\%$ and
$12.92\%$; the complete scaling sweeps over $M_A$, $T$, $N$ and $K$ are
supplementary.

\subsection{C3: Clock Profiling and Certification}
\label{subsec:res_c3}

Isolating Phase~II at $-10$~dB over $100$ shared MKSC-GI initializers,
correctly range-scaled 4-D VP and CCOP-JVP have indistinguishable precision
($0.509$ versus $0.509$~mm, $3.44$ versus $3.44$~ps; full precision
supplementary) with no catastrophic trial in either row, so C3 establishes
equivalence after correct scaling and claims no accuracy advantage. What
CCOP-JVP removes is the clock coordinate, cutting the median outer
evaluation count from $13$ to $9$. The explicit 4-D trajectory instead
depends on whether the clock is carried in seconds, nanoseconds or range
units: both mis-scaled labels lose one trial in $100$ that the range-scaled
and profiled routes keep, and the seconds label degrades conditional
position RMSE by $31\%$, whereas CCOP-JVP returns numerically identical
outputs under all three clock-unit parameterizations (verified by output
hash; supplementary). The certified gap normalized by its
tolerance has median $0.69$ and maximum $0.9997$ over the operating region,
so every visited clock profile met the tolerance with no cap, branch
ambiguity or safeguard triggered; this certifies only the inner optimum,
the outer 3-D search remaining local. In the independent SNR suite, 4-D VP
and CCOP-JVP sharing one Phase~I initializer have identical catastrophic
rates at every SNR, so the pipeline changes the tail only through its
initializer, and tail suppression is attributed to C2 rather than C3. The
control table, gap distributions and paired tail numbers are supplementary.

\subsection{Deployment Boundaries and Spatial Generalization}
\label{subsec:res_robustness}

Three boundaries bound deployment; all other mismatch and generalization
sweeps are supplementary. \textbf{Calibration.} RIS--BS angular
calibration costs precision long before capture: at $0.25^\circ$ the basin
rate is still $99.17\%$ ($P_{\rm cat}=0.62\%$) yet conditional RMSE has
risen from $0.447$ to $31.2$~mm, capture degrading only later, at
$59.58\%$ for $1^\circ$ -- so calibration first destroys accuracy, then
acquisition. This is a $-10$-dB accuracy boundary, not its SNR scaling, and
does not justify extrapolating a sub-$0.1^\circ$ tolerance never tested.
Receiver-side gain, phase and sensor-position mismatch, RIS element-phase
error, and first-order colored noise are all milder over their tested
ranges, degrading reconstruction well before capture; these empirical
boundaries do not substitute for spatial-wideband or colored-noise
modeling. Among the fixed Phase~I constants, the shortlist, beam-space
range grid and confidence weights are inert (the exhaustive $K!=6$
shortlist reproduces the frozen estimate on all $480$ trials), but the
association clock weight is the exception and goes the wrong way:
$w_{\rm clk}=0$ rescues all four catastrophic trials and introduces none,
though four discordant pairs do not resolve the effect ($p=0.125$), so
this suite gives no evidence that the term improves basin capture. Full
sweeps are supplementary.

\textbf{Model order.} This is the one assumption that fails abruptly. In
the dedicated $0$-dB sweep, $\widehat K=2$ is structurally destructive
(median error $0.710$~m, $P_{\rm cat}=65.4\%$), while $\widehat K=4$ and
$5$ retain submillimeter medians but carry catastrophic rates of $35.4\%$
and $49.0\%$; known $K$ is therefore a real assumption, and with the
calibration boundary shows why median-only robustness reporting would
mislead in either direction.

\textbf{Delay coincidence.} Across a $5\times5\times2$ grid of UE
positions, the proposed median per-position catastrophic rate is $0.62\%$
against $2.92\%$ for 4-D VP. Both hard corners lie near a cascaded-delay
coincidence surface, the worst with $31.25\%$ catastrophic trials at a
minimum separation of only $0.062/B_{\rm occ}$ -- yet the free-Jones EFIM
remains full rank at every tested position, including these corners. Local
information sufficiency therefore does not imply global acquisition
reliability: a PEB map alone is insufficient for deployment screening,
which should flag delay-coincidence surfaces and provide a coarse global
fallback there. Every sweep in this subsection holds one fixed
infrastructure layout, so it bounds deployment for that layout and samples
no panel-placement diversity.

\subsection{Cost, Training Budget, and Failure Diagnostics}
\label{subsec:res_cost}

Serialized single-thread means at $-10$~dB put the proposed route at
$3.20$~s and $2.51$~GB peak RSS, faster than ALS-CPD ($3.84$~s,
$1.07$~GB), VBI/SBL ($11.5$~s, $1.05$~GB) and near-field OMP--SAGE--WLS
($13.0$~s, $1.67$~GB) but about one third slower and heavier than
range-scaled 4-D VP ($2.42$~s, $1.79$~GB). A block-by-block ablation
(reproducing the mean to $0.2\%$) attributes the majority of the time to
the exact spherical panel projection ($1.68$~s for the remaining Phase~I
blocks) rather than certified clock profiling ($0.33$~s for Phase~II), and
the $2.51$~GB peak to retaining the raw Hankel tensor for the mode-1
contraction, not the -- never formed -- dense raw Phase~II dictionary;
certification therefore does not make the pipeline uniformly cheaper. Full
stage timings and memory provenance are supplementary.

With $\Delta f=5$~MHz the useful symbol is $200$~ns and a cyclic prefix
covering the $9.1$-ns delay spread and the $10$-ns clock box is met at
$25\%$, so one configuration per symbol puts the $T=256$ sweep at
$T_{\rm train}\simeq64~\mu$s, excluding a reconfiguration latency that
dominates the payload once it exceeds about $0.25~\mu$s per switch. The
binding constraint is displacement, not phase coherence: at $60$~GHz and
$1$~m/s the coherence time is about $2.1$~ms, some $33$ sweeps, whereas
the UE moves $64~\mu$m in one sweep, already $16\%$ of the median PEB, the
two becoming equal near $6$~m/s -- so treating $(\bp,\Delta t,\bx)$ as
constant over a sweep covers pedestrian mobility at the reported accuracy
and is exhausted at vehicular speed, where $T$ would have to be cut
against the aperture-driven acquisition gain of
Section~\ref{subsec:res_c2}. The receiver premise is equally concrete: six
components on $M_A=16$ sensors is $96$ calibrated chains.

The remaining failures are observable Phase~I events, and this is the
chapter's closing attribution: across the $4\,320$ operating-region trials
of the internal suite, exactly five proposed-route trials are
catastrophic and exactly the same five fail the basin diagnostic, each
with a Phase~I delay RMSE above $0.5$~ns, and panel association is
correct in $99.93\%$ of trials overall and in every trial at and above
$0$~dB. Every remaining proposed-route failure in the tested region is
therefore an acquisition failure, not a refinement failure: Phase~II is a
precise local repair, not a guaranteed rescue for a failed delay
acquisition.
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work studied joint 3-D localization and synchronization in a
blocked-LoS distributed near-field RIS uplink observed by a shared EVS
array. The central Maxwell--Jones factorization separates the calibrated
RIS--BS polarimetric manifold from the UE-dependent wavefront and common
clock. This structure yields three consequences: EVS component selection
controls both geometric information and panel discrimination; aggregate
Maxwell--Kronecker projection preserves the relevant likelihood and
information under the stated model conditions while suppressing
noise-only receive dimensions; and the common clock forms a unitary
phase-ramp orbit that reduces refinement to a 3-D position search with a
globally certified conditional clock solution.

The numerical results show that the principal benefit is improved basin
reliability rather than a new same-basin precision advantage. Once the
proposed route and a correctly scaled 4-D Jones-VP baseline enter the same
basin, their inlier position and clock accuracies are essentially
identical. The reduction in catastrophic tails is instead traced to
Phase-I acquisition, where EVS discrimination, compressed delay recovery,
physical panel association, and common-geometry fusion improve access to
the correct basin. CCOP-JVP contributes a lower-dimensional,
clock-unit-invariant refinement with a deterministic certificate for the
inner clock solve; that certificate does not globalize the nonconvex outer
position search.

The results also identify the present model's practical boundaries.
RIS--BS angular calibration limits millimeter-level accuracy before it
significantly affects basin capture, the panel count remains a meaningful
model-order assumption, and full-rank local information does not preclude
acquisition failure near cascaded-delay coincidence surfaces.
Spatial-wideband effects, direct leakage and diffuse multipath, and
panel-placement diversity lie outside the present model and require
explicit extensions rather than retuning of the current estimator. Natural
next steps are therefore spatial-wideband and multipath modeling,
model-order selection, and coarse global screening for difficult
coincidence regions and more diverse distributed-RIS layouts.
