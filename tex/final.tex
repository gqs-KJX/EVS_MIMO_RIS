\title{Maxwell--Jones Structure for Vector-Sensor-Aided Distributed
Near-Field RIS-OFDM Localization and Synchronization: Information
Splitting, Sufficient Compression, and Certified Clock Profiling}

\author{Qingshan Gong,~\IEEEmembership{Member,~IEEE}
\thanks{Manuscript received XX XX, 2026; revised XX XX, 2026.}}

\markboth{IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications,~Vol.~XX, No.~XX, 2026}%
{Gong: MKSC-GI Initialized CCOP-JVP for Distributed Near-Field RIS-EVS-OFDM Systems}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study joint 3-D localization, clock synchronization, and Jones-domain
channel reconstruction for a wideband uplink that reaches an electromagnetic
vector-sensor (EVS) array only through distributed near-field reconfigurable
intelligent surfaces (RISs), so that a single unknown clock couples every
cascaded delay. Each panel's sensing block separates into space--frequency
steering and a calibrated Maxwell--Jones manifold carrying no unknowns, a
separation exploited three times. First, per-panel free-Jones Fisher information becomes a geometric
matrix weighted by retained polarization energy; reduced modes reweight
panels, and at most two components provide no polarimetric discrimination
between space--frequency-equivalent panels. Second, aggregate
Maxwell--Kronecker projection preserves the profiled-likelihood optimizer
and the Fisher information, one discarded-energy scalar completing
sufficiency for unknown noise power without disturbing the shared delay
structure. Third, the common-clock unitary orbit makes the Jones-profiled
score a finite trigonometric polynomial, maximized by fast Fourier transform
(FFT)-assisted branch-and-bound with a deterministic objective-gap
certificate. Across signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) from $-10$ to $30$~dB,
Rayleigh-spaced acquisition and common-geometry initialization hold the
estimator near the free-Jones position reference at submillimeter
median accuracy and picosecond synchronization, with the lowest position and
clock catastrophic-event rates among five methods and none at or above
$0$~dB. RIS-to-BS angular calibration, not
noise, sets the residual accuracy boundary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{E}{lectrically} large reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)
panels extend the radiative near-field region, where spherical-wave
curvature conveys both range and angular information~\cite{wymeersch2020risloc,elzanaty2021bounds,cui2023nearfield,
yang2024nfris}. Several distributed panels can therefore act as passive
anchors for joint 3-D localization and synchronization. An electromagnetic
vector sensor (EVS) adds a complementary observable: its co-located
electric and magnetic components
map each two-dimensional Jones state to a calibrated six-component
Maxwell response~\cite{nehorai1994vector,khan2020sixcomponent}. The joint
model is nevertheless difficult because the raw tensor is large, every
path has unknown complex Jones coefficients, and the common clock couples
all ranges in a nonconvex 4-D position--clock likelihood.

These difficulties interact. With $M_A$ EVSs, $N$ tones, and $T$ RIS
configurations, full sensing produces $6M_A\,N\,T$ complex samples before
Hankel smoothing. Unknown gains and Jones states absorb part of a geometry
perturbation, so a small residual need not identify the correct panel or
position basin. Clock bias enters every cascaded delay in the same direction
as range, so an improperly scaled joint optimizer follows a unit-dependent
trajectory even though the physical likelihood is unchanged. At low
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) the resulting failures are gross wrong-basin
solutions on a small probability mass rather than variance inflation, so a
local error bound alone cannot describe the operating regime.

Near-field RIS localization already rests on a solid base. Distributed and
multi-RIS designs turn several panels into geometric diversity through
panel-wise measurements, learned angle extraction, or blockage-aware panel
selection~\cite{ma2023distributed,fadakar2024multiris,liu2025multiris}, and
asynchronous analyses carry position bounds and estimators to links whose
delays absorb an unknown
clock~\cite{fascista2022rissync,gong2024async,yan2025ris}.
These works share a premise rather than a receiver: the evidence reaching the
estimator is already panel-resolved, through panel-dependent spatial
evidence, angle extraction preceding asynchronous refinement, or group
training with canonical polyadic decomposition (CPD) and panel selection.
That premise is natural when panels are separated by design, and a single
shared aperture removes it. Here every passive path arrives at the same array
within one observation whose direct link is blocked, so a multilinear
decomposition returns algebraic labels rather than physical ones, and one
swapped label misassigns a range, a near-field response, and a clock replica
together. Adding anchors enlarges the geometry to be fused without resolving
that labeling, which must be decided from an observable the space--frequency
signature does not carry.

Polarization is such an observable, and it enters RIS work along two lines
that have not met. In system design it is a controllable resource:
dual-polarized surfaces expose co- and cross-polar links and the beamforming
gains they support~\cite{han2022dual,munawar2024dual}. In array processing it
is an unknown recovered jointly with angle and
delay~\cite{nehorai1994vector,khan2020sixcomponent,gong2026aimdf}. Neither
view has been carried into distributed near-field RIS geometry, and the model
closest to ours makes the omission concrete. The per-panel model used here
shares its cascaded near-field structure with the extremely large-scale RIS
channel-estimation model of~\cite{yang2024nfris}, and departs from it in three
places: those delays are not tied by a common clock, that receiver has no
polarization dimension, and we evaluate its per-subcarrier spatial response
at the carrier, an approximation audited in
Section~\ref{sec:system_model}. The first two decide the estimator, since a
path carrying no polarization is described entirely by the space--frequency
signature argued above to be insufficient. An EVS supplies the missing
dimension without enlarging the aperture, because the retained Maxwell
response of a path is fixed by the calibrated RIS-to-BS direction of its own
panel; Section~\ref{sec:analysis} shows that this response separates panels
left degenerate by space and frequency, and that at most two retained
components destroy the separation. The two lines therefore join on purpose: the near-field geometry carries
range information, the EVS carries panel identity, and polarization stays an
unknown nuisance state rather than a designed resource, at the cost of an
unknown Jones state per path and a receive dimension multiplied by the
retained Maxwell components.

Our acquisition stage inherits its machinery from the near-field
channel-estimation literature and departs from it in one respect. Tensor
estimators exploit RIS training multilinearity, typically through CPD with
alternating least squares (ALS)~\cite{lin2021tensor,liu2025multiris,%
kolda2009tensor,sidiropoulos2017tensor}; sparse and Bayesian methods search a
discretized spherical manifold using polar dictionaries, orthogonal matching
pursuit (OMP), sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) and variational Bayesian
inference (VBI), or off-grid refinement by space-alternating generalized
expectation--maximization (SAGE)
\cite{cui2022channel,bayraktar2022momp,tipping2001sbl,li2024vbi,pati1993omp,%
fleury1999sage}; and near-field beam training searches the same joint
angle--range structure with codebooks designed against wideband beam
split~\cite{yang2024nfris,zheng2025wideband}. We retain the multilinear front
end and the coarse spherical search, but not their discretization, for two
reasons. The purpose differs: this stage must return physical panel labels and
one common position basin for exact-likelihood refinement, so a solution
accurate on average yet mislabeling a panel is not merely imprecise. The
scaling differs too: their atoms \emph{approximate} the spherical manifold at
a count fixed in advance, whereas the angular resolution a panel supports
sharpens with its aperture, so a fixed-count dictionary undersamples precisely
the large panels that motivate near-field operation. Two exact constructions replace that
approximation, an adjoint rewriting into the element domain and a Fresnel
dechirp at a trial range, whose candidate spacing tracks the Rayleigh limit
at a cost decoupled from dictionary size
(Section~\ref{subsec:beamspace}). Because these candidates are only
proposals, re-scored by the exact spherical criterion, enlarging the set can
only lower the achieved objective, so no grid bias is inherited.

The common clock is the last structure to exploit. Variable projection (VP)
eliminates linear nuisance parameters from nonlinear least
squares~\cite{golub1973varpro,liu2026extended}, and recent joint
localization--synchronization work seeks global solutions by semidefinite
reformulation in other timing models~\cite{peng2025sdp}. Relaxation suits a
timing model that lifts to a linear one. The complex orthogonal
frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) likelihood here does not lift, yet it
carries something stronger, since the common clock acts on it as an exact
phase-ramp orbit along which the Jones Gram matrix is invariant. Profiling
along that orbit therefore certifies the clock in exact likelihood
coordinates, with no relaxation gap to bound. Our own EVS-OFDM
estimator~\cite{gong2026aimdf} supplies the multidimensional front end but
lacks distributed RIS geometry, physical panel association, sufficient
aggregate-subspace compression, common-geometry initialization, and clock
profiling. To the best of our knowledge, no existing method combines a
distributed free-Jones EVS likelihood, Jones-aware physical panel
association, and a certified conditional clock solve in one estimator.
The paper rests on a single structural premise. The sensing block associated
with the $k$th RIS panel factorizes as
\begin{equation}
	\bA_k(\bchi)=
	\underbrace{\bc_k(\bchi)}_{\text{space--frequency geometry}}
	\otimes
	\underbrace{\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}}_{\text{retained Maxwell--Jones}},
	\label{eq:premise}
\end{equation}
where $\bchi=[\bp^{\mathsf T},\Delta t]^{\mathsf T}$ collects the unknown
position and common clock, $\bc_k$ carries all geometry and clock
dependence, and $\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}\in\C^{d_{\rm evs}\times2}$ is fixed
by the calibrated RIS-to-BS direction and receiver selection.

What makes \eqref{eq:premise} consequential is where the unknowns sit:
$\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}$ does not depend on $\bchi$. Calibrated
infrastructure supplies the whole polarimetric dimension of the sensing
operator, and the estimation problem is confined to the other factor, an
organizing principle rather than an algebraic convenience. Problems of this
size are normally made tractable by three approximations (a discretized
manifold in place of the continuum, a reduced-order projection in place of
the full observation, and a relaxed or restarted search in place of the
nonconvex likelihood), each buying complexity at the price of a bias that is
seldom quantified. The parameter-free calibrated factor makes the first two
avoidable outright, and the common clock, acting on the likelihood as a
unitary orbit, makes the third avoidable as well. C1--C3 replace the three
approximations by an exact information split, an exact sufficient
compression, and an exactly certifiable search, at the level of information,
of data, and of optimization.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1: Maxwell--Jones information splitting.} Polarization enters
the Fisher information as a scalar, not as a geometry. We derive a splitting
identity showing that the free-Jones Fisher information of each RIS panel
reduces to a mode-independent geometric kernel weighted only by its retained
polarization energy. It turns receiver choice into a statement about which
panels a mode reweights: a reduced receiver reweights panels unevenly, and at
most two retained Maxwell components leave no polarimetric separation between
panels sharing a space--frequency signature.
\item \textbf{C2: Sufficient compression and reliable acquisition.}
Dimension reduction here costs no information, and acquisition costs no grid
bias. Every admissible panel response lies in a parameter-independent
aggregate Maxwell subspace of rank $r\leq2K$, known before any estimate is
formed. We prove that the compressed observation is sufficient under the matched
model, together with one discarded-energy scalar when the noise power is
unknown, and that it preserves the profiled maximum-likelihood solution and
the shared delay pole either way. The same projection removes out-of-subspace
noise ahead of the coupled factor recovery, on which we build a
Rayleigh-spaced beam-space acquisition whose resolution follows the panel
aperture rather than a preset atom count, and a scale-profiled fusion stage
that associates the recovered factors with physical panels and returns a
common-geometry initializer.
\item \textbf{C3: Certified common-clock profiling.} A nuisance parameter
that acts as a group can be searched exactly instead of relaxed. The common
clock evolves on a unitary phase-ramp orbit whose Jones Gram matrix is
invariant, which converts the conditional clock profile into a finite
trigonometric polynomial. A branch-and-bound solve then returns an inner
solution certified globally optimal to a prescribed tolerance by a
deterministic objective-gap bound at every visited position, reducing the
outer search from four to three dimensions.
\end{itemize}

Together these contributions yield a two-phase estimator that converts
unlabeled multilinear factors into a physically associated near-field
initializer, then refines position and clock on the exact complex OFDM
likelihood under a fixed directional Jones penalty. They also change what has
to be reported. With the split, the compression, and the clock solve exact,
the dominant residual risk is a discrete acquisition failure confined to a
few trials rather than a modeling bias spread over all of them, and no local
error bound describes it. We therefore evaluate basin capture,
catastrophic-event rates, and optimality certificates alongside conventional
accuracy, and find that local information sufficiency does not imply global
acquisition reliability: the free-Jones information is full rank at every
tested position, yet the positions that fail lie near a cascaded-delay
coincidence surface. None of this reasoning is specific to Maxwell--Jones
structure. The same three replacements are available whenever calibrated
infrastructure fixes a nuisance subspace and a shared parameter acts on the
likelihood as a group orbit.

Section~\ref{sec:algorithm} develops the two-phase estimator,
Section~\ref{sec:analysis} establishes C1--C3 and the resulting complexity,
and Section~\ref{sec:simulation} reports an extensive Monte Carlo
evaluation.

\section{System and Channel Model}
\label{sec:system_model}

A single-antenna user equipment (UE) transmits pilots on $N$ subcarriers
spaced by $\Delta f$, giving an occupied bandwidth
$B_{\rm occ}=(N-1)\Delta f$. Its direct path to the base station (BS) is
weak enough to be neglected under obstructed propagation, as is standard in
RIS-aided localization~\cite{fascista2022rissync,zhao2021timing}, so the
signal arrives only after reflection at $K$ distributed RIS panels of
$M_R=M_{Rx}M_{Ry}$ elements, which sweep $T$ known phase configurations
while the BS receives on a uniform linear array (ULA) of $M_A$
electromagnetic vector sensors. Retaining $d_{\rm evs}$ of the six
Maxwell components gives the receive dimension $I=d_{\rm evs}M_A$, and the
receive tensor $\mathcal Y\in\C^{I\times N\times T}$ holds one such block
per subcarrier and configuration. Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry} shows the
reference geometry.
Each ingredient below (the one-sided near-field cascade, the training sweep,
the six-component receive manifold, the shared clock) is standard on its own
and adopted from the literature where it is used, so only what their
combination produces is derived here. That combination has one property none
of them has alone: calibrated infrastructure fixes the polarimetric response
of a path entirely, whereas position and clock enter only through the
panel-side wavefront. Sections~\ref{subsec:calibrated}
and~\ref{subsec:defchain} build these two factors of the
premise~\eqref{eq:premise}, and Section~\ref{subsec:obsmodel} assembles them
into the observation model and the estimation problem.

\subsection{Calibrated Infrastructure: the $\bchi$-Independent Factor}
\label{subsec:calibrated}

Deployment calibration fixes the BS phase center $\bp_B$, the panel centers
$\br_k$ and global-to-local rotations $\bR_k\in{\rm SO}(3)$, the element
coordinates $\brho_{k,m}\in\R^3$ in the $k$th panel frame, the BS array
manifold, the EVS Maxwell manifolds, and the training codes
$\bOmega_k\in\C^{T\times M_R}$. Passive phase shifters make the codes constant
modulus, $[\bOmega_k]_{t,m}=e^{j\omega_{k,t,m}}/\sqrt{M_R}$, as in RIS
channel-estimation practice~\cite{lin2021tensor}. On an
$M_{Rx}\times M_{Ry}$ grid of spacings $(d_x,d_y)$ the elements are centered
on the panel, $\brho_{k,m}=[(m_x-\tfrac{M_{Rx}-1}{2})d_x,\,
(m_y-\tfrac{M_{Ry}-1}{2})d_y,\,0]^{\mathsf T}$, the BS ULA lies along the
global $x$ axis at $x_i=(i-\tfrac{M_A-1}{2})d_A$,
$\{\bm u_x,\bm u_y,\bm u_z\}$ are the global axes, and
$\kappa=2\pi/\lambda_c$ is the center-frequency wavenumber. Calibration error
is not assumed away but swept in Section~\ref{sec:simulation}.
Which hop is near field is set by the panel aperture, not the carrier. With
a panel diagonal $D\simeq0.226$~m the reactive boundary
$0.62\sqrt{D^3/\lambda_c}\simeq0.94$~m and the Fraunhofer distance
$2D^2/\lambda_c\simeq20.5$~m bracket the two hops: the $28.6$--$31.2$~m
RIS--BS links lie in the far field of a panel and use center-frequency plane
waves, whereas the $3.6$--$4.2$~m UE--RIS links fall between the two
boundaries, in the radiative near field, and retain exact spherical-wave
phase differences.
Placing only the panel-side hop in the Fresnel region is the one-sided
convention adopted for XL-RIS
channels~\cite{yang2024nfris,cui2022channel}, applied here once per
panel.

The far-field hop therefore contributes a plane-wave panel response and a BS
steering vector. With
\begin{equation}
	d_{{\rm RB},k}=\|\bp_B-\br_k\|_2,
	\qquad
	\bs_{{\rm RB},k}=\frac{\bp_B-\br_k}{d_{{\rm RB},k}}
	\label{eq:rbgeom}
\end{equation}
the RIS--BS distance and propagation direction, these are
\begin{align}
	\big[\ba_{{\rm RB},k}\big]_m
	&=e^{-j\kappa\,\brho_{k,m}^{\mathsf T}\bR_k\bs_{{\rm RB},k}},\nonumber\\
	\big[\bv_{{\rm B},k}\big]_i
	&=e^{j\kappa x_i\,\bm u_x^{\mathsf T}\bs_{{\rm RB},k}}.
	\label{eq:arb_vb}
\end{align}
A transverse plane wave along $\bs_{{\rm RB},k}$ has Maxwell pair
$(\bE,\bH)=(\bq,\bs_{{\rm RB},k}\times\bq)$ in impedance-normalized units,
so fixing the transverse basis
\begin{equation}
	\bq_{k,1}=\frac{\bs_{{\rm RB},k}\times\bm u_z}
	{\|\bs_{{\rm RB},k}\times\bm u_z\|_2},
	\qquad
	\bq_{k,2}=\bs_{{\rm RB},k}\times\bq_{k,1},
	\label{eq:qbasis}
\end{equation}
with $\bm u_z$ replaced by $\bm u_y$ whenever
$|\bs_{{\rm RB},k}^{\mathsf T}\bm u_z|>0.95$ to keep \eqref{eq:qbasis} well
conditioned, the six-component vector-sensor
manifold~\cite{nehorai1994vector,khan2020sixcomponent} specializes to the
calibrated Maxwell--Jones block
\begin{equation}
	\bTheta_k^{(6)}=\begin{bmatrix}
	\bq_{k,1} & \bq_{k,2}\\[1pt]
	\bq_{k,2} & -\bq_{k,1}\end{bmatrix}\in\C^{6\times2},
	\label{eq:Theta}
\end{equation}
whose columns are orthogonal of squared norm two, so
$[\bTheta_k^{(6)}]^{\mathsf H}\bTheta_k^{(6)}=2\bI_2$, and whose electric and
magnetic responses couple through the direction-dependent cross product. The
basis choice is a gauge: another transverse frame differs by a $2\times2$
orthogonal factor that the Jones state absorbs. A fixed receiver selection
$\bS_{\rm mode}\in\R^{d_{\rm evs}\times6}$, with $d_{\rm evs}=1,2,6$ for the
scalar, dual-polarized, and full-EVS modes retaining respectively the first
electric component, the first two electric components, and all six, then
gives the retained block
$\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}\triangleq\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_k^{(6)}
\in\C^{d_{\rm evs}\times2}$. Full EVS presumes calibrated co-located
electric and magnetic components with matched noise and dynamic range; the
scalar and dual-polarized selections stand for receivers retaining fewer of
them.
Nothing built so far involves the UE: both $\bTheta_k^{(6)}$ and
$\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}$ are determined by $\bs_{{\rm RB},k}$ alone, hence
independent of $\bchi$. This is the factor the estimator never has to
search; everything it must search enters through the other one, built next.

\subsection{Position, Clock, and the Near-Field Panel Response}
\label{subsec:defchain}

The unknown nonlinear parameter collects the UE position and the single
clock, $\bchi=[\bp^{\mathsf T},\Delta t]^{\mathsf T}
=[x,y,z,\Delta t]^{\mathsf T}\in\R^4$, with $\bp$ in a known service box
$\mathcal P$ and $\Delta t$ in a known interval $\mathcal T$ shorter than the
unambiguous delay period $1/\Delta f$. Distributed panels are known to deliver mutually asynchronous
cascaded delays~\cite{zhao2021timing}; here those delays are further
constrained, each being a geometric path length plus one UE--BS offset shared
by every panel, as in asynchronous RIS
localization~\cite{fascista2022rissync,gong2024async,yan2025ris}. What
distinguishes the present case is that the blocked direct path leaves nothing
to difference $\Delta t$ against, so it stays coupled to range in every
cascaded delay; path-specific constant phases are absorbed into the Jones
coefficients.
Seen in the $k$th panel frame the UE is at $\bxi_k(\bp)=\bR_k(\bp-\br_k)$,
from which
\begin{equation}
	d_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp)=\|\bxi_k(\bp)\|_2,
	\qquad
	\bs_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp)=\frac{\bxi_k(\bp)}{d_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp)}
	\label{eq:urgeom}
\end{equation}
with $d_{{\rm UR},k}=\|\bp-\br_k\|_2$ because $\bR_k$ is a rotation. The
first two components of $\bs_{{\rm UR},k}$ are the direction cosines
$u_k=\bm u_x^{\mathsf T}\bs_{{\rm UR},k}$ and
$v_k=\bm u_y^{\mathsf T}\bs_{{\rm UR},k}$, so the local parameterization
searched in Section~\ref{sec:algorithm} is
$\boldsymbol\eta_k=(d_{{\rm UR},k},u_k,v_k)$, in bijection with $\bxi_k$ on
the half-space in front of the panel.
The near-field structure stays inside the physical RIS response,
\begin{equation}
	\bh_k(\bp) = \bOmega_k\,\bg_k(\bp),
	\qquad
	\bg_k(\bp) = \ba_{{\rm RB},k}\odot\ba^{\rm NF}_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp),
	\label{eq:hk}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
	\big[\ba^{\rm NF}_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp)\big]_m
	&= e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_c}\delta_{k,m}(\bp)},\nonumber\\
	\delta_{k,m}(\bp)
	&= \big\|\bxi_k(\bp)-\brho_{k,m}\big\|_2
	- d_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp),
	\label{eq:delta}
\end{align}
is the exact wavefront difference between element $m$ and the panel center.
No Fresnel or parabolic expansion enters the forward model, so
\eqref{eq:delta} stays exact and the known map
$\bp\mapsto\bh_k=\bOmega_k\bg_k$ carries the whole near-field structure;
Phase~I of Section~\ref{sec:algorithm} recovers it only up to scale and
permutation before mapping it back onto the same manifold.

Combining \eqref{eq:rbgeom} and \eqref{eq:urgeom}, the cascaded delay and
its OFDM phase ramp are
\begin{align}
	\tau_k(\bp,\Delta t)
	&= \underbrace{\frac{d_{{\rm UR},k}(\bp) + d_{{\rm RB},k}}{c_0}}_{
	\textstyle \tau_{g,k}(\bp)} + \Delta t,\nonumber\\
	\bd_k(\bp,\Delta t)
	&= \big[\,e^{-j2\pi n\Delta f\,\tau_k(\bp,\Delta t)}\,\big]_{n=0}^{N-1}
	\in\C^N,
	\label{eq:dk}
\end{align}
with $c_0$ the speed of light. Spatial manifolds are evaluated at the
carrier $f_c$ while this delay phase is retained on every subcarrier. The
term so neglected is the spatial-wideband phase $\epsilon_{{\rm sq},k}$, the
difference between the exact per-element wavefront phase at a subcarrier and
at $f_c$; maximized over subcarriers and panel elements, its largest value
across the three panels is $0.102$~rad in the reference configuration. The
closed-form design condition and the per-panel audit are supplementary.

One scalar sample assembles these pieces. Pilots are unit modulus and
removed by pilot normalization, so with $\bBm_k=\bv_{{\rm B},k}\otimes
\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})}\in\C^{I\times2}$ the calibrated receive block and
$y_{i,n,t}$ the sample at receive index $i$, subcarrier $n$, and
configuration $t$,
\begin{equation}
		y_{i,n,t}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\!
	\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{M_R}[\bOmega_k]_{t,m}
	[\bg_k(\bp)]_m\!\Big)
	e^{-j2\pi n\Delta f\tau_k}
	[\bBm_k]_{i,:}\bx_k+n_{i,n,t}.
	\label{eq:scalar}
\end{equation}
The inner sum is the physical reflection at panel $k$, exactly the $t$th
entry of $\bh_k(\bp)$ in \eqref{eq:hk}, carrying both the spherical UE--RIS
phase and the plane-wave RIS--BS phase through $\bg_k$; the exponential is
the OFDM ramp of the cascaded delay, and $[\bBm_k]_{i,:}\bx_k$ is the
Maxwell--Jones response at receive index $i$. All $K$ panels reflect in every
configuration, so their contributions add coherently. Because \eqref{eq:scalar} separates in $t$, $n$, and $i$,
ordering $\mathcal Y$ with the EVS index fastest and stacking gives the
$k$th sensing block
\begin{equation}
	\bA_k(\bchi)
	= \bh_k(\bp)\otimes\bd_k(\bp,\Delta t)\otimes\bBm_k
	= \bc_k(\bchi)\otimes\bTheta_k^{({\rm mode})},
	\label{eq:Ak}
\end{equation}
where associativity of the Kronecker product isolates the space--frequency
factor $\bc_k(\bchi)=\bh_k(\bp)\otimes\bd_k(\bp,\Delta t)\otimes
\bv_{{\rm B},k}\in\C^{TNM_A}$. Equation \eqref{eq:Ak} is the
premise~\eqref{eq:premise}, now established rather than asserted: the two subsections built its factors separately, and
every dependence on position and clock ended up in $\bc_k$. Since
$\bA_k\in\C^{M\times2}$ in all three modes, the data, sensing matrix,
Jacobian, and information bounds inherit $\bS_{\rm mode}$ while $\bc_k$ does
not.

\subsection{Observation Model and Estimation Problem}
\label{subsec:obsmodel}

Stacking the panels as
$\bA(\bchi)=[\bA_1(\bchi),\ldots,\bA_K(\bchi)]\in\C^{M\times 2K}$ and
vectorizing $\mathcal Y$ in the same order gives
\begin{equation}
	\by = \bmu(\bchi,\bx) + \bn = \bA(\bchi)\,\bx + \bn,
	\qquad
	\bn\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf 0,\sigma^2\bI_M),
	\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
where $M=I\,N\,T$ and the noise is spatially and temporally white with a
power $\sigma^2$ that is \emph{not} provided to the estimator. The path gain
and polarization state are combined as $\bx_k=\beta_k\be_k\in\C^2$ with
$\be_k=[\sin\gamma_k e^{j\psi_k},\ \cos\gamma_k]^{\mathsf T}$ and
$\bx=[\bx_1^{\mathsf T},\ldots,\bx_K^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}\in\C^{2K}$,
where $\beta_k\in\C$ is the complex path gain and $\be_k$ the unit Jones
state, $\|\be_k\|_2=1$, parameterized by $\gamma_k\in[0,\pi/2]$ and
$\psi_k\in(-\pi,\pi]$ because the global phase is absorbed into $\beta_k$;
only the product $\beta_k\be_k$ is identifiable. The gain $\beta_k$ absorbs
path loss, RIS efficiency, and the center-frequency phase of the cascade,
and both $\beta_k$ and $\be_k$ are taken flat over the occupied band. Four assumptions complete the specification. Panel phase shifts are scalar
and identical for the two polarizations, so a panel redirects a wave without
mixing its Jones components. The cascaded delay lies within the cyclic
prefix, so carrier-frequency offset, intercarrier interference, and phase
noise are absent from the nominal model. Each panel is illuminated once per
configuration and the nominal model carries no further component, so the
model order is the panel count $K$ and is taken known. The $T$ configurations
are swept within one coherence block, so $\bchi$, the Jones states, and the
gains are constant over them. The last two are tested rather than assumed
away in Section~\ref{sec:simulation}, which sweeps an incorrect order,
colored noise, and additional propagation components, and reports model order
as the one assumption that fails abruptly. Spatial-wideband and general
multipath extensions remain outside the model.

Because $\sigma^2$ is unknown, profiling it at
$\hat\sigma^2=\|\by-\bA\bx\|_2^2/M$ concentrates the log-likelihood to
$-M\ln(\pi e\hat\sigma^2)$, strictly decreasing in the residual energy, so
maximum-likelihood estimation of $(\bchi,\bx)$ is equivalent to the nominal
least-squares problem
\begin{equation}
	\min_{\bp\in\mathcal P,\ \Delta t\in\mathcal T,\ \bx\in\C^{2K}}
	\big\|\by-\bA(\bp,\Delta t)\,\bx\big\|_2^2.
	\label{eq:ml_problem}
\end{equation}
The $2K$ Jones coefficients enter linearly and admit closed-form
elimination, whereas position and clock enter nonlinearly through the
spherical UE--RIS phase \eqref{eq:delta} and the common phase ramp
\eqref{eq:dk}. Nonconvex in four real coordinates, \eqref{eq:ml_problem} is the
data-fidelity term of both phases: Phase~I supplies a physically associated initializer for it, and
Phase~II minimizes it augmented by the directional Jones
penalty~\eqref{eq:penalized_short}, whose consequences for the reported
bounds are stated in Remark~\ref{rem:peb_reading}.
One structural consequence closes the model. The $K$ cascaded delays supply
only $K$ scalar constraints on the four unknowns in $\bchi$, so for $K=3$
they leave one direction undetermined, and range-only reasoning cannot
recover it. The near-field aperture supplies it: the phase slope and curvature of
\eqref{eq:delta} vary with position across a panel, the mechanism by which
curvature restores joint localization--synchronization
observability~\cite{wymeersch2020nearfield}.
Section~\ref{sec:analysis} states the corresponding local rank condition.

\section{Proposed Estimator: MKSC-GI Initialized CCOP-JVP}
\label{sec:algorithm}

Section~\ref{sec:system_model} leaves the estimator with
\eqref{eq:ml_problem}, nonconvex in the four coordinates of $\bchi$ and
carrying $2K$ linear Jones nuisances. Attacking it directly fails for the
reason given in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: position and clock sit inside
every one of $INT$ samples, and a small residual does not by itself identify
the right basin. What follows therefore takes the observation apart in the
order in which structure removes obstacles. The
pipeline compresses the receive dimension with no loss of information,
extracts the delays without knowing position or clock, recovers the
remaining factors linearly once the delays are fixed, assigns physical panel
labels to those algebraic factors, and fuses the labels into one common
position. Only then does it optimize \eqref{eq:ml_problem}, augmented by a fixed
directional Jones penalty, solving its worst coordinate to a certificate. The first five steps constitute Phase~I,
\emph{Maxwell--Kronecker sufficient compression with common-geometry
initialization} (MKSC-GI), and the last Phase~II, \emph{common-clock
orbit-profiled Jones variable projection} (CCOP-JVP); the supplementary
figures label them Stage~I and Stage~II.

\subsection{Maxwell--Kronecker Factor Recovery}

Position and clock are entangled in every cascaded delay, so neither can be
estimated before the delays themselves. Equation \eqref{eq:dk} shows the way
out: the delay of path $k$ reaches the observation only through the single
pole $z_k=e^{-j2\pi\Delta f\tau_k}$, shared by the two frequency factors
$\bd_{P,k}=[z_k^p]_{p=0}^{P-1}$ and $\bd_{L,k}=[z_k^\ell]_{\ell=0}^{L-1}$, so
under the pole-separation, smoothing dimension, and snapshot-rank conditions
collected in Section~\ref{sec:analysis} the $K$ delays are identifiable from
the frequency axis alone, with no knowledge of $\bchi$. Smoothing that axis
into a Hankel pair with $P+L-1=N$ turns the shared pole into a shift
invariance that subspace methods read off
directly~\cite{roy1989esprit,gong2026aimdf}, and forms
$\mathcal Z\in\C^{I\times P\times L\times T}$. Both factors are always
rebuilt from the same estimated $z_k$ and are never projected independently,
which keeps the delay one degree of freedom per path rather than two.

Before the poles are estimated, the receive dimension can be reduced for
free. Each EVS-array path lies in a known two-dimensional Maxwell--Jones
subspace fixed by \eqref{eq:premise}, so whatever the UE does, every
admissible response lies in the span of those $K$ known planes, a subspace
of dimension at most $2K$, and the receive directions orthogonal to that
span carry noise but no signal. Because $\bBm_k$ already carries the receiver selection of
Section~\ref{sec:system_model}, set $\bPsi_k=\bBm_k\in\C^{I\times2}$ and let
$\bQ_S\in\C^{I\times r}$ contain the left singular vectors of
$\bPsi=[\bPsi_1,\ldots,\bPsi_K]$ whose singular values exceed $10^{-12}$ of
the largest. Its numerical rank obeys $r\le2K$; in the reference setting
$I=96$ and $r=6$. The basis uses no UE position, gain, or true Jones
direction, and only the EVS mode is compressed,
$\mathcal Z_{\rm d}=\mathcal Z\times_1\bQ_S^{\mathsf H}
\in\C^{r\times P\times L\times T}$. This one projection plays three roles
under three distinct conditions, separated in Remark~\ref{rem:c2_scope}; the
role used here, ahead of factor recovery, is the removal of the noise-only
EVS directions just described.

Forward--backward total-least-squares estimation of signal parameters via
rotational invariance techniques
(ESPRIT)~\cite{roy1989esprit,gong2026aimdf} then estimates the $K$ poles
from $\mathcal Z_{\rm d}$, and each estimate is projected onto the unit
circle. Fixing the poles is what makes the rest cheap: with $\bD_\tau$ known,
\eqref{eq:scalar} is linear in every outstanding factor, so the coupled
recovery is one least-squares solve instead of the alternating iteration that
unstructured multilinear decompositions
require~\cite{kolda2009tensor,sidiropoulos2017tensor}, the same algebraic
economy that makes tensor-based IRS channel estimation
attractive~\cite{lin2021tensor}. The difference here is that one pole
generates both frequency factors, so the factors are coupled rather than
independently identifiable. Writing $\bZ_{rT,PL}\in\C^{rT\times PL}$ for the unfolding of
$\mathcal Z_{\rm d}$ that groups the EVS and training modes against the two
frequency modes, and $\bD_\tau=[\bd_{P,k}\otimes\bd_{L,k}]_{k=1}^K$, the
coupled factors follow from
\begin{equation}
 \hat\bF=\bZ_{rT,PL}\bD_\tau^{\ast}
 (\bD_\tau^{\mathsf T}\bD_\tau^{\ast}+\lambda_\tau\bI)^{-1},
 \label{eq:coupledls_short}
\end{equation}
whose loading
$\lambda_\tau=\lambda_{\rm rel}\tr(\bD_\tau^{\mathsf T}\bD_\tau^{\ast})/K
+\lambda_{\rm fl}$ tracks the Gram scale through a dimensionless relative
term and a positive floor, both fixed in the supplement, and neither enters
the common-pole reconstruction. Column $j$ of $\hat\bF$ is reshaped to
$\C^{r\times T}$ and split by its leading singular pair into an EVS
space--polarization factor and an RIS training factor, the former lifted by
$\bQ_S$ to $\tilde\ba_j\in\C^{I}$ and the latter kept as
$\tilde\bt_j\in\C^{T}$, the ratio $\varrho_j=\sigma_{2,j}/\sigma_{1,j}$ of
that split being retained as a per-column confidence.

What the split returns is still algebraic: nothing ties column $j$ to panel
$k$, yet every downstream step needs that tie, because a swapped label
misassigns a range, a near-field response, and a clock replica at once. Association is therefore settled before any geometry is fused, by
searching permutations under a composite cost. For column $j$ and panel
$k$,
\begin{equation}
 s_{j,k}=
 \frac{\|\bPi^{\perp}_{\bPsi_k}\tilde\ba_j\|}{\|\tilde\ba_j\|}
 +\min_{\boldsymbol\eta\in\mathcal E_k}
 \frac{\|\bPi^{\perp}_{\bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)}\tilde\bt_j\|}
 {\|\tilde\bt_j\|},
 \label{eq:assoc_score}
\end{equation}
with $\bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)$ the exact near-field panel response
\eqref{eq:hk} and $\mathcal E_k$ the local search box. Both terms are
relative residual norms in $[0,1]$, so they combine without a calibration
constant. Writing $\hat d_{j,k}$ for the range coordinate of the minimizer
in \eqref{eq:assoc_score} and $\hat\tau_j$ for the delay of the estimated
pole, each pair also implies a clock replica
$\widehat{\Delta t}_{j,k}=\hat\tau_j-(\hat d_{j,k}+d_{{\rm RB},k})/c_0$,
and a permutation is scored by
\begin{equation}
 C(\pi)=\sum_{j=1}^{K}s_{j,\pi(j)}
 +\frac{w_{\rm clk}}{t_0}
 \big[\,\mathrm{sd}(\pi)+e_{\mathcal T}(\pi)\big],
 \label{eq:assoc_perm}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sd}(\pi)$ is the spread of the $K$ replicas
$\widehat{\Delta t}_{j,\pi(j)}$ about their mean, $e_{\mathcal T}(\pi)$ is
their mean excursion outside $\mathcal T$, $w_{\rm clk}=1/2$, and
$t_0=1$~ns.
The three terms are complementary: Maxwell mismatch alone cannot separate a
wrong panel under a reduced receiver, RIS residual alone can favor a
near-field sidelobe, and delay alone is ambiguous near the coincidence
surface. The clock term also makes the decision joint, since it is a function
of the whole permutation, so $C(\pi)$ is not separable into pairwise costs,
\eqref{eq:assoc_perm} does not reduce to $K$ independent nearest-panel
decisions, and no linear-assignment solver accepts it without approximation.
That structural role is separate from an empirical one:
Section~\ref{sec:simulation} sweeps $w_{\rm clk}$ and finds no evidence that
the clock term improves basin capture at $-10$~dB.
The search over $C$ is two-tier: all $K!$ permutations are ranked on a coarse
correlation and the two lowest are re-scored with the exact spherical
projection, their relative gap being retained as an assignment margin, so the
fine search of Section~\ref{subsec:beamspace} runs once per panel after the
labels are fixed rather than once per $(j,k)$ pair. The shortlist is a
heuristic and, unlike the clock solve of Section~\ref{subsec:ccop}, carries no
optimality certificate; its first tier is also the one part of the estimator
whose cost grows factorially in $K$, negligible at $K=3$ but a real limit on
panel count, the obstruction being exactly the non-separability that hardens
the decision.

\subsection{Beam-Space Acquisition and Common-Geometry Initialization}
\label{subsec:beamspace}

Labelled factors are still only training-domain vectors; the geometry they
encode has to be read back out through the physical model of
Section~\ref{subsec:defchain}, one panel at a time, before the $K$ readings
can be reconciled into a single position. Write $\tilde\bt_k$ for the
training-domain factor that $\hat\pi$ assigns to panel $k$. Its local
range--direction estimate minimizes
\begin{equation}
 J_{\rm RIS}(\boldsymbol\eta)
 =\|\bPi_{\bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)}^\perp\tilde\bt_k\|^2,
 \quad \bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)=\bOmega_k
 [\ba_{{\rm RB},k}\odot\ba^{\rm NF}(\boldsymbol\eta)],
 \label{eq:Jris_short}
\end{equation}
over $\boldsymbol\eta=(d_{{\rm UR},k},u_k,v_k)$ as parameterized in
Section~\ref{subsec:defchain}. A fixed-count angular dictionary of the polar
or beam-training kind~\cite{cui2022channel,zheng2025wideband} undersamples
large panels because its step is set by the search box while the panel
resolution $\lambda_c/D$ improves with aperture. Beam-space acquisition removes the training dimension and
the dictionary at once. Because the training code enters $\bh_k$ only on
the left, the numerator correlation of \eqref{eq:Jris_short} satisfies the
identity
\begin{equation}
 \bh_k(\boldsymbol\eta)^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bt_k
 =\big[\ba^{\rm NF}(\boldsymbol\eta)\big]^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bg_k,
 \quad
 \tilde\bg_k=\ba_{{\rm RB},k}^{\ast}\odot
 (\bOmega_k^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bt_k),
 \label{eq:adjoint_short}
\end{equation}
so one adjoint pass moves the factor into the element domain for every
subsequent trial geometry. This is an exact rewriting of the correlation,
not an inversion of $\bOmega_k$. The Fresnel expansion of \eqref{eq:delta}
splits the remaining phase into a term linear in the direction cosines and
a quadratic term fixed by the trial range $r$, so the dechirp
$\tilde\bg_{k,r}[m]=\tilde\bg_k[m]e^{j\kappa\|\brho_{k,m}\|^2/(2r)}$
leaves, to Fresnel order, a plane-wave sum over the element lattice, and
one zero-padded two-dimensional discrete Fourier transform (2-D DFT)
evaluates it at every direction cosine at once,
\begin{equation}
 S_r(u,v)=\left|\sum_{m_x,m_y}\tilde\bg_{k,r}[m_x,m_y]
 e^{-j\kappa(um_xd_x+vm_yd_y)}\right|,
 \label{eq:beamscore_short}
\end{equation}
with $\kappa$ and $(d_x,d_y)$ as in Section~\ref{subsec:calibrated}. The
direction-cosine step tracks the Rayleigh limit and also removes the azimuth
singularity at boresight: in the reference $64\times64$ half-wavelength
panel, fourfold zero padding gives $\Delta u=1/128$, four samples per $1/32$
Rayleigh mainlobe. If $N_x$ and $N_y$ denote the zero-padded transform
dimensions along the two panel axes, the $O(N_xN_y\log(N_xN_y))$ cost is
decoupled from a spherical dictionary size. Range is resolved more weakly
than direction, so each visible angular peak receives an exact spherical
range scan before re-scoring by \eqref{eq:Jris_short} and local refinement;
the fast Fourier transform (FFT) only proposes candidates and never replaces
the spherical model.

Candidates are the union of the beam-space proposals and the fixed-count
angular dictionary, every member judged by the same exact criterion
\eqref{eq:Jris_short}, so enlarging the set can only lower the achieved
objective and a proposal that never attains the minimum can be dropped without
changing any output. That is the situation for the reference $64\times64$
panel, whose beam set already resolves each mainlobe; the fixed-count set is
retained only where the aperture is swept down, because the beam-space
advantage reverses for a sufficiently small panel. Acquisition coverage and
cost change with the candidate set; the forward model does not.
Each panel-local fix $\hat{\boldsymbol\eta}_k$ maps through the calibrated
panel pose to a global position $\hat\bp_k^{\rm loc}$. These $K$ positions
disagree, and the common-geometry stage replaces them by the single
position that best explains all $K$ training factors at once. Unknown
complex factor scales are eliminated in the objective
\begin{align}
 J_{\rm geom}(\bp)
 &=\sum_{k=1}^K w_k\left[\|\tilde\bt_k\|^2-
 \frac{|\bh_k^{\mathsf H}(\bp)\tilde\bt_k|^2}{\|\bh_k(\bp)\|^2}\right]
 \label{eq:Jgeom_short}\\
 	 &=\min_{\balpha\in\C^K}\sum_{k=1}^K w_k
 \|\tilde\bt_k-\alpha_k\bh_k(\bp)\|^2,\nonumber
\end{align}
whose profiled scale
$\hat\alpha_k=\bh_k^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bt_k/\|\bh_k\|^2$ makes the two
lines identical, so $J_{\rm geom}$ sees only the component of $\tilde\bt_k$
orthogonal to $\coll(\bh_k)$ and is blind to the unknown path gains. The
weights are $w_k=\max\{e^{-\varrho_k/\varrho_0},w_{\min}\}$ with
$\varrho_0=1/2$ and $w_{\min}=0.05$, so a column whose rank-one split is poor
is down-weighted smoothly while no panel can be driven out of the fit. Four
deterministic bounded L-BFGS-B~\cite{byrd1995lbfgsb} starts minimize
\eqref{eq:Jgeom_short}, built in fixed order from the clipped coordinate-wise
median $\hat\bp_0=\clip_{\mathcal P}\{\median_k\hat\bp_k^{\rm loc}\}$ and
listed in full in the supplement. No random restart and no oracle state is
used.

At the selected $\hat\bp_{\rm GI}$, one unregularized fixed-position clock
profile refreshes the Jones directions,
\begin{equation}
 (\hat{\Delta t}_{\rm ref},\hat\bx_{\rm ref})=
 \arg\min_{\Delta t\in\mathcal T,\bx}
 \|\by-\bA(\hat\bp_{\rm GI},\Delta t)\bx\|^2.
 \label{eq:refresh_short}
\end{equation}
Writing $\hat\bx_{{\rm ref},k}$ for the $k$th two-entry block of
$\hat\bx_{\rm ref}$, the anchor is
$\hat\be_k=\hat\bx_{{\rm ref},k}/\|\hat\bx_{{\rm ref},k}\|_2$ for every
computed nonzero block, however small, with the degenerate zero-norm case
handled in the supplement by an inert projector and no amplitude threshold.
The refresh \eqref{eq:refresh_short} re-estimates, rather than freezes, the
polarimetric direction at a common position and a globally profiled clock; its
anchors are data adaptive and are not independent side information.

\subsection{Common-Clock Orbit-Profiled Jones Variable Projection}
\label{subsec:ccop}

Phase~II returns to the exact criterion \eqref{eq:ml_problem} from the basin
just built. Its $2K$ Jones coefficients enter linearly and are eliminated in
closed form by variable projection~\cite{golub1973varpro,liu2026extended},
which leaves four nonlinear coordinates. Of these the clock is the worst:
it is common to all panels, so it cannot be localized panel by panel, and it
enters every cascaded delay in the same direction as range. It is also the
only coordinate along which the likelihood has an exact symmetry, and that
symmetry is what removes it. Let $\bA_0(\bp)\triangleq\bA(\bp,0)$ and
$\bD_t(\Delta t)=\diag([e^{-j2\pi n\Delta f\Delta t}]_{n=0}^{N-1})$.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \bU_t(\Delta t)=\bI_T\otimes\bD_t(\Delta t)\otimes\bI_I,
 \quad \bA(\bp,\Delta t)=\bU_t(\Delta t)\bA_0(\bp),
 \label{eq:orbit_short}
\end{equation}
and unitarity makes the Jones Gram clock invariant,
$\bA^{\mathsf H}\bA=\bA_0^{\mathsf H}\bA_0\triangleq\bG_0(\bp)$.
Phase~II uses a Gram-scaled block-diagonal penalty
\begin{equation}
 \bR_{\rm dir}(\bp)=\blkdiag_k\left[
 \lambda_k\tfrac12\tr\bG_{0,kk}(\bp)\,\bPi_{\hat\be_k}^{\perp}\right],
 \label{eq:Rdir_short}
\end{equation}
which penalizes departure of $\bx_k$ from the refreshed anchor direction
$\hat\be_k$ without freezing either coefficient. The weight is dimensionless
and set to $\lambda_k=1$ throughout, so the penalty enters at exactly the
scale of the per-path data Gram. Being a fixed multiple of
that Gram rather than a function of the noise level, it neither anneals nor
vanishes with SNR; the data-only free-Jones position error bound (PEB) of
Section~\ref{sec:analysis} is therefore a reference, not a claimed
finite-sample mean-square error bound for the penalized estimator.

Phase~II accordingly minimizes
\begin{equation}
 J(\bp,\Delta t,\bx)=\|\by-\bA(\bp,\Delta t)\bx\|_2^2
 +\bx^{\mathsf H}\bR_{\rm dir}(\bp)\bx,
 \label{eq:penalized_short}
\end{equation}
whose first term is the nominal criterion \eqref{eq:ml_problem}. With
$\bH=\bG_0+\bR_{\rm dir}$ and
$\bb=\bA_0^{\mathsf H}\bU_t^{\mathsf H}\by$, the inner minimizer is
$\hat\bx=\bH^{-1}\bb$ and analytic Jones elimination gives
$F(\bp,\Delta t)\triangleq\min_{\bx\in\C^{2K}}J=\|\by\|^2-Q(\bp,\Delta t)$
with $Q=\bb^{\mathsf H}\bH^{-1}\bb$, hence the 3-D outer objective
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde F(\bp)=\|\by\|^2-
 \max_{\Delta t\in\mathcal T}Q(\bp,\Delta t).
 \label{eq:nested_short}
\end{equation}
Grouping the sufficient-statistic correlations by tone as
$\bb(\bp,\Delta t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\bu_n(\bp)
e^{j2\pi n\Delta f\Delta t}$, the quadratic form through the
clock-independent $\bH^{-1}$ retains only tone differences $\ell=n-n'$.
Hence, for fixed $\bp$, $Q$ is a real trigonometric polynomial of degree at
most $N-1$,
\begin{equation}
 Q(\bp,\Delta t)=\sum_{\ell=-(N-1)}^{N-1}c_\ell(\bp)
 e^{j2\pi\ell\Delta f\Delta t},\quad c_{-\ell}=c_\ell^\ast,
 \label{eq:trig_short}
\end{equation}
so one factorization of $\bH$ and one zero-padded FFT supply all initial
clock candidates. With
$L_2=(2\pi\Delta f)^2\sum_\ell\ell^2|c_\ell|$, an interval centered at
$t_c$ with half-width $r_t$ obeys
\begin{equation}
 Q(t)\le Q(t_c)+|Q'(t_c)|r_t+\tfrac12L_2r_t^2.
 \label{eq:bound_short}
\end{equation}
Newton refinement and branch-and-bound therefore return an incumbent with
$Q(\Delta t^\star)-Q(\hat{\Delta t})\le U-Q(\hat{\Delta t})
\triangleq g_{\rm cert}$, where $U$ is the largest surviving interval upper
bound. Subdivision stops once $g_{\rm cert}\le\epsilon_{\rm clk}$, a tolerance
combining an absolute part $10^{-12}$ and a relative part $10^{-10}$ of the
data scale under the subdivision cap $N_{\rm cap}=2\times10^{4}$, and the
bound remains valid if that cap is reached. Theorem~\ref{thm:clock} states
the resulting guarantee, which covers the continuous interval and not merely
the FFT grid.

The outer bounded search starts from $\hat\bp_{\rm GI}$ and uses the envelope
gradient $\partial Q/\partial p_i=2\Real\{\bb_i^{\mathsf H}\bz\}
-\bz^{\mathsf H}\bH_i\bz$ for $\bz=\bH^{-1}\bb$,
$\bb_i=\partial\bb/\partial p_i$, and $\bH_i=\partial\bH/\partial p_i$,
which does not differentiate the optimizing clock when its peak is unique and
interior. A nearly tied pair of clock peaks is therefore handled as a branch
event triggering a Powell safeguard~\cite{powell1964direct}, instead of by
trusting that derivative. The certificate is only for the inner clock
maximum, not the nonconvex outer position optimum. The noise variance is not
an input to the optimization; it is used only to generate noise, evaluate
bounds, and report diagnostics. Algorithm~\ref{alg:mksc_ccop_short} collects
the two phases.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{MKSC-GI Initialized CCOP-JVP}
\label{alg:mksc_ccop_short}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\mathcal Y$, calibrated infrastructure, $\mathcal P$, $\mathcal T$, $K$
\REQUIRE constants $\lambda_{\rm rel},\lambda_{\rm fl},w_{\rm clk},
\varrho_0,w_{\min},\{\lambda_k\},\epsilon_{\rm clk}$ as specified in the text
\STATE Hankelize; build $\bQ_S$; compress only the EVS mode.
\STATE Estimate $K$ poles; rebuild both frequency factors from each shared pole.
\STATE Recover coupled factors by \eqref{eq:coupledls_short}; split and lift each column.
\STATE Assign columns to panels by \eqref{eq:assoc_score}--\eqref{eq:assoc_perm}.
\STATE Generate Rayleigh-spaced beams by \eqref{eq:beamscore_short}; re-score using the exact spherical-wave phase model and refine.
\STATE Minimize scale-profiled $J_{\rm geom}$ from the four median-based starts.
\STATE Refresh Jones anchors by a fixed-position free-Jones clock profile.
\STATE Minimize $\widetilde F(\bp)$, certifying the clock at every position.
\RETURN $\hat\bp,\hat{\Delta t},\hat\bx$ and certificate diagnostics.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

Let $\bD_\chi=[\partial(\bA\bx)/\partial\chi_i]_{i=1}^4$. Eliminating the
unknown complex Jones coefficients from the white-Gaussian Fisher
information matrix gives the data-only free-Jones equivalent Fisher
information matrix (EFIM)
\begin{equation}
 \bJ_\chi^{\rm free}=\frac{2}{\sigma^2}
 \Real\{\bD_\chi^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}.
 \label{eq:efim_short}
\end{equation}
After partitioning position and clock blocks,
$\bJ_p^{\rm eff}=\bJ_{pp}-\bJ_{pt}J_{tt}^{-1}\bJ_{tp}$, the position and clock
error bounds are
${\rm PEB}=\sqrt{\tr[(\bJ_p^{\rm eff})^{-1}]}$ and
${\rm CEB}=\sqrt{[(\bJ_\chi^{\rm free})^{-1}]_{4,4}}$. The pseudoinverse
projector is part of the statement rather than a numerical convenience: it
keeps the nested-mode PEB ordering valid when a scalar or dual mask makes the
Jones block singular, because each geometric information quadratic is
variationally the minimum residual energy after a Jones perturbation and
adding observed components appends nonnegative terms before that
minimization.
With $K=3$ the cascaded delays alone leave one position direction
undetermined, and the near-field aperture supplies it. After complex-scale
elimination the projected tangents $\bPi_{\bh_k}^{\perp}
[\partial\bh_k/\partial x,\partial\bh_k/\partial y,
\partial\bh_k/\partial z]$ retain the phase slope and curvature of
\eqref{eq:delta}, which is what supplies those directions. Local
position--clock identifiability after eliminating the Jones nuisance
requires
\begin{equation}
 \rank_{\R}[\Real\{\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}^{\mathsf T},
 \Imag\{\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}=4,
 \label{eq:rankcond}
\end{equation}
which is exactly $\bJ_\chi^{\rm free}\succ\mathbf0$, since
$\Real\{\bD_\chi^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}$ is the Gram matrix
of that real stacking. The test is conditional and excludes neither global
panel-permutation nor finite-bandwidth ambiguities; its proof and the exact
spatial-narrowband design condition are supplementary.

\begin{remark}[Status of the reference bound]
\label{rem:bound_status}
Equation~\eqref{eq:efim_short} treats gains and Jones directions as unknown
deterministic nuisance parameters and does not use the Phase~I anchors, so it
is the free-Jones data-model reference. It is conservative relative to a
genie with known Jones directions, which restricts the nuisance column space
and can only increase information, though that gain is numerically negligible
in the tested geometry (Section~\ref{subsec:res_setup}). The clock Schur
complement removes the clock as a reported coordinate while retaining the
information carried by the shared delay, and is not equivalent to declaring
the clock known.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[C1: Maxwell--Jones information splitting]
\label{thm:split}
For one panel, write $\bA=\bc\otimes\bTheta$,
$\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\bTheta\bx$,
$\dot\bc_i=\partial\bc/\partial\chi_i$, and
$\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_i=(\partial\bTheta/\partial\chi_i)\bx$. Then
\begin{equation}
 [\bD_\chi^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi]_{ij}
 =\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^2\dot\bc_i^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bc}^\perp\dot\bc_j
 +\|\bc\|^2\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_i^{\mathsf H}
 \bPi_{\bTheta}^\perp\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_j.
 \label{eq:split_short}
\end{equation}
Since calibrated infrastructure gives
$\partial\bTheta/\partial\bchi=\mathbf0$, the second term vanishes. Here and
in \eqref{eq:panelres_short}, $\bTheta=\bTheta_k^{(6)}$ is the \emph{full}
six-component block of \eqref{eq:Theta}, and $\bS_{\rm mode}$ ranges over
the candidate component selections of Section~\ref{sec:system_model}. The
mode retaining $\bS_{\rm mode}$ yields
$\bJ_\chi^{\bS_{\rm mode}}=\alpha_{\bS_{\rm mode}}\bJ_\chi^{\rm full}$ with
$\alpha_{\bS_{\rm mode}}
=\|\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta\bx\|^2/\|\bTheta\bx\|^2$ whenever the retained
signal is nonzero. This exact scaling is a single-panel statement; the
multi-panel case is treated after the theorem.

For this comparison, define
$\bA_k^{(\bS)}(\bchi)=\bc_k(\bchi)\otimes
\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_k^{(6)}$. If a competitor block matches panel $k$'s
space--frequency signature, i.e.
$\bc_{k'}(\bchi')\parallel\bc_k(\bchi)$, its free-Jones residual is
\begin{equation}
 \|\bPi_{\bA_{k'}^{(\bS)}(\bchi')}^\perp
 \bA_k^{(\bS)}(\bchi)\bx_k\|
 =\|\bc_k\|\,\|\bPi_{\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_{k'}^{(6)}}^\perp
 \bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_k^{(6)}\bx_k\|.
 \label{eq:panelres_short}
\end{equation}
For a full-row-rank mask retaining $d\le2$ components this residual is
zero. With all six components it is at least
$\|\bc_k\|\|\bTheta_k^{(6)}\bx_k\|\sin\theta_{\min}$, where
$\theta_{\min}$ is the minimum principal angle between the two panel
Maxwell subspaces.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof sketch:} the projector onto $\coll(\bc\otimes\bTheta)^\perp$
splits as $\bP_{\bc}\otimes\bPi_{\bTheta}^\perp+\bPi_{\bc}^\perp\otimes\bI$,
and substitution then gives both claims; the full proof is supplementary.
Equation~\eqref{eq:panelres_short} bounds one pairwise hypothesis distance and
therefore identifies a discrimination mechanism; it neither forces the full
multi-panel hypothesis distance to vanish nor yields a closed-form threshold
SNR.

For one panel, C1 fixes exactly what component selection can change: the
information eigenvectors are unaffected and every error bound scales by
$\alpha_{\bS_{\rm mode}}^{-1/2}$. Several panels do not separate for free,
because \eqref{eq:efim_short} uses $\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}$ with the full
$\bA=[\bA_1,\ldots,\bA_K]$, which in general does not factor into per-panel
projectors; a per-panel sum is exact only when the panel blocks and their
projected geometric tangents are mutually orthogonal. When the off-diagonal
block and projected-tangent Gram terms are small, as in a resolved-path
regime, it remains an approximately block-orthogonal representation of the
EFIM,
\begin{equation}
 \bJ_\chi^{\rm free}\simeq\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\sum_k
 \|\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_k^{(6)}\bx_k\|^2\bG_k,
 \label{eq:panelsum_short}
\end{equation}
with $[\bG_k]_{ij}=\Real\{\dot\bc_{k,i}^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bc_k}^\perp
\dot\bc_{k,j}\}$ the selection-independent per-panel geometric matrix. Each
panel retains a different fraction of its polarization energy, so a selection
reweights the panels unevenly and reshapes the aggregate geometry instead of
rescaling it, the multi-panel counterpart of the exact single-panel scaling.
The separation statement \eqref{eq:panelres_short} is complementary: it
concerns a wrong-panel hypothesis and predicts gross association tails that a
local Cram\'er--Rao bound cannot represent, without asserting global
identifiability for the complete dictionary.

\begin{theorem}[C2: Maxwell--Kronecker compression]
\label{thm:compress}
Let $\bU_S=\bI_{NT}\otimes\bQ_S$ be the semi-unitary lifting matrix in the
vectorization of \eqref{eq:obs}, and suppose
$\coll[\bA(\bchi)]\subseteq\coll(\bU_S)$ for all admissible $\bchi$. Write
$\by_c=\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\by$ and
$c_\perp=\|(\bI-\bU_S\bU_S^{\mathsf H})\by\|^2$. Under the matched
white-Gaussian model:
(i) for known $\sigma^2$, $\by_c$ is sufficient for $(\bchi,\bx)$;
(ii) for unknown $\sigma^2$, the pair $(\by_c,c_\perp)$ is sufficient for
$(\bchi,\bx,\sigma^2)$, and $\by_c$ alone still preserves the maximizer in
$(\bchi,\bx)$ of the $\sigma^2$-profiled likelihood, because
\begin{equation}
 \|\by-\bA(\bchi)\bx\|^2
 =\|\by_c-\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\bA(\bchi)\bx\|^2+c_\perp
 \label{eq:isometry_short}
\end{equation}
and $c_\perp$ does not depend on $(\bchi,\bx)$;
(iii) the free-Jones EFIM of $\by_c$ equals \eqref{eq:efim_short}.
Thus $(\by_c,c_\perp)$ reconstructs the full likelihood when $\sigma^2$ is
unknown, while $\by_c$ alone preserves the profiled optimizer, the parameter
derivatives, and the free-Jones EFIM; residual and Jacobian rows decrease by a
factor $I/r$. The noiseless delay poles are preserved provided the compressed
non-frequency snapshot matrix
$[\beta_k(\bh_k\otimes\bQ_S^{\mathsf H}\bBm_k\be_k)]_{k=1}^K$ has rank $K$,
which holds generically for nondegenerate panel responses and training when
$rT\ge K$, but is checked rather than inferred from dimension alone.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof sketch:} a unitary completion of $\bU_S$ separates the data into a
parameter-dependent projection and noise-only coordinates entering the
likelihood only through $c_\perp$, which gives (i) and (ii); the same
semi-unitary map preserves every Gram product in \eqref{eq:efim_short} and,
acting only on the EVS mode, commutes with the shared Vandermonde delay
factors. Details are supplementary.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the three projection roles]
\label{rem:c2_scope}
The projection does three things under three validity conditions.
Sufficiency and EFIM preservation apply to the matched statistical model and
require the white-noise model of Section~\ref{subsec:obsmodel}. The coupled
recovery is equivariant only \emph{after} the observation is projected, so it
is the raw recovery applied to a projected observation and can change the
estimate through noise suppression. Only the likelihood isometry
\eqref{eq:isometry_short} is deterministic, holding pointwise for every
candidate and any observation, so it can move neither the final objective nor
its optimum. Pole preservation adds four conditions (distinct poles, adequate
smoothing dimensions, nonzero paths, and full compressed snapshot rank) that
the implementation records instead of assuming. Under colored noise an optimal
processor would whiten or use a covariance-weighted projector, so the
unwhitened sweep of Section~\ref{subsec:res_robustness} is a mismatch test
and not an extension of Theorem~\ref{thm:compress}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[C3: nested certified clock profile]
\label{thm:clock}
Assume $\bH(\bp)=\bG_0(\bp)+\bR_{\rm dir}(\bp)\succ\mathbf0$. Then analytic
Jones elimination and global inner clock maximization nest the penalized
criterion \eqref{eq:penalized_short} exactly:
$F(\bp,\Delta t)=\min_{\bx}J(\bp,\Delta t,\bx)=\|\by\|^2-Q(\bp,\Delta t)$,
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\bp\in\mathcal P}\widetilde F(\bp)
 =\min_{\bp\in\mathcal P,\,\Delta t\in\mathcal T,\,\bx\in\C^{2K}}
 J(\bp,\Delta t,\bx),
 \label{eq:nestval_short}
\end{equation}
and the two argument sets correspond:
$\arg\min_{\bp}\widetilde F$ is the projection onto $\bp$ of
$\arg\min_{\bp,\Delta t,\bx}J$, and pairing any outer minimizer
$\bp^\star$ with a global inner clock maximizer of $Q(\bp^\star,\cdot)$ and
with $\bx^\star=\bH^{-1}(\bp^\star)\bb(\bp^\star,\Delta t^\star)$ recovers
a joint minimizer. The outer profile is invariant under every invertible
affine clock-unit reparameterization with $\mathcal T$ transformed
accordingly. The interval
upper bound \eqref{eq:bound_short} further guarantees at every visited position
\begin{equation}
 Q(\Delta t^\star)-Q(\hat{\Delta t})\le g_{\rm cert}
 =U-Q(\hat{\Delta t}),
 \label{eq:cert_short}
\end{equation}
and $g_{\rm cert}\le\epsilon_{\rm clk}$ whenever termination occurs by the
prescribed tolerance rather than the subdivision cap.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof sketch:} unitarity in \eqref{eq:orbit_short} makes $\bH$ clock
independent, so completing the square eliminates $\bx$ exactly and the order of
minimization may be exchanged, while termwise differentiation of
\eqref{eq:trig_short} bounds the second derivative so that $U$ dominates the
unknown global maximum; the full proof is supplementary. The theorem certifies
the one-dimensional clock solve and removes its outer coordinate; it does not
guarantee global convergence of the 3-D position search.

\begin{remark}[Reading the PEB comparison]
\label{rem:peb_reading}
Two distinctions govern Section~\ref{sec:simulation}. First, the certificate
is separate from its stopping test: were the subdivision cap reached,
\eqref{eq:cert_short} would still be a valid a posteriori gap without
necessarily meeting the tolerance, so the correctness of the bound and the
empirical success of the test are two facts. Second, the regularized and free
estimates in a common basin share their first-order high-SNR distribution
only if the anchor error is $O_{\mathsf P}(\sigma)$ \emph{and} the weights
vanish, whereas the weights here are held at $\lambda_k=1$ for every path. An
observed root-mean-square error (RMSE) slightly below the free-Jones PEB is
therefore consistent with the data-adaptive bias that the fixed-weight
penalty introduces, and is neither a bound violation nor evidence of
asymptotic efficiency. The compatibility proposition is supplementary.
\end{remark}

Two features of the resulting cost matter. Each Phase~II outer position
evaluation costs $O(qM_r+q^2(N+T))+O(q^2N^2)+O(qM_c\log M_c+q^3)
+C_{\rm cert}$ with $q=2K$, $M_r=rNT$, and $M_c$ the zero-padded clock-FFT
length, so the profiled solve acts on $M_r$ compressed rows instead of the
$M=I\,N\,T$ raw rows. The Gram update is $O(q^2(N+T))$ rather than
$O(q^2M_r)$ because the Kronecker structure of \eqref{eq:Ak} makes each Gram
block the product of a cached, position-independent Maxwell--Jones factor
with one length-$N$ delay inner product and one length-$T$ training inner
product. The term-by-term accounting for both phases, including the
branch-and-bound term $C_{\rm cert}$, is supplementary. The exact spherical
panel projection, not the certified clock profile, dominates the measured
runtime (Section~\ref{subsec:res_cost}).

\section{Numerical Results}
\label{sec:simulation}

\subsection{Protocol and Reference Bounds}
\label{subsec:res_setup}

Unless varied, the matched scene uses $f_c=60$~GHz, $K=3$ oriented
$64\times64$ half-wavelength RISs, an $M_A=16$ EVS ULA, $N=63$
subcarriers at $\Delta f=5$~MHz, and $T=256$ training blocks. The UE is at
$(1.25,0.55,0.75)$~m, the true clock is $5$~ns in a $[0,10]$-ns search
interval, and the minimum cascaded-delay separation is $3.28$~ns, close to
$1/B_{\rm occ}=3.23$~ns. Per trial the path gains and Jones states are drawn
independently as $\beta_k\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1/K)$,
$\gamma_k\sim\mathcal U(0.35,1.10)$, and $\psi_k\sim\mathcal U(-\pi,\pi]$,
and with $\bmu_{\rm act}$ the noiseless samples on the active receive
components and $M_{\rm act}$ their scalar count, the noise variance is set so
that ${\rm SNR}=10\log_{10}[\|\bmu_{\rm act}\|_2^2/(M_{\rm act}\sigma^2)]$.
The reported SNR is therefore the average pre-noise sample SNR on the active
receive entries, not a post-processing integration SNR, and absolute path loss
is absorbed into this normalization; the receiver-mode study instead sets
$\sigma^2$ from the full-EVS observation and reuses that absolute variance for
every component mask. Phase~I acquires the basin when it returns a finite
$\hat\bp_{\rm I}$ with $\|\hat\bp_{\rm I}-\bp^\star\|_2\le0.1$~m. Complete
geometry and signal parameters are supplementary.

Each accuracy cell contains $480$ paired trials, except the external
benchmark of Section~\ref{subsec:res_benchmark}, which uses $960$; the C3
factor control uses $100$ trials and the serialized cost cells $30$.
Pairing is \emph{within} a suite: methods in the same cell share the scene,
RIS codes, Jones states, gains, and noise realization, verified by a stored
observation hash, whereas different suites draw from independent seed roots
and share no realization. A position estimate is catastrophic if the method
fails or its error exceeds $0.1$~m, a clock estimate if its absolute error
exceeds $1$~ns; no failed or nonfinite row is discarded, and $P_{\rm cat}$
denotes the empirical catastrophic-event rate for the stated criterion.
Catastrophic-rate intervals are exact $95\%$ Clopper--Pearson intervals, the
95th and 99th percentiles p95 and p99 are nearest-rank observations, paired
differences use bootstrap intervals, and outlier exchanges use McNemar's exact
test. All constants, including every baseline hyperparameter, were fixed
before the reported cells were evaluated and were not tuned per SNR or
realization. Runtime claims come only from the cost suites, which force a
single process and one linear-algebra-library thread and sample the peak
resident set size (RSS) every $10$~ms; parallel-harness times are excluded
throughout.

Position errors are bimodal, with millimeter inliers separated from decimeter
wrong-basin estimates, so moving the threshold to $0.05$ or $0.20$~m leaves
every stated proposed-route rate unchanged. Unless labeled unconditional,
continuous-error summaries are computed over the finite trials below that
threshold and are always reported with a tail rate, so a conditional RMSE
never stands alone as an unconditional accuracy claim; unconditional
reconstruction means are added wherever wrong-basin trials dominate the
aggregate channel error. The evidence comprises receiver modes, compression,
calibration and noise mismatch, a $50$-position grid, five resource sweeps,
resolvability maps, and an external benchmark, each fixed-SNR study measured
at $-10$~dB except the model-order and RIS element-phase sweeps at $0$~dB.
Complete curves below $-10$~dB appear only in the supplement; selected
below-region ablations are quoted here solely to expose failure mechanisms and
do not extend the claimed operating region. A nominally identical
configuration is therefore measured more than once and its estimates differ by
sampling alone: the proposed route's $-10$~dB
catastrophic rate is $0.31\%$, $0.62\%$, and $0.83\%$ in the benchmark,
compression, and internal suites, which is three trials in $960$ and three
or four in $480$, whose exact $95\%$ intervals share the common overlap
$[0.23,0.91]\%$. Every rate is quoted with the suite that produced it and
never pooled across suites.
At $-10$~dB, over $20$ Jones/gain realizations at the default position, the
median free-Jones PEB and CEB are $0.404$~mm and $3.35$~ps, the $x$ axis being
the weak one ($0.347$~mm against $0.156$ and $0.131$~mm) because all panels
lie in the $+x$ half-space, and treating the clock as known improves the
median PEB by only $5\%$. The oracle-Jones and free-Jones PEBs differ by less
than $0.005\%$ over all benchmark evaluations, so the data-only bound is the
sole reference. Because the geometry is fixed and the EFIM scales as
$2/\sigma^2$, both bounds scale as $\sigma$, so any CEB quoted at another SNR
is this median scaled accordingly, the benchmark suite logging no per-trial
CEB\@. The complete bound table is supplementary.

\subsection{C1: EVS Information and Resolvability}
\label{subsec:res_c1}

The C1 identities hold to numerical precision. For one panel the
receiver-mode EFIMs obey the predicted scalar relation to within
$5.7\times10^{-16}$; with three panels, reduced modes depart from a global
rescaling by up to $8.1\%$, confirming panel-dependent geometric reweighting
rather than a systematic condition-number penalty. Cross-panel polarimetric
residuals vanish numerically for the scalar and dual modes but remain
$0.0404$--$0.0837$ for full EVS, matching the principal-angle bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:split}.

Estimator behavior follows these identities across the three receiver modes
(Fig.~\ref{fig:c1_combined}). At $0$~dB, scalar, dual, and full modes have
conditional RMSEs $0.815$, $0.330$, and $0.133$~mm, consistent with their
matched PEB order. At $-10$~dB their catastrophic rates are $14.17\%$,
$2.29\%$, and $1.04\%$: the $13.6\times$ scalar/full rate ratio exceeds the
$5.7\times$ bound ratio because reduced modes lose panel-label separation as
well as energy. The resolvability map isolates that mechanism. At $1$~ns delay
separation, full EVS resolves the pair with probability $0.87$--$0.88$ across
all tested Jones overlaps, whereas at overlap $0.1$ the dual-polarized
receiver falls to $0.002$ and the scalar mode to zero; at the other tested
overlaps the dual-polarized receiver remains at or above $0.51$. Across delay separations, full-EVS resolvability is $0.95$ at $2$~ns and about
$0.53$ at $0.5$~ns, while at $0.1$~ns no mode resolves more than one pair in
$480$ trials. That invariance is a tested null: a Cochran--Armitage trend test
against overlap is insignificant in all six full-EVS delay cells (Holm-adjusted
$p=1.00$) whereas the reduced modes reject at $p$ down to $10^{-181}$, and the
largest full-EVS spread across overlap, $1.7$ percentage points, lies inside
the $4.6$-point half-width of a single cell against $82.7$ points for the
dual-polarized mode. The supplement gives the full test table. Two distinct
effects therefore act here, a shared bandwidth limit that full EVS does not
escape and a polarization-subspace loss that only the reduced modes suffer, so
full EVS keeps a cross-panel discrimination mechanism the reduced modes lose
without making arbitrarily coincident delays identifiable.

The fixed directional regularization is benign for the dual and full modes
but produces a high-SNR scalar floor: scalar conditional RMSE saturates near
$0.086$~mm while its PEB continues to fall, because with one retained
component the refreshed two-dimensional Jones anchor is not identifiable from
the retained data and the nonvanishing penalty can pull the solution. No
efficiency claim is therefore extended to scalar sensing. Full EVS serves
throughout as a calibrated information and algorithm reference, with the
scalar and dual modes as the hardware-nearer controls.
For full EVS, the RMS error divided by the RMS per-trial PEB is
$0.980$--$0.987$ in the receiver suite and $1.017$--$1.019$ in the
independently seeded internal suite, bracketing unity within $2\%$. That $2\%$
is the spread of the point estimates and not the resolution of the experiment,
since resampling the $480$ trials in pairs places a $95\%$ interval of about
$\pm6\%$ on each ratio, so the two suites establish consistency with
efficiency rather than agreement with the bound to two percent. The same
resampling leaves the dual-polarized ratio indistinguishable from unity and
from the full-EVS ratio, so no efficiency ordering is claimed among the modes;
the separation this paper claims is the information gap of
Fig.~\ref{fig:c1_combined}(a), a factor of $5.7$ and $2.3$. Ratios slightly
below one reflect the finite-sample, data-adaptive bias of
Remark~\ref{rem:peb_reading} and are not evidence of super-efficiency.

\subsection{C2: Compression and Acquisition}
\label{subsec:res_c2}

A single-factor test changes only the domain in which the delay subspace is
estimated; both arms share the remaining pipeline, and the full curves over
the campaign range are supplementary. At $-10$~dB, compression from $I=96$ to
$r=6$ improves delay RMSE by $1.5\times$ in the median
($0.0375\to0.0253$~ns) and $35\times$ at p95 ($4.87\to0.141$~ns), raises
basin acquisition from $97.50\%$ to $99.38\%$, and lowers the catastrophic
rate from $2.29\%$ to $0.62\%$. From $0$~dB upward the two routes return
numerically coincident positions, as expected once both delay-acquisition
procedures reliably enter the same basin. That empirical coincidence is a separate fact from the deterministic
likelihood isometry of Theorem~\ref{thm:compress}, which is an identity and is
therefore not credited with an empirical accuracy gain. The subspace
diagnostics agree: the aggregate-subspace rank is $r=2K=6$ in every trial, and
at $-10$~dB the subspace-leakage ratio $\sigma_{K+1}/\sigma_K$ falls from
$0.822$ in the raw EVS mode to $0.353$ after compression, so compression widens
the delay-subspace margin exactly where acquisition is fragile while leaving
the noiseless poles where they are. Rank, retained-energy and leakage ladders
are supplementary.

The full component ladder is flat inside the stated operating region and is
deferred. Quoted only to expose the mechanism, at $-20$~dB it separates:
compression reduces the catastrophic rate from $21.5\%$ to $5.6\%$ and
common-geometry fusion further to $4.0\%$, the latter lifting basin acquisition
from $78.1\%$ to $96.0\%$, and it is the fusion step rather than additional
restarts that does so, since the four-start, seven-start and oracle-position
controls agree to within one trial. Anchor refresh leaves the rate unchanged
while lowering the p95 channel-domain normalized mean-square error (NMSE) by
$27\times$, so it changes reconstruction rather than localization. The gain is
therefore tail suppression by delay denoising and basin acquisition, not a
guarantee that Phase~II estimates better; the ladders, controls and paired
tables are supplementary.
The aperture sweep tests the beam-space design directly. Growing $M_R$ from
$16^2$ to $64^2$ lowers conditional RMSE from $1.66$ to $0.42$~mm while holding
the catastrophic rate in $[0.21,0.62]\%$, and over the same range the
fixed-count Phase~I basin acquisition rises from $14.58\%$ to $95.62\%$ while
the proposed acquisition holds $99.58\%$ and $99.38\%$ at the two endpoints, so
aperture robustness is a property of the acquisition stage and not of the
refinement. Accuracy improves monotonically with $M_A$, $T$ and bandwidth as
well, the last with diminishing returns beyond $N=63$, and at $K=4$ the
proposed and range-scaled 4-D routes have catastrophic rates $1.88\%$ and
$12.92\%$.

\subsection{C3: Clock-Unit Control}
\label{subsec:res_c3}

Correctly range-scaled 4-D VP and CCOP-JVP have indistinguishable
position/clock precision once Phase~II is isolated at $-10$~dB over $100$
shared MKSC-GI initializers (Table~\ref{tab:c3_control}): $0.508811$ versus
$0.508802$~mm and $3.443828$ versus $3.443813$~ps, with no catastrophic trial
in either row. C3 therefore establishes equivalence after correct scaling and
claims no accuracy advantage. What CCOP-JVP removes is the clock coordinate,
which cuts the median outer evaluation count from $13$ to $9$. The explicit
4-D trajectory, by contrast, depends on whether the clock is carried in
seconds, nanoseconds, or range units: both mis-scaled labels lose one trial
out of $100$ that the range-scaled and profiled routes keep, and the seconds
label additionally degrades the conditional position RMSE by $31\%$.
Rerunning CCOP-JVP under all three labels returns identical candidate hashes
in every trial.
The certified gap normalized by its tolerance,
$\rho_{\rm cert}=g_{\rm cert}/\epsilon_{\rm clk}$, has median $0.69$ and
maximum $0.9997$ over the operating region, so every visited clock profile met
the prescribed tolerance with no subdivision cap, clock-branch ambiguity or
derivative-free safeguard triggered, at little runtime because each outer
evaluation reuses one factorization of $\bH$ across the whole clock grid.
Absolute gap distributions are supplementary. These facts certify only the
inner clock optimum: the outer 3-D position search remains local and
initialization dependent.

In the independent SNR suite, 4-D VP and CCOP-JVP sharing the same Phase~I
initializer have identical catastrophic rates at every SNR and matching
conditional RMSE, so the complete pipeline changes the tail only where its
initializer differs, at the low-SNR edge: $0.83\%$ and $0.21\%$ at $-10$ and
$-5$~dB against $2.50\%$ and $0.83\%$ for range-scaled 4-D VP, with inlier
conditional RMSEs agreeing to within $0.3\%$ there and to four significant
figures from $0$~dB upward. The difference is entirely which trials enter the
correct basin. C3 is therefore credited with dimensional reduction, unit
invariance and certification, while tail suppression is attributed to C2.

\subsection{External Benchmark}
\label{subsec:res_benchmark}

Five methods run on shared realizations under a method-consistent refinement
protocol (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark_combined}): every acquisition route receives
the continuous read-out or refinement its own formulation prescribes. These
stages are consistent in role rather than identical in operator, since ALS-CPD
refines in the CP-factor domain, near-field OMP runs SAGE sweeps followed by a
weighted least-squares (WLS) read-out, and the VBI/SBL adaptation receives the
joint refinement because its closed-form read-out cannot remove the common
clock without a direct path. Stopped at that as-published rule instead it has
$0.49$--$0.81$~m median position error and a clock error that does not vanish
with SNR, so reporting the stronger tier is the conservative choice; the
supplement preserves the as-published tier as a scope diagnostic. All
acquisition stages use fixed medium-size configurations and are adaptations to
the common forward model rather than claims that the reference methods were
designed for this blocked-LoS polarimetric scene.

At $-10$~dB the proposed, range-scaled 4-D VP, and VBI/SBL routes have nearly
identical median position errors ($0.316$, $0.320$, and $0.325$~mm) but
catastrophic rates of $0.31\%$, $1.77\%$, and $2.40\%$; the near-field OMP
route also has $1.77\%$ and the ALS-CPD adaptation $44.1\%$. Because the
trials are paired, McNemar's exact test on the catastrophic indicator rejects
equality against each of the three closest comparators: $16$ rescued against
$2$ introduced versus range-scaled 4-D VP ($p=1.3\times10^{-3}$), $17$ against
$3$ versus near-field OMP ($p=2.6\times10^{-3}$), and $23$ against $3$ versus
VBI/SBL ($p=8.8\times10^{-5}$). All three survive a Bonferroni correction at the $5\%$ level, and the ALS-CPD
adaptation is separated by $420$ exchanges against none. The error
distributions separate at the same place: by the $0.1$-m threshold the
proposed complementary CDF has fallen to $0.31\%$ while the three comparable
routes still carry $1.8$--$2.4\%$ and the ALS-CPD adaptation $44\%$. From
$-5$~dB upward the proposed route has no catastrophic trial and the paired
discordance is one-sided in every comparison, no comparator solving a
realization the proposed route loses. The OMP route instead exhibits a
$0.79$-mm floor, so its median gap grows with SNR, while the ALS-CPD
adaptation carries a persistent geometric-read-out branch whose catastrophic
rate remains $30.5$--$33.5\%$ from $0$ to $30$~dB. This behavior is specific
to the evaluated adaptation and implementation and is not attributed to the
reference CPD method.

The tail is read from the distribution rather than from one order statistic,
because no single quantile survives this sample size: once a route carries
about $1\%$ catastrophic mass, the $99$th percentile of $960$ trials straddles
the in-basin and wrong-basin modes, its bootstrap interval spanning four orders
of magnitude for the VBI/SBL adaptation above $20$~dB. The complementary CDFs
of Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark_combined}(b) and~(e) carry the same comparison
without conditioning on a rank, their threshold crossing being exactly the
rate plotted beside them. Near-field OMP is the one comparator also separated
in the median, its floor growing from a $2.6\times$ ratio at $-10$~dB to
$253\times$ at $30$~dB; since that method does receive continuous refinement,
the floor comes from a grid-induced local basin that its SAGE stage does not
leave, the discrete seed fixing a support inside which the continuous steps
converge.
Paired inference supports only the low-SNR tail claim. Against range-scaled
4-D VP in the internal suite, the proposed RMSE difference has a bootstrap
interval excluding zero at $-10$ and $-5$~dB ($-0.135$ and $-0.069$~m), while
at and above $0$~dB neither route exchanges an outlier and their paired
position difference is below $1.1\times10^{-8}$~m, so no high-SNR accuracy
advantage is inferred from coincident curves.

Synchronization mirrors the position ranking. At $0$~dB the proposed clock
inlier-conditional RMSE is $1.05$~ps against the $1.06$-ps CEB reference
obtained by scaling the $-10$~dB median bound of
Section~\ref{subsec:res_setup}. That comparison is descriptive rather than a
bound test: the reference is the median over $20$ Jones/gain realizations at
the default position for the unpenalized data model, not the per-trial bound
of this cell, and not a finite-sample bound for a penalized estimator
summarized over inliers (Remark~\ref{rem:peb_reading}). The correctly scaled
4-D route matches this inlier precision and separates only through tail mass,
which the doubled sample resolves as high as $0$~dB. Clock failures follow the
position ordering: at $-10$~dB the clock-catastrophic rates are $0.31\%$ for
the proposed route against $1.56$--$7.50\%$ for the four comparators.
Reconstruction separates the routes more sharply than position, and the two
conditionings must be read separately. In the observation-domain NMSE at
$0$~dB the proposed and range-scaled 4-D routes coincide among inliers
($5.1\times10^{-6}$ and $5.6\times10^{-6}$) but have unconditional means of
$5.1\times10^{-6}$ and $1.0\times10^{-3}$, one wrong-basin trial contributing
an $O(1)$ residual, while the three external routes retain
$8.1\times10^{-4}$--$2.4\times10^{-2}$ among inliers. That gap is therefore a
property of the geometries they acquire rather than of their tail masses, and
their failure mechanisms differ accordingly: a permutation and read-out tail
for ALS-CPD, a discrete acquisition basin for OMP, and blocked-LoS adaptation
for VBI/SBL\@. Full tables are supplementary.

Below the declared region the initializer-free near-field OMP grid has a lower
catastrophic rate than the proposed local pipeline at $-20$~dB ($2.60\%$
against $4.48\%$) and ties it at $-15$~dB, while retaining its permanent
resolution floor. This crossover, detailed in the supplement, supports a
coarse global screen as a fallback initializer and prevents a claim of uniform
dominance outside the tested operating region.

\subsection{Robustness and Spatial Generalization}
\label{subsec:res_robustness}

All mismatch and generalization sweeps are plotted in the supplement; three
boundaries are reported here because they bound deployment. The tightest is
RIS--BS angular calibration, and it costs precision long before it costs
capture: at $0.25^\circ$ the basin rate is still $99.17\%$ and
$P_{\rm cat}=0.62\%$, yet the conditional RMSE has risen from $0.447$ to
$31.2$~mm, while capture degrades only later, at $59.58\%$ for $1^\circ$. The
$-10$-dB sweep therefore establishes a calibration-limited accuracy boundary,
not its SNR scaling, and does not justify extrapolating a sub-$0.1^\circ$
tolerance that was never tested.
The fixed Stage-I constants were swept under the same paired protocol at
$-10$~dB with $480$ trials per value. The shortlist, the beam-space range grid
and the confidence weights are inert, the exhaustive $K!=6$ shortlist
reproducing the frozen estimate on all $480$ trials, whereas the association
clock weight does not favor the frozen value: $w_{\rm clk}=0$ rescues all four
catastrophic trials and introduces none, though four discordant pairs do not
resolve the effect ($p=0.125$), so this suite gives no evidence that the term
improves basin capture rather than evidence that deleting it helps. The table
is supplementary.
Receiver-side gain, phase, and sensor-position mismatch, RIS element-phase
error, and first-order colored noise are milder over their tested ranges and
fail in the same direction, degrading reconstruction well before capture.
These empirical boundaries do not substitute for spatial-wideband or
colored-noise modeling.

Model order inverts the pattern and is the one assumption that fails
abruptly. In the dedicated $0$-dB sweep, underestimating the order with
$\widehat K=2$ is structurally destructive, with median error $0.710$~m and
$P_{\rm cat}=65.4\%$, whereas overestimating it with $\widehat K=4$ and $5$
retains submillimeter medians of $0.151$ and $0.329$~mm but carries
catastrophic rates of $35.4\%$ and $49.0\%$. Known order is therefore a real
assumption, swept in full in the supplement. Together with the calibration
case it shows why median-only robustness reporting would be misleading in
either direction.
Across a $5\times5\times2$ grid of UE positions, the proposed median
per-position catastrophic rate is $0.62\%$ versus $2.92\%$ for 4-D VP\@. Both
hard corners lie near a cascaded-delay coincidence surface, the worst with
$31.25\%$ catastrophic trials at a minimum separation of only
$0.062/B_{\rm occ}$. Yet the free-Jones EFIM is full rank at every tested
position and these corners rank $14$th and $16$th of $50$ by PEB, while the
position with the largest PEB has only the nominal $0.62\%$ rate; the four
cells below $1.2$~ns of delay separation are exactly the four worst cells.
Aperture and polarimetry do retain local spatial information near delay
coincidence, but delay-first acquisition cannot exploit it. Local information
sufficiency therefore does not imply global acquisition reliability: a PEB map
alone is insufficient for deployment screening, which should instead flag
delay-coincidence surfaces and provide a coarse global fallback there.

\subsection{Computational Cost and Failure Diagnostics}
\label{subsec:res_cost}

At $-10$~dB the proposed route runs faster than all three external baselines
($3.20$ versus $3.84$--$13.0$~s) but exceeds range-scaled 4-D VP by about one
third and uses more memory (Table~\ref{tab:cost}, serialized single-thread
means). A separately instrumented block-by-block ablation suite reproduces
that mean to $0.2\%$, and its median stage times, which are descriptive and
need not sum to the total, are $0.33$~s for certified Phase~II, $0.85$~s for
common geometry, $0.23$~s for compression, $0.09$~s for anchor refresh and
$1.68$~s for the remaining Phase~I blocks, so the dominant term is the exact
spherical panel projection rather than clock certification. Retaining the raw
Hankel tensor for the mode-1 contraction dominates the $2.51$~GB peak, and the
dense raw Phase~II dictionary is never formed. The trade is memory for
continuous spherical resolution, with the aggregate subspace keeping repeated
refinement affordable; certification does not make the pipeline uniformly
cheaper. Detailed mismatch, position, scaling and cost plots are
supplementary.
The remaining failures are observable Phase~I events. Across the $4\,320$
operating-region trials of the internal suite, exactly five proposed-route
trials are catastrophic and exactly the same five fail the basin diagnostic,
each with a Phase~I delay RMSE above $0.5$~ns. Panel association is correct in
$99.93\%$ of trials overall and in every trial at and above $0$~dB. Phase~II
is thus a precise local refinement, not a guaranteed repair for failed delay
acquisition.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

One calibrated physical structure, the Maxwell--Jones factorization, yields
three results. Receiver modes scale per-panel geometric information and
determine whether polarimetry can distinguish panels; projection onto a known
aggregate Maxwell--Kronecker subspace preserves the profiled-likelihood
optimizer and information while removing signal-free directions before factor
recovery; and the common-clock unitary orbit reduces joint position--clock
refinement to a 3-D outer search with a certified clock optimum at every
visited position. Combined with Rayleigh-spaced beam acquisition and
common-geometry fusion, these properties suppress wrong-basin tails while
retaining near-reference inlier position and clock accuracy.
The results support a mechanism-specific interpretation rather than a claim of
uniform superiority. Full EVS supplies a discrimination mechanism that reduced
modes lack, Maxwell--Kronecker compression improves the fragile delay tail,
common geometry converts panel-local factors into a reliable basin, anchor
refresh mainly improves channel reconstruction, and CCOP-JVP makes the clock
solve unit invariant and certifiable. Once two continuous routes are inside
the same basin their position and clock precision are essentially the same, so
CCOP-JVP is credited with dimensional reduction, unit invariance, and
certification rather than with accuracy, and its certificate does not
globalize the outer position search.

The limits are equally explicit. Millimeter accuracy depends strongly on
RIS--BS angular calibration, model order must be known, and full-rank local
information does not prevent acquisition failure near a cascaded-delay
coincidence surface. Three modeling extensions remain outside the present
scope: the spatial-narrowband manifolds do not quantify beam-squint mismatch,
the blocked-LoS single-path-per-panel support covers neither direct leakage nor
diffuse multipath, and every suite uses one infrastructure layout, so
panel-placement diversity is never sampled. Extending the aggregate subspace
and clock profile to the first two requires a change to the declared signal
model rather than a retuning of the present physics, so spatial-wideband
modeling, explicit model-order selection and a coarse global fallback for the
coincidence regions are the principal extensions. Complete proofs, ablations,
robustness sweeps and below-threshold results are supplementary.
