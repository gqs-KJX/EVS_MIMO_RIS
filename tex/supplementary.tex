\section{Analytical and Algorithmic Details}

\subsection{Free-Jones EFIM and Closed-Form Derivatives}

Let $\bchi=[\bp^{\mathsf T},\Delta t]^{\mathsf T}$, let
$\bD_\chi=[\partial(\bA\bx)/\partial\chi_i]_{i=1}^{4}$ at the true
parameters, and explicitly realify the complete parameter vector as
$\bvt=[\bchi^{\mathsf T},\Real\{\bx\}^{\mathsf T},
\operatorname{Im}\{\bx\}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$.  For
$\by\sim\mathcal{CN}(\bA(\bchi)\bx,\sigma^2\bI)$, its complex Jacobian blocks
with respect to these real coordinates are
\begin{equation}
 \bG=\big[\,\bD_\chi,\ \bA,\ j\bA\,\big].
 \label{eq:s_efim_jac}
\end{equation}
The last two blocks span over $\R$ the complex column space
$\coll(\bA)$ viewed as a real subspace.  Because this subspace is closed
under multiplication by $j$, its projector under the real inner product
$\Real\{\ba^{\mathsf H}\bv\}$ equals the usual complex projector
$\bA\bA^\dagger$.  Eliminating the real and imaginary Jones coordinates is
therefore orthogonal projection of the geometric derivatives, giving
\begin{equation}
 \bJ_\chi^{\rm free}
 =\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\Real\!\left\{\bD_\chi^{\mathsf H}
 \big(\bI-\bA\bA^\dagger\big)\bD_\chi\right\}.
 \label{eq:s_efim}
\end{equation}
This is the generalized Schur complement of the Jones nuisance block.  The
Moore--Penrose inverse makes it valid when $\bA$ is rank deficient; it reduces
to the ordinary Schur complement when the nuisance information block is
invertible.

The EFIM used for the PEB and CEB is evaluated without numerical
differencing.  Put $\nu_n=n-(N-1)/2$ and
$\bN_\nu=\bI_T\otimes\diag(\nu_0,\ldots,\nu_{N-1})\otimes\bI_I$.  The common
clock orbit of main-paper Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:orbit_short} gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial(\bA\bx)}{\partial\Delta t}
 =-j2\pi\Delta f\,\bN_\nu\bA\bx .
 \label{eq:s_clock_jac}
\end{equation}
For position coordinate $p_i$, define
$\boldsymbol\delta_k=[\delta_{k,1},\ldots,\delta_{k,M_R}]^{\mathsf T}$ and
use the aperture derivative in main-paper Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:apertjac}.  The
remaining factor derivatives and the product rule are
\begin{align}
 \frac{\partial\bd_k}{\partial p_i}
 &=-j2\pi\Delta f\,\diag(\nu_0,\ldots,\nu_{N-1})\bd_k
 \frac{[\bR_k^{\mathsf T}\bs_{{\rm UR},k}]_i}{c_0},\nonumber\\
 \frac{\partial\bh_k}{\partial p_i}
 &=\bOmega_k\!\left[\bg_k\odot
 \left(-j\kappa\frac{\partial\boldsymbol\delta_k}{\partial p_i}\right)
 \right],\nonumber\\
 \frac{\partial\bA_k}{\partial p_i}
 &=\left(\frac{\partial\bh_k}{\partial p_i}\otimes\bd_k
 +\bh_k\otimes\frac{\partial\bd_k}{\partial p_i}\right)\otimes\bBm_k .
 \label{eq:s_position_jac}
\end{align}
Thus the $i$th position column of $\bD_\chi$ is
$\sum_k(\partial\bA_k/\partial p_i)\bx_k$.  A parameter-independent
compression uses the same formulas after replacing
$(\bA,\bD_\chi,\bBm_k)$ by
$(\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\bA,\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\bD_\chi,
\bQ_S^{\mathsf H}\bBm_k)$, respectively.  The unknown variance does not add
an EFIM correction: for circular Gaussian noise, its Fisher cross block with
all mean parameters is zero.

The absorbed carrier phase $e^{-j2\pi f_c\tau_k}$, including the common
range phase $e^{-j\kappa d_{{\rm UR},k}}$, and any smooth pathwise scalar
amplitude factor multiply $\bA_k\bx_k$ by a scalar; their position
derivatives lie in $\coll(\bA)$ and are annihilated by
$\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}$, so omitting them from~\eqref{eq:s_position_jac} is
exact for free complex $\bx_k$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:s_clock_jac} also isolates the free-Jones clock information,
\begin{align}
 J_{tt}^{\rm free}
 &=\frac{2}{\sigma^2}(2\pi\Delta f)^2
 \big\|\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bN_\nu\bA\bx\big\|_2^2,\nonumber\\
 {\rm CEB}
 &\ge \frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi\Delta f\,
 \|\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bN_\nu\bA\bx\|_2}.
 \label{eq:s_clock_info}
\end{align}
The second line accounts for the additional position nuisance through the
Schur complement; equality holds when the clock column is EFIM-orthogonal to
the position columns.  The same generator $\bN_\nu$ makes the conditional
clock profile $Q(\bp,\cdot)$ a finite trigonometric polynomial in C3 and
quantifies the information carried by that profile.

\subsection{Phase-II Normal Matrix and Envelope Gradient}

Let $s_k=\tfrac12\tr\bG_{0,kk}$ and partition
$\bA_0=[\bA_{0,1},\ldots,\bA_{0,K}]$ into its two-column Jones blocks.

\begin{lemma}[Anchor-column test for positive definiteness]
\label{lem:s_glambda}
Suppose every anchor $\hat\be_k$ is nonzero and $\lambda_ks_k>0$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \bZ_{\hat e}=\big[\bA_{0,1}\hat\be_1,\ldots,
 \bA_{0,K}\hat\be_K\big].
 \label{eq:s_anchor_columns}
\end{equation}
Then $\bG_\lambda\succ\mathbf0$ if and only if
$\rank\bZ_{\hat e}=K$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
For $\bx=[\bx_1^{\mathsf T},\ldots,\bx_K^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$,
\begin{equation}
 \bx^{\mathsf H}\bG_\lambda\bx
 =\|\bA_0\bx\|_2^2+
 \sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_ks_k
 \|\bPi_{\hat\be_k}^{\perp}\bx_k\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:s_pd_quadratic}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is zero exactly when
$\bx_k=a_k\hat\be_k$ for some $a_k\in\C$ and
$\bZ_{\hat e}[a_1,\ldots,a_K]^{\mathsf T}=\mathbf0$.  Its only zero is
therefore $\bx=\mathbf0$ exactly when $\bZ_{\hat e}$ has column rank $K$.
\end{IEEEproof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:s_glambda} turns the nullspace condition of main-paper
Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:glambda_pd} into a pointwise rank test.  In the scalar
receiver mode, $\bS_1\bTheta_k^{(6)}\hat\be_k\ne\mathbf0$ is necessary for
each anchor column to survive, and the $K$ surviving columns must additionally
remain jointly independent.  The resulting rank test costs $O(M_rK^2)$ at
any candidate position.

The implementation also evaluates the Phase-II position gradient
analytically.  At a fixed clock branch, put
$\hat\bx=\bG_\lambda^{-1}\bb$ and write $\partial_i=\partial/\partial p_i$.
Differentiating $Q=\bb^{\mathsf H}\bG_\lambda^{-1}\bb$ without
differentiating $\hat\bx$ explicitly gives
\begin{equation}
 \partial_iQ=2\Real\{\hat\bx^{\mathsf H}\partial_i\bb\}
 -\Real\{\hat\bx^{\mathsf H}(\partial_i\bG_\lambda)\hat\bx\}.
 \label{eq:s_envelope_grad}
\end{equation}
With the refreshed anchors held fixed during Phase~II, every term is
available from the response derivatives above:
\begin{align}
 \partial_i\bb
 &=(\partial_i\bA_0)^{\mathsf H}\bU_{\rm clk}^{\mathsf H}\by,\nonumber\\
 \partial_i\bG_0
 &=(\partial_i\bA_0)^{\mathsf H}\bA_0
 +\bA_0^{\mathsf H}(\partial_i\bA_0),\nonumber\\
 \partial_i\bR_{{\rm dir},kk}
 &=\lambda_k\Real\!\left\{\tr\!\left(
 \bA_{0,k}^{\mathsf H}\partial_i\bA_{0,k}\right)\right\}
 \bPi_{\hat\be_k}^{\perp}.
 \label{eq:s_envelope_terms}
\end{align}
At a unique interior clock maximizer, main-paper Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:envelope}
then gives $\nabla\widetilde F=-\nabla Q$.  The implementation divides both
$F$ and this gradient by $M=INT$, which does not change the optimizer.  The
calculation reuses $\hat\bx$ and the existing normal-matrix factorization; the
Gram derivatives use the same Kronecker products as main-paper
Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:gram_factorization}, so no additional $2K$-dimensional
factorization is required.

\subsection{Coupled Factor Recovery}

The coupled recovery uses the balanced Hankel window
$P=\lceil N/2\rceil$ and $L=N+1-P$.  Its anti-diagonal multiplicity
$w_n$ counts the pairs $(p,\ell)$ satisfying $p+\ell=n$.  After absorbing the
centered-tone column scalar into the recovered factor, write
$[\bD]_{n,k}=z_k^n$.  Let $\bY_{{\rm d},t}\in\C^{r\times N}$ denote the
compressed receive--tone slice at configuration $t$.  Define its balanced
Hankel lift by
\[
 [\mathcal Z]_{i,p,\ell,t}=[\bY_{{\rm d},t}]_{i,p+\ell},
 \qquad 0\le p<P,\quad 0\le\ell<L,
\]
and denote its row-$(i,t)$, column-$(p,\ell)$ matricization by
$\mathcal Z_{(i,t),(p,\ell)}\in\C^{rT\times PL}$.  For each estimated pole
$z_k$, the implementation constructs
$[\bv_{P,k}]_p=z_k^p$ and $[\bv_{L,k}]_\ell=z_k^\ell$ from that same pole;
the $k$th lifted design column is $\bv_{P,k}\otimes\bv_{L,k}$.  Hence
\begin{align}
 [\bD_{\rm lift}]_{(p,\ell),k}&=z_k^{p+\ell},\nonumber\\
 \bD_{\rm lift}^{\mathsf T}\bD_{\rm lift}^{\ast}
 &=\bD^{\mathsf T}\bW\bD^{\ast},\nonumber\\
 \mathcal Z_{(i,t),(p,\ell)}\bD_{\rm lift}^{\ast}
 &=\bY_{rT,N}\bW\bD^{\ast}.
 \label{eq:s_lift_equivalence}
\end{align}
Thus the lifted and weighted matrix-domain normal equations, including the
same diagonal loading, are identical to main-paper
Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:coupledls_short}.  Both Vandermonde factors are powers of
one $z_k$, so each design column depends on one delay only; the lift is a
weighting device, not an independent projection or a second decomposition.

\subsection{Information and Compression Scope}

For a real geometric direction $\bu$, free-Jones information has the
variational representation
\begin{equation}
 \bu^{\mathsf T}\bJ_\chi^{\rm free}\bu
 =\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\min_{\delta\bx}
 \left\|\bD_\chi\bu+\bA\delta\bx\right\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:s_nested_info}
\end{equation}
Adding a nested set of receive components appends nonnegative residual terms
to this minimized quadratic form and hence cannot decrease the free-Jones
information.  This aggregate order remains valid even when a receive mask
makes an individual Jones block singular; a single-panel scalar information
ratio alone need not order a multi-panel EFIM.

The likelihood isometry in main-paper Theorem~\ref{main-thm:compress} is a
pointwise deterministic identity for every observation and candidate.
Interpreting the compressed observation as a sufficient statistic and
preserving the stated EFIM additionally use the matched white-Gaussian model.
With colored covariance, whitening or the corresponding covariance-weighted
projection is required; that case is outside the theorem.

More generally, any parameter-independent semi-unitary map whose range
contains both $\coll(\bA)$ and $\coll(\bD_\chi)$ preserves the free-Jones
information by the same argument.  The calibrated Maxwell--Kronecker basis is
useful because it is fixed without UE-dependent quantities.  Preservation of
the noiseless delay poles further requires distinct nonzero paths, adequate
frequency aperture, and rank $K$ of the compressed non-frequency snapshot
matrix; these are acquisition conditions, not consequences of likelihood
sufficiency.

\section{Phase-I Complexity by Block}

Let $N_{\rm pk}$ be the number of retained angular peaks, $N_r$ the exact
range-scan length, $N_\pi$ the number of shortlisted permutations, $N_s$ the
number of exact local starts, and $I_{\rm ex}$ the objective/gradient
evaluations used per start.
Receive compression costs $O(IrNT)$.  The reference delay solver forms the
$N\times N$ snapshot covariance and eigendecomposes it, costing
$O(N^2rT+N^3)$; its FB-TLS step adds $O(NK^2+K^3)$.  A direct thin-SVD
implementation would instead cost $O(NrT\min\{N,rT\})$ for the subspace.

For the balanced $P\times L$ lift, constructing and solving the coupled
normal equations costs
$O(rTPLK+PLK^2+rTK^2+K^3)$, followed by
$O(KrT\min\{r,T\})$ for the $K$ rank-one splits.  For one panel--column pair,
the beam-space proposal and exact candidate re-scoring cost
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm pair}=O\!\left(TM_R+G_rF\log F
 +N_{\rm pk}(N_rM_R+TM_R)\right),
 \label{eq:s_pair_cost}
\end{equation}
where $F=F_xF_y$.  The $N_r$ exact range tests act on the element-domain
adjoint and therefore cost $O(N_rM_R)$ per peak, not $O(N_rTM_R)$.
Candidate generation is evaluated for all $K^2$ panel--column pairs.  At
most $N_\pi K$ distinct pairs from the shortlisted permutations undergo
multi-start exact spherical refinement, adding
$O(N_\pi K N_s I_{\rm ex}TM_R)$, while permutation arithmetic costs
$O(KK!)$.  When enabled, the fixed-count dictionary of size
$|\mathcal C|$ adds $O(K|\mathcal C|TM_R+K^2|\mathcal C|T)$ for cached
panel responses and pairwise correlations.  Finally, $S$ common-geometry
starts with $I_g$ evaluations cost
$O(SI_gKTM_R)$.  Here $N_\pi=2$ and all other fixed budgets are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:s_settings}.  Thus $KK!$ is the asymptotic bottleneck in $K$;
for the reference $K=3$, beam-space and exact spherical response work,
rather than permutation arithmetic, carries the leading operation count.
Refreshing the Jones anchors at $\hat\bp_{\rm GI}$ requires one conditional
clock profile---approximately one $C_{\rm outer}$ in main-paper
Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:cost}---and introduces no outer iteration.

\section{Focused Numerical Audits}

Here p95 denotes the nearest-rank empirical 95th percentile.  The audits use
the protocols of the main paper and are included only where they delimit a
claim or make a reported comparison reproducible.

\subsection{Compression}

The calibrated aggregate subspace has numerical rank $r=2K=6$ in every
operating-region trial.  At $-10$~dB, projecting from $I=96$ components to
$r=6$ reduces the delay-subspace leakage
$\sigma_{K+1}/\sigma_K$ from $0.824$ to $0.357$ and the p95 Phase-I delay
error from $3.96$ to $0.156$~ns.  This is a finite-sample acquisition effect;
the matched profiled likelihood itself is unchanged by the sufficient
projection.

As an intentionally unwhitened mismatch test at $-10$~dB, receive-noise
correlations $[\bR]_{ij}=\rho^{|i-j|}$ across EVS components, white across
tones and configurations, give at $\rho=0,0.5,0.8$ catastrophic rates of
$0.83\%,0.42\%,0.21\%$ for the proposed route and
$1.04\%,1.88\%,2.71\%$ for its raw-delay control, which estimates the delay
subspace from the uncompressed observation while leaving all subsequent
stages unchanged.  The criterion is not covariance weighted, so these
results do not extend the matched-white-noise sufficiency statement.

\subsection{Full-EVS Directional Weight}

A paired full-EVS sweep uses $\lambda_k\in\{0,0.1,1,10\}$, five SNRs and
$480$ common-initializer trials per cell.  Across the four weights, the
largest position and clock movements are $6.23\times10^{-6}$~mm and
$3.76\times10^{-5}$~ps, respectively; every catastrophic indicator is
unchanged, and the largest penalty/data-objective ratio is
$6.45\times10^{-6}$.  Thus the reported full-EVS results are not driven by
the selected weight.  Algebraically, the penalty closes the Jones directions
orthogonal to the refreshed anchors; Lemma~\ref{lem:s_glambda} states the
remaining anchor-column rank condition for $\bG_\lambda\succ\mathbf0$.

\subsection{Clock Certificates}

Across the $4\,320$ operating-region trials, all $35\,696$ conditional clock
profiles returned a certified gap at or below the prescribed tolerance, and
none reached the subdivision cap.  The Powell safeguard is never selected,
and panel pairing is correct in every trial; two trials are catastrophic.  Each
certificate covers only the global clock maximization conditional on the
tested position.  It certifies neither statistical accuracy nor globality of
the outer position search.

\subsection{Model-Boundary Diagnostics}

\emph{Receiver calibration.}
In a separate generation-only audit at $-10$~dB,
$480$ paired trials per level, EVS log-gain standard deviation up to $0.1$,
EVS phase error up to $10^\circ$, and BS sensor-coordinate error up to
$0.5$~mm leave the conditional position RMSE at most $0.497$~mm, versus
$0.416$~mm without mismatch.  This statement covers only the tested ranges.

\emph{Spatial wideband.}
The spatial-wideband diagnostic generates each tone with
$f_n=f_c+\nu_n\Delta f$ and $\lambda_n=c_0/f_n$, while holding the physical
arrays, panel geometry and RIS codes fixed, and then applies the unchanged
center-frequency estimator.  It uses nine prespecified noiseless samples
(three positions and three realizations) and starts a local refinement from
the truth; Phase~I is not exercised.  Position bias spans
$0.0037$--$0.0804$~mm and has a maximum PEB-normalized value of $0.171$
relative to the sample-specific
$-10$-dB PEB.  Clock bias spans $0.19$--$5.38$~ps and exceeds the
sample-specific $-10$-dB CEB in four of the nine samples.  These values
diagnose deterministic spatial-wideband mismatch and do not bound capture or
catastrophic-event rates.
