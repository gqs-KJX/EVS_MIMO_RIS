\section{Scope}

This supplement contains the material intentionally removed from the
page-limited main paper. Part~I gives the complete reference configuration,
error-bound audit, spatial-narrowband condition, component ablation,
paired comparisons, robustness, generalization, scaling, cost, certificate
diagnostics, and external-benchmark tables. Part~II reports the $-15$ and
$-20$~dB threshold region, where Phase~I delay poles collapse. Part~III
contains the complete proofs of the three flagship theorems together with
the supporting local-identifiability, coupled-lift, scale-elimination, and
regularization results.

Abbreviations defined in the main paper retain their meanings. Throughout
this supplement, $P_{\rm cat}$ denotes the empirical catastrophic-event
rate, p95 and p99 the 95th and 99th percentiles, CI a confidence interval,
c-RMSE the inlier-conditional root-mean-square error, NMSE normalized
mean-square error, and RSS resident set size. Unqualified vector norms are
Euclidean, and unqualified matrix norms are spectral.

Every campaign statistic in both parts comes from the frozen
\texttt{paper\_v3} campaign, with the estimator constants and within-suite
paired protocol stated in the main paper. Different suites use independent
seed roots and share no realization. Each accuracy cell contains $480$
trials unless a cost or C3 control is explicitly identified. The external
benchmark uses its own seed root and extends its own stream to $960$ trials
per cell; its first $480$ trials are the exact prefix of that benchmark
stream, not realizations shared with the independently seeded suites.
Nothing here is re-run or re-tuned: the figures are produced by the same
script as the main-paper figures with the SNR floor moved from $-10$ to
$-20$~dB. Wherever a main-paper counterpart exists, the supplementary
curves are its full-range extension rather than a separate experiment.

\section{Spatial-Narrowband Sufficient Condition}
\label{sec:squint_supp}

Section~\ref{main-sec:system_model} of the main paper audits the neglected
spatial phase
$\epsilon_{{\rm sq},k}$ through the exact wavefront differences. The
following closed-form sufficient condition is the design-time version of
that audit. With fractional bandwidth $\beta_{\rm BW}=B_{\rm occ}/f_c$,
aperture radius $a_k$, UE range $R_k$, and in-plane direction component
$\bu_{k,\parallel}$, the center-frequency spatial-manifold model is
justified when
\begin{equation}
	\beta_{\rm BW}\left(
	\frac{a_k}{\lambda_c}\|\bu_{k,\parallel}\|_2
	+ \frac{a_k^2}{\lambda_c R_k}\right)\ll 1,
	\label{eq:squint_cond}
\end{equation}
whose second term is the fractional bandwidth times the Fresnel number
$R_{F,k}/R_k$ with $R_{F,k}=a_k^2/\lambda_c$. Near broadside the linear
aperture term is small and the curvature term dominates; away from
broadside the conventional squint term $\beta_{\rm BW}a_k/\lambda_c$ must
also be small. The same test applies separately to the inter-element path
differences of the RIS--BS and BS-ULA responses: knowing the infrastructure
geometry does not remove the frequency dependence of its spatial response.
Condition \eqref{eq:squint_cond} is a design screen and is not a substitute
for the exact audit, which must be evaluated through the exact wavefront
differences $L_k$ rather than inferred from the fractional bandwidth alone.
In the reference configuration ($\beta_{\rm BW}=0.52\%$) the audited values
are $0.102$, $0.058$ and $0.048$~rad, driven by $L_k$ of $31.3$, $17.8$ and
$14.8$~mm. All reported results are generated under the same
center-frequency manifold used by the estimator and therefore do not
measure sensitivity to \eqref{eq:squint_cond}.

\section{Resolvability Test Statistics}
\label{sec:resolv_supp}

This section gives the full test table behind the resolvability claim of
Section~\ref{main-subsec:res_c1}. Each cell is $480$ paired trials at
$-10$~dB at one delay separation and one Jones overlap, and a pair counts as
resolved when both components are recovered within the stated position
tolerance. Table~\ref{tab:s_resolv} reports the resolution probability
against overlap for the three receiver modes.

At $1$~ns separation full EVS resolves the pair with probability
$0.87$--$0.88$ across all four tested overlaps, whereas at overlap $0.1$ the
dual-polarized receiver falls to $0.002$ and the scalar mode to zero; at the
other three overlaps the dual-polarized receiver stays at or above $0.51$.
Across separations, full-EVS resolvability is $0.95$ at $2$~ns and about
$0.53$ at $0.5$~ns, varying by less than $0.018$ over the four overlaps,
while at $0.1$~ns no mode resolves more than one pair in $480$ trials.

The overlap-invariance of the full-EVS curves is a tested null and not a
reading of the plot. A Cochran--Armitage trend test of resolution probability
against overlap is insignificant in all six full-EVS delay cells, with
Holm-adjusted $p=1.00$, and a Fisher exact test between the two extreme
overlaps gives $p\ge0.70$; the reduced modes reject the same null at $p$ down
to $10^{-181}$. The largest full-EVS spread across overlap is $1.7$
percentage points, well inside the $4.6$-point exact Clopper--Pearson
half-width of a single cell, whereas the dual-polarized and scalar modes span
$82.7$ and $61.9$ points. The two statements are therefore separable: a
bandwidth limit that no mode escapes, and a polarization-subspace loss
confined to the reduced modes.

\section{Compression Diagnostics in the Operating Region}
\label{sec:compdiag_supp}

The aggregate Maxwell subspace attains its predicted dimension in every
trial of the operating region: the numerical rank of $\bPsi$ is $r=2K=6$
throughout, so the receive dimension falls from $I=96$ to $6$ with no
observed rank deficiency. The retained energy fraction rises from the
white-noise floor $r/I=1/16$, which is what a signal-free observation
projects onto a $6$-of-$96$ subspace, to $0.9991$ at $30$~dB, so the
projection discards essentially only noise once the signal dominates.

The subspace-leakage ratio $\sigma_{K+1}/\sigma_K$ measures how cleanly the
retained delay subspace separates from the noise subspace, smaller being
better. At $-10$~dB it falls from $0.822$ in the raw EVS mode to $0.353$
after compression, which is the margin that produces the p95 delay-error
improvement quoted in Section~\ref{main-subsec:res_c2}. Below the operating
region the same diagnostic is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:compression_below}, where both variants approach unity and
fail structurally.

\section{Receiver-Side Calibration, Colored Noise, and Model Order}
\label{sec:mismatch_supp}

Fig.~\ref{fig:s_robustness} gives the four mismatch sweeps at $-10$~dB with
generation-side perturbation only; panel~(a), the binding RIS--BS
incidence-angle boundary, is discussed in the main paper and is repeated
here for context.

\emph{Receiver-side calibration.} Table~\ref{tab:s_calib} gives the
endpoints of the three receiver-side sweeps against the RIS--BS angular
boundary of the main paper, on a common scale. Over the tested ranges (EVS
gain standard deviation up to $0.1$, EVS phase error up to $10^\circ$, and
BS sensor position error up to $0.5$~mm, or $\lambda_c/10$), the
catastrophic rate stays within $[0.62,1.04]\%$, statistically
indistinguishable from the nominal $0.83\%$, and the conditional RMSE rises
by at most $22\%$ (to $0.546$~mm, at the largest sensor-position error).
The pattern is uniform across the three: receiver-side errors cost channel
accuracy, degrading the median reconstruction NMSE by two to four orders
of magnitude, well before they cost position accuracy or reliability,
because the Jones coefficients absorb them while the geometry does not
move. The RIS--BS angular row is included at the same $-10$~dB operating
point to make the contrast quantitative: a $0.25^\circ$ incidence-angle
error, which leaves the catastrophic rate at its nominal value, has already
cost a factor of $70$ in conditional accuracy, whereas the largest tested
receiver-side error costs $22\%$. In all these sweeps the compressed route
retains its advantage over the raw-delay route (e.g.\ a $0.83\%$ against
$1.88\%$ catastrophic rate at zero mismatch), so the projection does not
trade robustness for variance reduction.

\emph{RIS element-phase calibration} (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_ris_calibration}) is
a different and far more forgiving quantity than the RIS--BS angular
calibration, and the two should not be conflated. It is tolerated up to
$10^\circ$ of per-element phase error, where the median error grows from
$0.097$ to $0.858$~mm at an unchanged $0\%$ catastrophic rate, and even at
$20^\circ$ the median is $1.69$~mm at a $0.21\%$ rate. The same figure
quantifies the indispensability of Phase~II: the Phase~I output alone is a
basin, not a millimeter-accurate final estimate. Its median error is
$6.6$~mm at perfect calibration ($68$ times the refined median and $53$
times the oracle-calibrated PEB), and it degrades some forty times faster
in absolute terms ($3.4$ against $0.08$~mm per degree of per-element phase
error), reaching $37$~mm at $10^\circ$ and failing outright at $20^\circ$
($75$~mm median, $32.3\%$ catastrophic). The oracle-calibrated PEB is flat
at $0.124$~mm across the whole sweep, so every departure from it is
estimator bias induced by the mismatch and not a change in what the
geometry permits.

\emph{Colored noise.} The estimator uses an unwhitened objective. Under
first-order correlated noise its catastrophic rate stays within one trial
of its matched value across the sweep ($0.21\%$ at $\rho=0$, $0.62\%$ at
$\rho=0.5$, $0.21\%$ at $\rho=0.8$), while the raw-delay variant
degrades from $1.46\%$ to $3.54\%$: the compression also absorbs part of
the model mismatch, since correlated noise energy outside the
Maxwell--Kronecker subspace is discarded before delay estimation. The
conditional accuracy improves with $\rho$ ($0.442\to0.299$~mm), which is
expected at fixed total noise power, since correlation concentrates that
power into fewer effective degrees of freedom. This is a boundary check
rather than a claim of robustness to unwhitened noise in general.

\emph{Model order.} With $\widehat K=2$ the estimator fails structurally
($65.4\%$ catastrophic, median error $0.71$~m). Overestimation is less
damaging in the median but is \emph{not} graceful: $\widehat K=4$ and
$\widehat K=5$ keep the median error at $0.15$ and $0.33$~mm, better
than the correctly ordered p95, while carrying catastrophic rates of
$35.4\%$ and $49.0\%$ and p95 errors of $1.28$ and $1.70$~m, at a runtime
that grows from $12.2$ to $15.9$ and $20.0$~s. The distribution is bimodal:
most trials are unaffected and a large minority lose the basin entirely.
The operational consequence, stated in the main paper, is that the order
must be known and not merely bracketed.

\section{Implementation Specification}
\label{sec:impl_supp}

This section fixes the operational detail of
Section~\ref{main-sec:algorithm} that the main text states only by name. All
values are those of the frozen \texttt{paper\_v3} campaign, which builds its
configuration from the package defaults; where a call site carries a
different fallback constant, the campaign value below is the one that
applies.

\subsection{Hankel Construction and Delay Poles}

The frequency axis is smoothed with the balanced window
$P=\lceil N/2\rceil$, $L=N+1-P$, and the lift is the plain Hankel map
$[\mathcal Z]_{i,p,\ell,t}=[\mathcal Y]_{i,\,p+\ell,\,t}$ for
$p=0,\ldots,P-1$ and $\ell=0,\ldots,L-1$, so the two frequency modes share
every tone through their anti-diagonals. The inverse map averages
anti-diagonals with the multiplicity weights $1/\#\{(p,\ell):p+\ell=n\}$.

Pole estimation does not run on the lifted tensor directly. The compressed
tensor is first mapped back by that averaging inverse, reshaped to a
frequency-by-snapshot matrix $\bY_f\in\C^{N\times rT}$ that treats the
compressed EVS and training indices as snapshots, and then augmented in
forward--backward form
$\bY_{\rm aug}=[\bY_f,\ \bJ\bY_f^{\ast}]$ with $\bJ$ the exchange matrix.
The $K$ leading left singular vectors of $\bY_{\rm aug}$ give the signal
subspace $\bE_s$; the shift-invariant pair is $\bE_1=\bE_s(1{:}N-1,:)$ and
$\bE_2=\bE_s(2{:}N,:)$, and the rotation is taken by total least squares,
$\bPsi_{\rm TLS}=-\bV_{12}\bV_{22}^{-1}$ from the right singular vectors of
$[\bE_1,\bE_2]$, falling back to ordinary least squares when
$N-1<2K$. The poles are the eigenvalues of $\bPsi_{\rm TLS}$, each divided
by its modulus so that only the delay phase survives. Hankelization
therefore acts as a structured denoiser and the shift invariance is read on
the tone index itself.

The coupled recovery \eqref{main-eq:coupledls_short} uses the Tikhonov
loading $\lambda_\tau=\lambda_{\rm rel}
\tr(\bD_\tau^{\mathsf T}\bD_\tau^{\ast})/K+\lambda_{\rm fl}$ with the
dimensionless relative term $\lambda_{\rm rel}=10^{-6}$ and the absolute
floor $\lambda_{\rm fl}=10^{-12}$. The relative term tracks the Gram scale so
that the loading is invariant to an overall rescaling of the data, and the
floor keeps the solve positive definite when that scale underflows. Neither
constant enters the reconstruction of the two frequency factors from the
shared pole, so neither can move a delay estimate.

\subsection{Beam-Space Acquisition}

The adjoint pass forms
$\tilde\bg_k=\ba_{{\rm RB},k}^{\ast}\odot(\bOmega_k^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bt_k)$
and never inverts $\bOmega_k$. Trial ranges are $G_r=9$ points spaced
uniformly over the panel range bounds. At each of them the Fresnel dechirp
$e^{+j\kappa\|\brho_{k,m}\|^2/(2r)}$ is applied and a two-dimensional
transform zero-padded by a factor $4$ per axis, capped at $512$ bins per
axis, evaluates the correlation on the direction-cosine grid
$u=\lambda_c\nu_x/d_x$, $v=\lambda_c\nu_y/d_y$ at the transform frequencies
$\nu$. A bin is admissible when $u^2+v^2<1$ and when its normal cosine
$w=\sqrt{1-u^2-v^2}$ and azimuth fall inside the panel search bounds; the
phase-only response is front/back ambiguous, so only $w\ge0$ is enumerated.

Peaks are then taken on the range-maximized angular map
$\max_r S_r(u,v)$ rather than on the full cube, because the aperture
resolves direction far better than range. Four peaks are extracted greedily,
each followed by a guard of $\lceil 4/2\rceil=2$ bins per axis to prevent
re-selecting the same mainlobe, and every selected direction receives an
exact spherical range scan on $41$ uniformly spaced ranges before local
refinement. The retained fixed-count dictionary
$\mathcal C_k^{\rm cnt}$ is the $5\times5\times7$ range/elevation/azimuth
grid over the global panel bounds, and the local grid used during refinement
is $5\times5\times7$ over spans of $0.45$~m and $0.12$~rad about the current
iterate.

\subsection{Association}

The first tier evaluates all $K!$ permutations with the RIS term of
\eqref{main-eq:assoc_score} taken on the cached coarse codebook rather than
on the exact spherical projection, which is what makes the exhaustive
ranking affordable. The
\texttt{stage1\_assignment\_num\_exact\_permutations} setting fixes the
shortlist at two; the two best coarse permutations are then re-scored with
the exact spherical projection. The reported margin is the relative gap
$(C^{(2)}-C^{(1)})/C^{(1)}$ between the two exact totals, and a trial is
flagged unreliable when it falls below $0.3$.

\subsection{Phase~II Solvers and Tolerances}

The inner clock search seeds its incumbent from a zero-padded transform of
length $M_c=4096$ over $\mathcal T$, then runs interval branch-and-bound
with the second-derivative bound \eqref{main-eq:bound_short}, bisecting the
interval of largest upper bound. Subdivision stops when the global gap falls
below
\begin{equation}
	\epsilon_{\rm clk}=M\,\epsilon_{\rm abs}
	+\epsilon_{\rm rel}\max\{\|\by\|^2,|Q_{\rm best}|\},
	\label{eq:eps_clk_supp}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon_{\rm abs}=10^{-12}$ and $\epsilon_{\rm rel}=10^{-10}$, or
when the cap $N_{\rm cap}=2\times10^4$ subdivisions is reached; the reported
gap remains a valid bound in either case. The outer position search is
L-BFGS-B~\cite{byrd1995lbfgsb} restricted to $\mathcal P$, with at most
$20$ iterations, $\texttt{ftol}=10^{-12}$ and $\texttt{gtol}=10^{-8}$, and a
Powell restart~\cite{powell1964direct} when a near-tied clock branch
makes the envelope derivative unreliable. The common-geometry stage uses the
same L-BFGS-B settings from its four deterministic starts.
Those four starts are built in a fixed order and contain no random or oracle
state. The first is the clipped coordinate-wise median of the $K$ panel-local
fixes, $\bp_0=\mathrm{clip}_{\mathcal P}\{\mathrm{median}_k
\hat\bp_k^{\rm loc}\}$; the remainder are taken in order from the
axis-aligned displacements of $\bp_0$ by $\pm25$~cm and then from height
samples spanning $\mathcal P$, each clipped back to $\mathcal P$, until four
distinct points have been collected.

The refreshed Jones anchor of \eqref{main-eq:refresh_short} is
$\hat\be_k=\hat\bx_{{\rm ref},k}/\|\hat\bx_{{\rm ref},k}\|_2$ for every
computed block of nonzero norm, however small, so no amplitude threshold is
introduced. Only when $\|\hat\bx_{{\rm ref},k}\|_2$ is zero at working
precision, which leaves the direction undefined, is the projector set to
$\bPi_{\hat\be_k}^{\perp}=\mathbf0$; that disables the directional penalty on
path $k$ and leaves the data term of \eqref{main-eq:penalized_short}
untouched.

\section{Complexity Accounting}
\label{sec:complexity_supp}

This section gives the term-by-term cost accounting summarized in
Section~\ref{main-sec:analysis}. Phase~I costs $O(IrPLT)$ for the mode-1
contraction and $O(rTPLK+PLK^2+K^3)$ for compressed factor recovery, the
last term being the $K\times K$ solve in
\eqref{main-eq:coupledls_short}. Beam-space acquisition requires one RIS
adjoint pass \eqref{main-eq:adjoint_short} plus $G_r$ two-dimensional FFTs
per panel, $G_r$ being the number of trial ranges, and common-geometry fusion
scales with its starts, iterations, panels, training blocks, and RIS
elements.

Each of the $I_p$ Phase~II outer position evaluations costs
\begin{align}
	C_{\rm eval}={}&O\big(qM_r+q^2(N+T)\big)+O(q^2N^2)\nonumber\\
	&+O(qM_c\log M_c+q^3)+C_{\rm cert},\nonumber\\
	&q=2K,\qquad M_r=rNT,
	\label{eq:complexity_supp}
\end{align}
with $M_c$ the zero-padded clock-FFT length. The first bracket is the matched
filter followed by the Gram update. That update is $O(q^2(N+T))$ rather than
$O(q^2M_r)$ because the Kronecker structure of \eqref{main-eq:Ak} makes each
Gram block the product of a cached, position-independent Maxwell--Jones
factor with one length-$N$ delay inner product and one length-$T$ training
inner product. The second term assembles the $N$ coefficients of
\eqref{main-eq:trig_short} from the $O(N^2)$ tone pairs, each weighted
through the $q\times q$ inverse Gram. The third covers the clock FFT and the
factorization of $\bH$. The certificate term is
$C_{\rm cert}=O\big(N_{\rm int}(N+\log N_{\rm int})\big)$, since each of the
$N_{\rm int}\le N_{\rm cap}$ visited intervals costs one $O(N)$ evaluation of
the polynomial and its derivative plus one heap operation. The profiled solve
therefore acts on $M_r$ compressed rows instead of the $M=I\,N\,T$ raw rows,
and the exact spherical panel projection, not the certified clock profile,
dominates the measured runtime (Section~\ref{main-subsec:res_cost}).

\section{Certificate and Failure Diagnostics in Detail}
\label{sec:cert_supp}

Over the $4\,320$ operating-region trials of the internal SNR suite, the
clock solver returned a valid certificate in $100\%$ of the trials and at
every one of the $35\,833$ clock-profile calls those trials made. The
objective-gap distribution, in units of the profiled score $Q$, has median
$2.9\times10^{-11}$, p95 $1.8\times10^{-10}$ and maximum
$8.5\times10^{-10}$, a certified inner optimality gap negligible against
the clock accuracy it supports ($1.10$~ps RMSE at $0$~dB). The prescribed
subdivision cap was never reached, the clock branch was never flagged as
ambiguous, and the derivative-free Powell safeguard was therefore never
triggered: all $4\,320$ estimates were returned by the L-BFGS-B candidate.
The outer search used a median of $8$ position evaluations (p95 $11$), each
reusing one factorization across a median of $2\,036$ clock grid points.
The dedicated C3 control instruments the full outer trajectory rather than
only the selected final point: the canonical proposed route makes $933$
clock-profile calls over its $100$ trials, all returning a certificate
within the prescribed tolerance, with a maximum ratio
$g_{\rm cert}/\epsilon_{\rm clk}$ of $0.9988$ over all visited positions
and a maximum objective gap of $9.3\times10^{-10}$. The same $100\%$
per-position certification holds in the mismatch, colored-noise,
position-grid and scaling suites, i.e.\ across $93$ further
configurations.

\section{The As-Published Baseline Tier}
\label{sec:aspub}

The method-consistent tier of the main paper follows each route by the
continuous read-out or refinement of its own formulation. This section reports the complementary setting, in
which the VBI/SBL route is stopped at the closed-form weighted
least-squares (WLS) fusion of its per-panel directions and delays, exactly where
\cite{li2024vbi} stops. This tier comparison is reported only for VBI/SBL.
The measurement uses the same paired protocol as the main comparison, on
the first $480$ trials of the $960$-trial external-benchmark stream, at four
SNR points; every method-consistent number quoted in this section is
therefore taken on that same paired subset.

\emph{The closed-form fusion does not localize in this scene.} Its position
error has a median of $0.81$, $0.81$, $0.62$ and $0.49$~m at $-15$, $-10$,
$0$ and $10$~dB, with a catastrophic rate of $99.4\%$, $97.9\%$, $87.1\%$
and $82.1\%$; the median improves by only a factor of $1.6$ over a $25$~dB
span, which is consistent with a persistent error floor rather than
noise-limited decay.

\emph{The diagnostics are consistent with an unresolved common-clock
floor.} The clock error of the same runs has a median of $3.21$, $3.40$,
$2.53$ and $2.31$~ns and is likewise almost flat in SNR, against a prior box
$\Delta t\in[0,10]$~ns; between $7\%$ and $21\%$ of trials return an estimate
at the box boundary and its clock-catastrophic rate is $67$--$88\%$. A
$2$--$3$-ns median absolute error inside a $10$-ns box is comparable to the
scale of an uninformative estimate, so the fusion extracts little usable
clock information here. The position error is strongly coupled to it: the
per-trial position error and the
range-equivalent clock error $c_0|\hat{\Delta t}-\Delta t^\star|$ have a
Pearson correlation of $0.88$--$0.92$ at $-10$~dB and above, and their
range-equivalent-clock/position median ratio is $1.18$--$1.40$. These
diagnostics indicate that common-clock error, propagated into range,
dominates the residual position error of this tier.

\emph{This is a property of the scene, not a deficiency of the reference
algorithm.} \cite{li2024vbi} recovers position in closed form from the
converged angle support together with a delay \emph{difference} taken
against the direct access-point (AP)-to-UE link, which removes the common
clock before the geometric inversion. The configuration studied here has no
direct AP-to-UE
path, so that difference is unavailable and the clock survives as an
explicit unknown that the per-panel angle and delay estimates cannot
themselves resolve. The as-published tier therefore measures what happens
when a construction is transplanted outside the geometry it was designed
for, and reporting it as a bare accuracy number against the other routes
would misattribute a missing observable to the variational core.

\emph{Consequence for the main comparison.} Under the method-consistent
tier the same route, on the same realizations, reaches a median error of
$0.58$, $0.33$ and $0.104$~mm at $-15$, $-10$ and $0$~dB and a clock median
of $3.9$, $2.2$ and $0.70$~ps. The improvement exceeds three orders of
magnitude in position and is approximately three orders of magnitude in
clock. It appears when joint exact-model refinement is added; fitting all
$K$ panels against the forward model restores clock observability. This is
the quantitative reason the method-consistent tier is used
throughout the main comparison: it is the setting in which the residual gap
between routes reflects the reliability of the acquisition that seeds the
final refinement, rather than whether a route carries a construction that this
geometry happens to disable.

\section{Anatomy of the ALS-CPD Read-Out Failure}
\label{sec:als}

The main paper reports the ALS-CPD adaptation at face value, with a
catastrophic rate of $44.1\%$ at $-10$~dB and $30.5$--$33.5\%$ from $0$ to
$30$~dB. The persistent high-SNR floor is consistent with a geometric
read-out branch downstream of the tensor factorization. This section gives
the evidence, all of it from the released per-trial records of the same
$960$-trial cells.

Four facts hold jointly on the failing trials. \emph{(i)~The failing branch
has a nearly fixed offset}: the median position error of the
failing subset is $0.1782$, $0.1784$ and $0.1786$~m at $0$, $10$ and
$30$~dB, constant to three significant figures over a $30$~dB span, and
already $0.1804$~m at $-10$~dB. This is behavior inconsistent with ordinary
noise-limited decay. \emph{(ii)~The tensor fit remains accurate}: the median
observation-domain NMSE of the failing trials is
indistinguishable from that of the succeeding trials of the \emph{same}
cell ($8.05\times10^{-4}$ against $8.01\times10^{-4}$ at $0$~dB,
$8.00\times10^{-7}$ against $8.00\times10^{-7}$ at $30$~dB), and it decays
with SNR exactly as the succeeding trials' does. A route whose factorization
had failed could not reconstruct the observation to the same accuracy as one
whose factorization succeeded. \emph{(iii)~The panel association is
complete and unique} in every failing trial at every SNR
($423/423$, $293/293$, $322/322$ and $317/317$ at $-10$, $0$, $10$ and
$30$~dB), so the failure is not a permutation error. \emph{(iv)~The
CP-factor refinement reports convergence} in every one of them.

These diagnostics localize the observed failure downstream of factor
fitting and association, in the map from converged CP factors to position.
They do not establish that the membership of the failing subset is selected
by the scene, nor do they exclude a discrete mapping or implementation
branch; that membership changes with the noise realization. We report the
route under the same paired protocol as the others because suppressing it
would hide a persistent behavior of the evaluated adaptation. The result
should not be read as a property of the reference algorithm
of~\cite{lin2021tensor}: it is specific to this adaptation and
implementation in the oriented, mixed near/far-field geometry studied here.

statement is unaffected; the empirical benefit is not established here, and
a larger paired suite would be needed to settle it.

\section{Position Generalization, Resource Scaling, and Cost}
\label{sec:scaling_supp}

The $5\times5\times2$ position grid contains $480$ trials at each of $50$
UE locations. The proposed route has median per-position catastrophic rate
$0.62\%$ (interquartile range $0.21$--$0.83\%$), versus $2.92\%$
($2.29$--$3.33\%$) for the range-scaled 4-D route. The two hard corners at
$(2.60,1.40,\cdot)$~m reach $31.25\%$ and $7.71\%$ for the proposed route,
but all $24\,000$ free-Jones EFIM evaluations remain full rank. The four
positions below $1.2$~ns delay separation are exactly the four largest
catastrophic-rate cells. This establishes a global acquisition boundary,
not a local-information deficiency.

\section*{Part II: The Threshold Region Below $-10$~dB}

\section{Threshold Region: Mechanism}
\label{sec:thresh}

The main paper reports the operating region ${\rm SNR}\in[-10,30]$~dB. The
remaining sections report the two points of the same campaign below
it. The single mechanism that organizes all of them is the collapse of the
Phase~I delay poles. As the SNR falls the compressed delay subspace stops
being separated from the noise subspace. The median compressed
subspace-leakage ratio $\sigma_{K+1}/\sigma_K$ rises from $0.353$ at
$-10$~dB to $0.569$ at $-15$~dB and $0.809$ at $-20$~dB, while the retained
energy fraction falls from $0.148$ to $0.091$ and $0.072$ against the
white-noise floor $r/I=1/16=0.0625$. The projection is by then retaining
little beyond what a fixed subspace retains of pure noise. The multi-start
common-geometry pool consequently stops bracketing the true basin, and a
certified local refinement cannot recover what the initializer did not
bracket. The catastrophic rate therefore grows while the conditional
accuracy of the surviving trials still tracks the bound, the signature
of an acquisition failure rather than an estimation failure.

\section{Internal Structure Validation Below the Region}

Fig.~\ref{fig:s_internal} reports the full internal SNR sweep behind the
main paper's C2/C3 attribution, including the two lower points. The proposed
route degrades from $0.83\%$ catastrophic at $-10$~dB to $1.46\%$
(CI $[0.59,2.98]$) at $-15$~dB and $5.00\%$ (CI $[3.23,7.35]$) at
$-20$~dB, with the Phase~I basin acquisition rate falling from $99.17\%$ to
$98.33\%$ and $94.79\%$. Panel assignment, correct in $100\%$ of trials at
and above $0$~dB and in $99.58\%$ at $-10$~dB, degrades to $98.75\%$ at
$-15$~dB and $95.00\%$ at $-20$~dB, and the p95 Phase~I delay error rises
from $0.11$~ns to $0.75$ and $27.1$~ns while its median rises only from
$0.024$ to $0.049$ and $0.125$~ns. Both diagnostics localize the breakdown
in the Phase~I delay/association stage rather than in the certified
profile, and both are visibly tail effects.

The internal ordering is preserved throughout and widens. At
$-20$~dB the catastrophic rate is $5.00\%$ for the proposed route and for
the same route without the anchor refresh, against $22.92\%$ for both R2
and R3. The components that differentiate the proposed route from R2, the
compressed delay estimator and the common-geometry pool, therefore still
remove about four fifths of the wrong-basin mass where they removed all of
it above $0$~dB. The certified profile still makes no difference to the
rate, R2 and R3 coinciding at every point as they must. The paired
comparison against R2 (Fig.~\ref{fig:s_paired}) strengthens accordingly:
the paired RMSE difference is $-0.240$~m ($[-0.334,-0.155]$) at $-15$~dB

against $3$ and $8$ introduced outliers ($p=1.4\times10^{-6}$ and
$9.4\times10^{-20}$, McNemar exact). The introduced-outlier counts (zero
at and above $0$~dB, at most three inside the operating region, eight at
$-20$~dB) are the visible signature of the initializer itself becoming
unreliable.

\section*{Part III: Complete Proofs}

\section{Local Identifiability with Unknown Jones Nuisance}
\label{sec:ident_proof_supp}

Let $(\bchi_\star,\bx_\star)$ be the true parameters, let
$\bD_\chi=[\partial(\bA\bx_\star)/\partial\chi_i]_{i=1}^4$, and define
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\bD}_\chi=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 \Real\{\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}\\
 \Imag\{\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}
 \end{bmatrix},\qquad
 \bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}=\bI-\bA_\star\bA_\star^\dagger.
 \label{eq:s_ident}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Local position--clock identifiability]
Suppose $\bA_\star$ has constant rank near the truth, the paths are
resolvable in the raw OFDM domain, the panel assignment is fixed, the truth
is interior, and $\rank_{\R}(\widetilde{\bD}_\chi)=4$. Then $\bchi_\star$
is first-order locally identifiable after eliminating the complex Jones
coefficients. Under white Gaussian noise this rank condition is equivalent
to positive definiteness of the free-Jones EFIM.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
A first-order perturbation is
$\delta\bmu=\bD_\chi\bq+\bA_\star\delta\bx$ with $\bq\in\R^4$. A nonzero
geometric direction can be canceled by a Jones perturbation exactly when
$\bD_\chi\bq\in\coll(\bA_\star)$, or equivalently
$\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\bq=\mathbf0$. Realifying this map gives
\eqref{eq:s_ident}, whose nullspace is trivial precisely at real rank four.
Its Gram matrix is
$\Real\{\bD_\chi^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi\}$, proportional
to the free-Jones EFIM, proving the equivalence.
\end{IEEEproof}

The statement is local and conditional. In particular, three panels are
not sufficient by equation counting alone: the RIS codes must preserve the
near-field spatial tangents, the curvature must be nondegenerate, and the
panel geometry must avoid collinearity and symmetry. Global permutations
and finite-bandwidth ambiguities are not excluded.

\section{Complete Proof of Theorem~\ref{main-thm:split}}
\label{sec:split_proof_supp}

\begin{lemma}[Kronecker projector splitting]
Let $\bc\ne\mathbf0$, and let $\bTheta$ have full column rank. With
$\bA=\bc\otimes\bTheta$,
\begin{equation}
 \bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}
 =\bP_{\bc}\otimes\bPi_{\bTheta}^{\perp}
 +\bPi_{\bc}^{\perp}\otimes\bI,
 \qquad \bP_{\bc}=\frac{\bc\bc^{\mathsf H}}{\|\bc\|^2}.
 \label{eq:s_kron_split}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Since $\bA^{\mathsf H}\bA=(\bc^{\mathsf H}\bc)
(\bTheta^{\mathsf H}\bTheta)$,
$\bP_{\bA}=\bA\bA^\dagger=\bP_{\bc}\otimes\bP_{\bTheta}$. Expanding
$\bI=(\bP_{\bc}+\bPi_{\bc}^{\perp})\otimes
(\bP_{\bTheta}+\bPi_{\bTheta}^{\perp})$ and subtracting
$\bP_{\bA}$ gives \eqref{eq:s_kron_split}. The projector identity remains
valid with pseudoinverses after a component mask makes the Jones block
column-rank deficient.
\end{IEEEproof}

For Theorem~\ref{main-thm:split}, take
$\bTheta=\bTheta_k^{(6)}$ and write
$\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\bTheta\bx$. A Jacobian column is
$\bD_i=\dot\bc_i\otimes\boldsymbol{\zeta}
+\bc\otimes\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_i$. Substitution into
\eqref{eq:s_kron_split} leaves the pure geometric term
$\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|^2\dot\bc_i^{\mathsf H}\bPi_{\bc}^{\perp}\dot\bc_j$
and the pure
curvature term
$\|\bc\|^2\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_i^{\mathsf H}
\bPi_{\bTheta}^{\perp}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_j$.
Both cross terms contain either
$\bPi_{\bc}^{\perp}\bc=\mathbf0$ or
$\bPi_{\bTheta}^{\perp}\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\mathbf0$ and vanish. This proves the split in
main-paper Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:split_short}. Calibrated infrastructure makes
$\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_i=\mathbf0$, leaving the one-term factorization.
A receiver mask
replaces $\bTheta$ by $\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta$ without changing $\bc$, so the ratio to
the full-mode EFIM is exactly
$\alpha_{\bS_{\rm mode}}=\|\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta\bx\|^2/\|\bTheta\bx\|^2$ for one panel. With
several resolved panels these factors depend on $k$, and the weighted sum
is generally not a global rescaling.

For panel separation, the competing block is
$\bc_{k'}(\bchi')\otimes\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_{k'}^{(6)}$. Under
$\bc_{k'}(\bchi')\parallel\bc_k(\bchi)$, applying
\eqref{eq:s_kron_split} to the true vector
$\bc_k\otimes\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_k^{(6)}\bx_k$ annihilates the geometric-complement term
and gives main-paper Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:panelres_short}. If the mask retains
$d\le2$ components and
$\bS_{\rm mode}\bTheta_{k'}^{(6)}\in\C^{d\times2}$ has row rank
$d$, its range is $\C^d$, hence its orthogonal-complement projector is zero.
For full EVS, let $\bU$ and $\bV$ be orthonormal bases of the two Maxwell
subspaces. Since $\sigma_{\max}(\bU^{\mathsf H}\bV)=
\cos\theta_{\min}$, every nonzero $\bz\in\coll(\bTheta_k^{(6)})$ satisfies
$\|\bPi_{\bTheta_{k'}^{(6)}}\bz\|\le\cos\theta_{\min}\|\bz\|$ and therefore
$\|\bPi_{\bTheta_{k'}^{(6)}}^\perp\bz\|\ge
\sin\theta_{\min}\|\bz\|$. This completes all parts.

For nested component sets, the free-Jones information order also follows
without assuming an invertible Jones block from the variational identity
\begin{equation}
 \bu^{\mathsf T}\bJ_\chi^{\rm free}\bu
 =\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\min_{\delta\bx}
 \|\bD_\chi\bu+\bA\delta\bx\|^2.
\end{equation}
Adding observed components appends nonnegative residual terms, so it cannot
decrease the minimized quadratic form.

\section{Complete Proof of Theorem~\ref{main-thm:compress}}
\label{sec:compress_proof_supp}

Let $\bU_S$ be the semi-unitary embedding used in the main paper and let
$\bU_S^\perp$ complete it to a unitary matrix. The transformed data obey
\begin{align}
 \bU_S^{\mathsf H}\by &\sim
 \mathcal{CN}(\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\bA(\bchi)\bx,\sigma^2\bI),\\
 (\bU_S^\perp)^{\mathsf H}\by &\sim
 \mathcal{CN}((\bU_S^\perp)^{\mathsf H}\bA(\bchi)\bx,\sigma^2\bI).
\end{align}
The matched-subspace assumption makes the second mean zero, and the two
blocks are independent. For known $\sigma^2$, the second density is
independent of $(\bchi,\bx)$, so $\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\by$ is sufficient for
those parameters. For unknown $\sigma^2$, the discarded block still carries
noise-power information, but its isotropic Gaussian density depends on that
block only through
$c_\perp=\|(\bI-\bU_S\bU_S^{\mathsf H})\by\|^2$. The factorization theorem
therefore makes $(\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\by,c_\perp)$ sufficient for
$(\bchi,\bx,\sigma^2)$.

Write $\bA=\bU_S\bA_c$ and $\bD_\chi=\bU_S\bD_{\chi,c}$. Semi-unitarity
gives
\begin{equation}
 \bPi_{\bA}^{\perp}\bD_\chi
 =\bU_S\bPi_{\bA_c}^{\perp}\bD_{\chi,c},
\end{equation}
including with pseudoinverses. Taking the Gram of both sides proves that
the raw and compressed matrices entering the free-Jones EFIM are equal.
This also proves preservation under any semi-unitary map whose range
contains both $\coll(\bA)$ and $\coll(\bD_\chi)$.

For the isometry, decompose $\by$ into its orthogonal components in and out
of $\coll(\bU_S)$. Since $\bA=\bU_S\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\bA$, the cross term
vanishes and
\begin{equation}
 \|\by-\bA\bx\|^2=
 \|\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\by-\bU_S^{\mathsf H}\bA\bx\|^2+
 \|(\bI-\bU_S\bU_S^{\mathsf H})\by\|^2.
\end{equation}
The last term is independent of $(\bchi,\bx)$, proving main-paper
Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:isometry_short} and the claims for profiled coefficients,
derivatives, and the EFIM; when $\sigma^2$ is also unknown, its value is
retained as $c_\perp$.

Finally, the projection acts on the EVS mode only and commutes with both
Vandermonde frequency factors. It therefore leaves the noiseless pole set
unchanged. Standard shift-invariance identifiability is retained when the
compressed non-frequency snapshot matrix has column rank $K$. Each of its
$K\times K$ minors is real analytic in continuously distributed training
phases, Jones states, and nonzero gains. If one feasible design makes a
minor nonzero, that minor is not identically zero and its zero set has
measure zero. This proves the stated generic rank claim while excluding the
declared structural degeneracies: $rT<K$, an annihilated path, zero gain,
or systematically dependent signatures.

\section{Coupled-Recovery Lift and Exact Scale Elimination}
\label{sec:technical_proofs_supp}

\begin{lemma}[Equivariant coupled-recovery lift]
Let $\mathcal F$ map a Hankel tensor to its recovered EVS and RIS factors
through main-paper Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:coupledls_short} and a leading rank-one
split. Then
\begin{equation}
 \bQ_S\mathcal F_r(\mathcal Z\times_1\bQ_S^{\mathsf H})
 =\mathcal F(\mathcal Z\times_1\bQ_S\bQ_S^{\mathsf H}),
 \label{eq:s_lift}
\end{equation}
up to the singular-vector phase convention.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
The coupled least-squares map acts on the EVS mode by left multiplication,
which commutes with $\bQ_S^{\mathsf H}$. In addition,
$(\bQ_S\bM)^{\mathsf H}(\bQ_S\bM)=\bM^{\mathsf H}\bM$, so $\bQ_S\bM$
and $\bM$ share singular values and right singular vectors, with left
singular vectors related by multiplication by $\bQ_S$.
\end{IEEEproof}

Thus compressed recovery is the raw recovery on a projected observation,
not an identical estimator on the original noisy data. It removes the
$I-r$ signal-free EVS directions and can improve variance; that empirical
effect is kept distinct from the exact Phase~II isometry.

\begin{proposition}[Exact complex-scale elimination]
For every $\bp$ with $\bh_k(\bp)\ne\mathbf0$,
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\alpha_k\in\C}\|\tilde\bt_k-\alpha_k\bh_k(\bp)\|^2
 =\|\bPi_{\bh_k(\bp)}^\perp\tilde\bt_k\|^2,
 \quad
 \hat\alpha_k=\frac{\bh_k^{\mathsf H}\tilde\bt_k}{\|\bh_k\|^2}.
 \label{eq:s_scale}
\end{equation}
The profiled term is invariant to every nonzero complex rescaling of
$\bh_k$ and every phase rotation of $\tilde\bt_k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
For fixed $\bp$, the objective is a strictly convex quadratic in the real
and imaginary parts of $\alpha_k$. Setting the Wirtinger derivative with
respect to $\alpha_k^\ast$ to zero yields \eqref{eq:s_scale}; substitution
gives the orthogonal residual. An orthogonal projector depends only on the
one-dimensional span of $\bh_k$, and its output norm is unchanged by a
phase rotation of the input.
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{Complete Proof of Theorem~\ref{main-thm:clock}}
\label{sec:clock_proof_supp}

Unitarity in main-paper Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:orbit_short} gives the
clock-independent Gram $\bG_0$. Expanding the penalized objective for fixed
$(\bp,\Delta t)$ yields
\begin{equation}
 J_{\rm J}=\|\by\|^2-2\Real\{\bb^{\mathsf H}\bx\}
 +\bx^{\mathsf H}\bH\bx.
\end{equation}
When $\bH\succ\mathbf0$, this quadratic has the unique minimizer
$\hat\bx=\bH^{-1}\bb$ and minimum
$\|\by\|^2-\bb^{\mathsf H}\bH^{-1}\bb$. Minimizing in any order proves
the exact nested profile. An affine reparameterization of the clock changes
the coordinate but not the set of attained score values, proving unit
invariance of the profiled outer objective.

From main-paper Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:trig_short}, termwise differentiation gives
\begin{align}
 |Q'|&\le(2\pi\Delta f)\sum_\ell|\ell||c_\ell|,\nonumber\\
 |Q''|&\le(2\pi\Delta f)^2\sum_\ell\ell^2|c_\ell|=L_2.
\end{align}
Taylor's theorem on an interval centered at $t_c$ gives main-paper
Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:bound_short}. The solver maintains a finite cover of
$\mathcal T$, an upper bound for each surviving interval, and an incumbent
from FFT screening and Newton refinement. An interval below the incumbent
cannot contain a maximizer. Hence the largest surviving bound $U$ always
satisfies $Q(\Delta t^\star)\le U$, which proves
Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:cert_short} under any stopping rule. If termination is by the
tolerance, the returned gap is at most $\epsilon_{\rm clk}$; if it is by
the cap, the attained gap remains valid. This proves both parts without a
claim about the outer position optimizer.

\section{Directional Regularization: Conditional Compatibility}
\label{sec:regularization_proof_supp}

Write $\bvt=(\bchi,\bx)$ for the full parameter vector, so that
$\hat\bvt_{\rm free}$ and $\hat\bvt_{\rm reg}$ denote the unpenalized and
directionally penalized estimates of Phase~II.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional asymptotic compatibility]
\label{prop:compat}
For a fixed design with $\sigma\to0$, suppose the truth $\bvt_\star$ is
interior, the free-Jones Fisher information is nonsingular, and the free
estimator reaches the correct local basin with probability tending to one.
If the penalty
$\mathcal R_\sigma$ satisfies, for every fixed $C<\infty$,
\begin{equation}
 \|\nabla\mathcal R_\sigma(\bvt_\star)\|=o_{\mathsf P}(\sigma),\qquad
 \sup_{\|\bvt-\bvt_\star\|\le C\sigma}
 \|\nabla^2\mathcal R_\sigma(\bvt)\|=o_{\mathsf P}(1),
 \label{eq:s_taper}
\end{equation}
then the regularized and free estimates in that basin differ by
$o_{\mathsf P}(\sigma)$ and have the same first-order limit distribution.
For the Gram-scaled directional penalty, anchor consistency
$O_{\mathsf P}(\sigma)$ together with
$\max_k\lambda_{k,\sigma}=o_{\mathsf P}(1)$ is sufficient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Write the penalized objective as
$\mathcal L_\sigma=\mathcal L+\mathcal R_\sigma$. The free score at the
truth is $O_{\mathsf P}(\sigma)$ and the free Hessian converges uniformly on
an $O(\sigma)$ ball to a nonsingular matrix $\bJ_0$. Expanding the two
stationarity conditions about the truth and subtracting gives
\begin{equation}
 [\bJ_0+o_{\mathsf P}(1)](\hat\bvt_{\rm reg}-\hat\bvt_{\rm free})
 =-\nabla\mathcal R_\sigma(\bvt_\star)
 -\bE_\sigma(\hat\bvt_{\rm reg}-\bvt_\star),
\end{equation}
where $\|\bE_\sigma\|=o_{\mathsf P}(1)$ by \eqref{eq:s_taper}. Both
right-hand terms are $o_{\mathsf P}(\sigma)$, and inversion proves the
claim. For the directional penalty, its gradient at the truth is
proportional to the product of the weight and anchor-direction error, while
its Hessian is of the order of the weight relative to the data Gram, giving
the stated sufficient conditions.
\end{IEEEproof}

In every reported experiment the directional weight is fixed at
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_k=1,\qquad k=1,\ldots,K,
 \label{eq:s_lambda_fixed}
\end{equation}
so that main-paper Eq.~\eqref{main-eq:Rdir_short} reduces to
\begin{equation}
 \bR_{\rm dir}(\bp)=\blkdiag_{k=1}^{K}
 \left[\tfrac12\tr\bG_{0,kk}(\bp)\,\bPi_{\hat\be_k}^{\perp}\right].
 \label{eq:s_rdir_fixed}
\end{equation}
The penalty is scaled by the Jones data Gram but is neither path adaptive
nor annealed with SNR. Its Jones-direction curvature therefore remains of
the same order as that of the corresponding data term at every noise level.
In particular $\max_k\lambda_{k,\sigma}=o_{\mathsf P}(1)$ does not hold for
the reported estimator. Proposition~\ref{prop:compat} thus characterizes
the tapering condition required for asymptotic compatibility with the
free-Jones model, rather than an asymptotic property of the fixed-weight
estimator evaluated in this paper. Consequently nothing in that proposition
establishes that the reported estimator attains the free-Jones bound. The
bound is used only as a reference for the unpenalized data model, and the
observed RMSE/PEB proximity is reported as finite-SNR empirical behavior.
